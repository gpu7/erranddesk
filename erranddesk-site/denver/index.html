Denver document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Denver, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide, national or metro-wide rather than Denver-only. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City and County of Denver, Denver Public Library, USPS, the U.S. Department of State or any business named here. This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
Short version: Denver Public Library offers free printing and copying (up to 100 pages) and free send-only faxing [1]. Colorado caps notary fees at $15 per document in person [7]. The State Department's locator lists two post offices with Denver addresses for passport applications, both by appointment [5]. Denver County Court no longer offers passport services [4]. Birth certificates cost $25 for the first copy at Denver's vital records office [2].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax: Denver Public Library

ServiceWhat Denver Public Library says
PrintingFree "printing from library computers or mobile print from your own device." Letter (8.5x11) or legal (8.5x14) size. Only library-supplied paper can be used [1].
Copying"Printing and copying is limited to 100 pages" [1].
Scanning"You may also scan documents and send them to your email" [1].

The library says these services are available at all open locations. Questions: 720-865-1111 [1]. For prices at The UPS Store, FedEx Office and others, see printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Denver

  • Colorado fee limit (statewide): "the maximum fee allowed for a notarization is $15 per document." The maximum for an electronic or remote notarization is $25. Notaries must tell you all charges before the notarial act [7].
  • Banks (national policy): Bank of America says notary service is available "at no cost to our customers" and "in many of our financial centers." Schedule an appointment. All signers must be present [8]. For other banks, see free notary options.
  • The UPS Store (example store): the store at 700 N Colorado Blvd, Denver, CO 80206 says it has a commissioned notary public on staff and lists notary hours as "Available 7 Days." Phone (303) 320-4757. Its page doesn't post a price [9]. Under state law the fee can't exceed $15 per document in person [7].
  • Denver Public Library: we didn't find a notary service on the library's pages as of Oct 28, 2026. That doesn't mean there isn't one. Call 720-865-1111 to ask.
  • Denver Clerk and Recorder: the recording page doesn't list notary service [3].

Birth, death and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Denver vital records

  • Order a State of Colorado birth certificate or a Denver County death certificate online, by mail or in person. Walk-ins are welcome [2].
  • $25 for the first copy, $20 for each additional copy, and $25 for FedEx expedited shipping [2].
  • Since May 1, 2025, credit and debit card payments carry a 2.5% non-refundable service fee. Cash, business check and money order have no fee [2].
  • Office: 120 W. 5th Ave., Denver, CO 80204. Walk-in hours Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–3 p.m., closed city holidays. "Official copies can be obtained on site, the same day, if regulated requirements are met." Phone 720-295-7964 [2].
  • Online orders go through VitalChek. Mail orders: check or money order, don't send cash. Bring a valid, unexpired driver license, state ID or passport [2].
  • Colorado birth certificates are available at any Colorado county office. If you weren't born in Colorado, contact your birth state [2].
  • State office (statewide): the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment also takes online orders. It says normal processing for online orders is thirty business days, and a surcharge applies to card payments [11].

Recorded documents and marriage licenses: Denver Clerk and Recorder

  • Certified copy of a recorded document: "$1 for certification, per document, $0.25 per page." Recording costs $43 per document [3].
  • Marriage license $30. Committed partnership license $25. A copy of a marriage license or application is $0.25, or $1.25 certified. Recording a DD-214 is free [3].
  • 200 W. 14th Ave., Denver, CO 80204, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Mon–Fri. In-person service requires an appointment. Make checks payable to "Manager of Finance." The office says it cannot provide legal advice [3].

Money orders (national USPS fees)

Post offices, including those in Denver, sell USPS money orders. Fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [10]. Denver's vital records office takes money orders with no card fee [2]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Can I fax for free in Denver?

Yes, you can send a fax for free from Denver Public Library copiers, but you can't receive one. You get no confirmation receipt, so check with the recipient [1].

How much can a notary charge in Colorado?

Up to $15 per document for an in-person notarization, and up to $25 for an electronic or remote notarization, per the Colorado Secretary of State [7].

Where can I apply for a passport in Denver?

The State Department's locator lists Denver Downtown Station (951 20th Street) and University Park Post Office (3800 Buchtel Blvd.), both by appointment through usps.com/scheduler [5]. Denver County Court no longer offers passport services [4].

How much is a birth certificate in Denver?

$25 for the first copy and $20 for each additional copy at Denver's vital records office, 120 W. 5th Ave. Card payments add a 2.5% fee [2].
