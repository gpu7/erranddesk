New York City, New York document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in New York City, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City of New York, its five county clerks, the New York Public Library, Brooklyn Public Library, Queens Public Library, New York State, USPS, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. Fees change; confirm with the office before you go.
Short version: 98 passport acceptance facilities are inside the city, including 3 borough County Clerks [1][2]. NYC birth certificates are $15 each plus a processing fee [3]. A NYC marriage license is $35, by appointment through Project Cupid [4]. New York notaries may charge $2.00 per act [5]. Library printouts are 20¢ a page at NYPL and 10¢ at Brooklyn Public Library [6][7].
One city, five boroughs, five counties: New York City covers Manhattan (New York County), the Bronx (Bronx County), Brooklyn (Kings County), Queens (Queens County) and Staten Island (Richmond County) [8][9]. Some services are citywide (the NYC Health Department for birth certificates and the City Clerk for marriage licenses) [10][4]. Others split by borough: there are three separate library systems, and Staten Island deeds are with the Richmond County Clerk instead of the City Register [11].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

  • New York Public Library (Manhattan, the Bronx and Staten Island): computer printing is 20¢ a page. Children and teens can print 10 pages free per day, and you need a library card to use a computer [6]. Self-service copies and printouts are 20¢ for black-and-white letter size and $1.25 in color, and color isn't available at every location [12].
  • Brooklyn Public Library: printing is 10¢ per black-and-white page and 50¢ per color page. You need a library card or a $1 Guest Pass from a branch kiosk [7].
  • Queens Public Library: its remote-printing page refused our request, so we didn't list its prices [13].
  • Library fax: we didn't find a fax service on the NYPL or Brooklyn pages we read (last checked Nov 4, 2026).
  • The UPS Store: its New York, NY page lists 48 locations offering printing, shredding, notarizing and faxing [14]. That page covers New York, NY addresses, not every borough. Prices aren't listed online.
  • FedEx Office: FedEx's local pages list 51 FedEx Office Print & Ship Centers in the city: 42 in Manhattan, 6 in Brooklyn, 1 in the Bronx and 2 on Staten Island. Its Queens page listed none; we didn't check its Queens neighborhood pages (last checked Nov 4, 2026) [15][16][17][18][19].

Notary in New York City

  • Brooklyn Public Library: free notary service at select locations, by online reservation. It's for patrons 18 and older, up to 3 documents a day, and the signer must be present with valid ID [20].
  • NYPL and Queens Public Library: we didn't find a notary service on the pages we read (last checked Nov 4, 2026).
  • The UPS Store: its New York, NY page lists notarizing [14]. Notary rules and fees vary by state [21].
  • Notary fees (statewide): a New York notary may charge $2.00 for an oath or affirmation, or for taking an acknowledgment or proof of execution [22]. Executive Law §136 sets $2 for an acknowledgment by one person and $2 for each additional person, plus $2 for each witness sworn [23]. An electronic notary may charge up to $25.00 per electronic notarial act [22]. The Westchester County Clerk's notary FAQ gives the same $2.00 fee [5].

Birth, death, marriage and land record copies

Birth and death certificates (NYC Health Department, citywide)

  • Who: the NYC Health Department issues birth certificates for people born in all five boroughs. It doesn't issue them for Nassau, Suffolk or other New York counties [10]. The state Department of Health doesn't hold NYC birth records [26].
  • Fee: $15 per certificate, plus a processing fee: $9.30 online through VitalChek, $7.50 by mail or $2.75 in person [3].
  • In person: by appointment only; in some cases you can get the record the same day. This covers births in or after 1910 [27].

Marriage licenses and records (NYC City Clerk, citywide)

  • How: book an in-person appointment at any borough office, or a virtual one, through Project Cupid. There are no walk-ins [4]. Marriage certificates also come from the City Clerk, not the Health Department [10].
  • Fee and wait: $35. The ceremony must be at least 24 hours after you get the license, and the license is usually valid for 60 days. The County Clerk in the borough where you got the license can issue a judicial waiver of the wait at no fee [4].
  • Compared with the rest of the state: outside New York City a town or city clerk issues the license for $40, with the same 24-hour wait [28].

Deeds and land records

  • Manhattan, the Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens (City Register, ACRIS): certified copies are $4 per page. Uncertified copies printed at a City Register office are $1 per page, and ACRIS copies you print from your own computer are free. You can go to any borough City Register office [11].
  • Staten Island: use the Richmond County Clerk instead [11]. We didn't check its copy fees (last checked Nov 4, 2026).

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the New York State Department of State, for $10 per document. You can apply by mail or walk in at its offices in New York City, Albany, Binghamton, Buffalo and Utica. Drop-off is offered only in Albany and New York City, and same-day walk-in service is limited to 10 documents [29].
  • By mail (statewide): NY Department of State, Division of Licensing Services, Apostille and Authentication Unit, PO Box 22001, Albany, NY 12201-2001. Express carriers use the physical address instead. Phone 518-474-4429 [29].
  • State vital records (statewide): the state Department of Health can forward a state-issued certificate to the Department of State if you order by mail with expedited processing ($45.00 per copy) and enclose a separate $10.00 check or money order per certificate, payable to the Department of State [30].
  • New York City walk-in: same-day service 9:00 am to 4:00 pm at 123 William St., 2nd Floor, New York, NY 10038-3804 [29].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [31]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Where can I apply for a passport in New York City?

At 98 facilities in the locator data, mostly post offices, plus the Bronx, Kings and Queens County Clerks [1][2].

How much is a NYC birth certificate?

$15 per certificate plus a processing fee of $2.75 to $9.30, depending on how you order [3].

How much is a NYC marriage license?

$35 from the City Clerk, with a 24-hour wait before the ceremony [4].

How much does a New York notary charge?

$2.00 per oath, affirmation or acknowledgment [22][23]. An electronic notary may charge up to $25.00 per electronic notarial act [22].

Can I get something notarized at a New York City library?

Brooklyn Public Library offers free notary service at select locations, by online reservation, for patrons 18 and older, up to 3 documents a day. The signer must be present with valid ID. We didn't find a notary service on the NYPL or Queens Public Library pages we read [20].

How much does library printing cost in New York City?

New York Public Library computer printing is 20¢ a page (children and teens get 10 free pages a day), and self-service color copies are $1.25 and aren't at every location. Brooklyn Public Library printing is 10¢ per black-and-white page and 50¢ per color page. We didn't list Queens Public Library prices because its remote-printing page refused our request [6][12][7][13].
