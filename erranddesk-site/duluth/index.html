Duluth, Minnesota document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Duluth, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City of Duluth, Duluth Public Library, St. Louis County, the University of Minnesota Duluth, Resource Renew (WLSSD), the Minnesota Department of Health, the Minnesota Secretary of State, the Minnesota Judicial Branch, the State of Minnesota, USPS, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. This is general information, not legal advice. Prices and hours change; check the linked source before you go.
Which offices serve Duluth: the Census Bureau's 2020 place-by-county file lists Duluth only in St. Louis County [1]. The county's Recorder and Registrar of Titles is at the Duluth Courthouse, 100 N. 5th Ave. W., #101, open 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Monday–Friday; it also has vital records offices in Hibbing, Virginia, Ely and Cook [2]. Duluth Public Library is a service of the City of Duluth, with the Main Library (520 W. Superior St.), Mount Royal and West Duluth branches [3].
Short version: Duluth Public Library prints are 15¢ a page in black and white and 40¢ in color [4], and the Main Library offers notary service when staff are available [3]. St. Louis County takes passport applications at its Miller Hill Mall service center, including Saturday mornings, with $15 photos [5]. A certified birth certificate is $26 [6] and a marriage license $125, or $50 with premarital education [7].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Duluth Public Library

  • Computers: free public computer access at all locations: Main Library, West Duluth and Mount Royal [4].
  • Prints: 15¢ per black-and-white letter page and 30¢ for legal and ledger; 40¢ per color letter page and 60¢ for legal and ledger. Color printing is available at all locations. You can also send a print job from your own device; wireless jobs are held for 72 hours and are letter size only [4].
  • Fax: the Main Library, Mount Royal and West Duluth each list the "ability to fax anywhere in the US." We didn't find a fax price, or whether you can receive faxes [4].
  • Copies and scanning: we didn't find copier or scanner service or prices on the library's computers and printing page [4].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 2 locations in Duluth: 23 W Central Entrance, (218) 727-5550; and 1346 Arrowhead Rd, (218) 724-8595. Its city page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [8]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [9].

Notary in Duluth

  • Duluth Public Library (Main Library): notary service at 520 W. Superior St., Monday–Friday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., depending on staff availability. Walk-ins may be taken, but call 218-730-4221 first to confirm a notary will be there; the library says "Notary service and availability is not guaranteed." Bring the unsigned document and a government-issued ID (tribal IDs are valid) or a witness who knows you and has ID. Staff won't notarize wills, birth, death or marriage certificates, trusts, gun sales, deeds, titles, I-9 forms, depositions, real estate transactions or documents not in English [3]. We didn't find a fee on the page.
  • St. Louis County: the county Recorder files notary commissions [10]; we didn't find public notary service on the county pages we checked.
  • Fee limits (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 357.17 sets "the maximum fees to be charged and collected by a notary public": $5 for each protest and copy, $5 for making and serving each notice of nonpayment or nonacceptance, $5 per folio for "any affidavit or paper for which provision is not made herein" and $1 per folio for copies, $5 for each oath administered, $5 per folio for recording each instrument the notary is required by law to record, and for acknowledgments of deeds and other services, "the legal fees allowed other officers for like services" [11]. The Secretary of State's Notary FAQ quotes the same maximums [12]. Offices often post a flat price per signature or stamp; the local prices above are what each office lists.
  • Remote online notarization (statewide): the Secretary of State says a remote online notary public "must be physically located in the state of Minnesota to perform a remote online notarial act" and must register with its office under Minn. Stat. § 358.645 [13]. We couldn't confirm a current fee limit for remote online notarization on Oct 30, 2026, so ask the notary what it charges.

A $35 acceptance fee is paid separately to the acceptance facility, and expedited service is $60 more [15].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Minnesota Department of Health (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): the Minnesota Department of Health fee table lists a certified birth certificate at $26, with additional copies of the same certificate ordered at the same time $19 each; a noncertified (informational) birth record at $13, plus $6 for each additional copy; a certified death certificate at $13, plus $6 for each additional copy; a noncertified death record at $13, plus $6; and a verification of birth at $9. A birth certificate for a homeless youth or for the Veterans Administration, and a death certificate for the VA, are $0. Optional rush processing is $20 and rush shipping $21. The table marks these as available at "All vital records offices" [19].
  • In state law (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 144.226 sets the vital record fees. It waives the certified birth record fee for a homeless youth (subd. 8) and lets the state registrar and its agents charge a convenience fee of up to three percent for electronic, telephone or internet transactions (subd. 7) [20].

Birth and death certificates: St. Louis County

  • Birth fees: a certified copy is $26 (each extra copy of the same record bought at the same time $19) and a non-certified copy $13 (each extra $6) [6].
  • Death fees: a certified or non-certified copy is $13, and each extra copy of the same record bought at the same time $6 [21]. Fees are payable to the St. Louis County Recorder, in United States currency only [6][21].
  • How: in person at the Duluth Courthouse or the Virginia, Hibbing, Ely or Cook offices (check, cash or money order); by mail with a notarized application for a certified copy (check or money order to the St. Louis County Recorder, P.O. Box 157, Duluth, MN 55801-0157); or by email with a notarized application and an online card payment, which carries a service fee. Allow 1 to 2 weeks by mail. Phone 218-726-2559 [6][21].

Marriage licenses: St. Louis County

  • Fee: $125, or $50 with a premarital education statement that meets state requirements, paid by cash or check to the St. Louis County Recorder [7].
  • How: start with the county's online application. Both applicants must appear together in person with photo ID and be at least 18. The license is valid for six months, and the ceremony must take place in Minnesota. Phone 218-726-2559 [7].
  • Fee and premarital education discount (sources differ): Minn. Stat. § 517.08, subd. 1b, says the local registrar collects $125 for a civil marriage license, and that the fee "for parties who have completed at least 12 hours of premarital education is $50." To get the reduced fee, you bring a statement from the educator, on letterhead, "signed, dated, and notarized or marked with a church seal." The educator must be a licensed or ordained minister or the minister's designee, a person authorized to solemnize marriages, or a licensed marriage and family therapist. Subdivision 1c(b) of the same section refers to "the $40 fee under subdivision 1b, paragraph (b)" [22]. We don't say which number governs; the county notes above give the county's own figures.
  • Term and oath (statewide): the license is valid for six months. If you can't use it within that time "due to illness or other extenuating circumstances," you can surrender it and get a new one "without fee." The registrar may take your oath in person, by telephone, by web conference or by a verified statement signed by both of you, and may accept applications by mail, fax or electronic filing [22]. Each county decides which of these it offers; see the county notes above. We didn't find a waiting period in § 517.08.

Recorded documents: St. Louis County Recorder

  • Copies: a certified copy of any document is $10; a plain copy is $5 minimum plus $1 a page after 3 pages; a plain certificate of title $5 and a certified one $10 [23][24]. You can search on the public terminals at the Recorder's office, or order by email, fax or mail with payment [23].
  • Recording: the county says abstract and Torrens filing fees are set by state law (Minn. Stat. §§357.18 and 508.82) and must be paid at filing [23]. Its Abstract Department fee sheet lists recording a document at $46.00, a plat at $56.00 and a well disclosure statement at $54.00 [24]. Military discharge papers (DD214) recorded in the county are copied free for the veteran with photo ID [10].
  • Plat copies (sources differ): the county's real estate recording page lists a certified copy of an official plat at $15.00 and a plain copy at $5.00 [23]. Its Abstract Department fee sheet lists "Copy of an Official Plat $10.00," plus $5.00 to add certification for a large plat [24]. Confirm with the Recorder's office before you order.
  • Recording fees (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 357.18, subd. 1, sets county recorder fees: $46 to index and record a deed or other document, $56 to record a plat, $10 for a certified copy of any record, and $2 for a noncertified copy of a document submitted for recording when you bring a copy or duplicate original with it. The county board sets the price of other noncertified copies [25].

Court copy fees (statewide)

  • District court copies (statewide): the Minnesota Judicial Branch's district court fee chart lists a certified copy of any instrument from a civil or criminal proceeding at $14.00, an exemplified and certified copy at $28.00, and an uncertified copy at $0.00. A scanning fee of $25 applies "for each 50 pages, or part thereof of the filing," and faxes carry a "$25 transmission fee for each 50 pages, or part thereof." These are Minnesota base fees; they "do not include county law library fees." Checks are payable to District Court Administrator [26]. The chart doesn't list a per-page copy fee, and we didn't find a general records search fee on it (it does list a $5 judgment search fee for each name certified) [26].
  • In state law (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 357.021, subd. 2, sets the $14 certified copy fee and says "No fee may be charged to view or download a publicly available instrument from a civil or criminal proceeding or for an uncertified copy of that instrument" [27]. Court users who can't afford a fee can ask to have it reduced or waived [26].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: the Minnesota Secretary of State authenticates "the signature of a state public official or Minnesota Notary Public on documents to be used outside the United States of America" [28]. Its office doesn't notarize documents [29].
  • Fee: "$5.00 fee for each apostille certificate," in person or by mail [29].
  • In person, by appointment only: at the Secretary of State, First National Bank Building, 332 Minnesota Street, Suite N201, Saint Paul, MN 55101. Walk-ins aren't accepted. Appointments open 14 days in advance and cover up to 10 documents; book online or call 651-296-2803, option 2. In person you can pay by credit card, debit card, cash, check or money order [29].
  • By mail: send the certified or notarized documents, the fee and the completed Authentication Request Form to the same Suite N201 address, with a check or money order made out to MN Secretary of State. Documents come back by regular USPS mail at no charge unless you include a prepaid label [29][28]. The form lists common reasons for rejection: improper notarization, a private document that isn't notarized, a missing or short fee, a document that originated outside Minnesota, and no original signature [28].
  • Processing time: not posted. The office says documents "are processed in the order that they are received"; if appointments are booked and your need is urgent, email apostille.oss@state.mn.us [29].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [30]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • Free events: we didn't find an upcoming free document shred event on the official pages we checked.
  • Recycling shredded paper (ongoing, not an event): WLSSD's Materials Recovery Center accepts shredded paper for paper recycling; bring it "in a paper bag, labeled and stapled shut" [31]. That's recycling, not a shredding service.
  • The UPS Store: it lists shredding on its Duluth page [8]. We didn't find prices.

Questions

How much does printing cost at the Duluth library?

15¢ per black-and-white letter page and 40¢ per color letter page; legal and ledger cost more [4].

Can I get something notarized at the Duluth library?

Yes, at the Main Library, Monday–Friday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. when a notary is available; call 218-730-4221 first [3].

Where can I apply for a passport in Duluth?

At the St. Louis County Auditor's Service Center in Miller Hill Mall, the University of Minnesota Duluth or the Duluth Post Office [14].

How much is a marriage license in St. Louis County?

$125, or $50 with premarital education; both applicants must appear in person [7].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Minnesota Secretary of State in Saint Paul: $5.00 per apostille certificate (statewide), in person by appointment only (no walk-ins) or by mail with the Authentication Request Form [29].
