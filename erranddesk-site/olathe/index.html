Olathe, Kansas document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Olathe, Kansas, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those. Olathe is the Johnson County seat, so the county district court and Register of Deeds are both in the city.

Which offices serve Olathe: the Johnson County Register of Deeds is in the County Administration Building, 111 S. Cherry St., Olathe [12]. Marriage licenses come from the Johnson County District Court, 150 W. Santa Fe St., Olathe, not a county clerk [6]. Birth, death and marriage certificates come from the state KDHE Office of Vital Statistics in Topeka [7][10]. Olathe has its own library system, the Olathe Public Library, separate from Johnson County Library [1].
Short version: the State Department's locator lists two Olathe post offices for passports [4]. A marriage license is $85.50 and must be applied for online [6]. Register of Deeds copies are $0.25 a page and $13.00 to certify [13]. Kansas certified birth, death and marriage copies are $20 each (statewide) [9].

Notary in Olathe

  • Library: Johnson County Library, which doesn't notarize, says "Limited notary service may be available at Olathe Libraries" [1]. We couldn't open the Olathe Public Library's own pages to confirm details or cost.
  • The UPS Store: the 119 N Parker St store lists notary service [2]. We didn't find its notary hours or price.
  • Fees (statewide): the Kansas Secretary of State says "State law does not prohibit a notary from charging a fee for a notarial act." We didn't find a dollar limit in its notary page or handbook. Under K.A.R. 7-43-16 a fee must be disclosed to you and agreed to before the notarization, you must be told that the fee is permitted but not required by state law, and it must be collected at the time and recorded in the notary's journal [15][16]. Other states we cover set dollar caps instead: Nebraska's $5 acknowledgment cap, Utah's $10 per signature, Wyoming's $10 per notarial act and Colorado's $15 per document. Ask the price before you start.
  • Spouses (statewide): a Kansas notary may not notarize a record in which the notary or the notary's spouse is a party or has a direct financial or beneficial interest [15][16]. We didn't find a broader Kansas rule on other relatives on the pages we checked.
  • ID and journal (statewide): a notary may accept a driver's license, passport or other government ID that expired not more than three years before the notarization, and since January 1, 2022 Kansas notaries must keep a journal [15].
  • Banks (national policy): Bank of America says notary service is available "at no cost to our customers" and "in many of our financial centers." Schedule an appointment. All signers must be present [17]. We didn't check which Olathe branches offer it. For other banks, see free notary options.

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death and marriage certificates: KDHE Office of Vital Statistics

  • No local counter found: the district court says certified marriage certificates after May 1, 1913 are available only from the Office of Vital Statistics in Topeka, and its records office refers birth, marriage and death certificate requests to Kansas Vital Statistics at 785-296-1400 [7][10]. We didn't find an Olathe or Johnson County office that issues them.
  • Fees (statewide): under K.A.R. 28-17-6, as amended July 1, 2022, certified copies of birth, death, stillbirth, marriage and divorce certificates are $20.00 for the first copy and $20.00 for each additional copy of the same record ordered at the same time [9].
  • Who can order a marriage certificate: a person named on it, parents or immediate family named on it, a legal representative, or someone who can show a direct interest in writing [7].
  • Walk-in hours and ordering options: KDHE's own pages blocked our automated check, so we didn't confirm walk-in hours or online ordering fees. Check kdhe.ks.gov or call 785-296-1400.

Marriage licenses: Johnson County District Court

  • Online only: the court says it isn't accepting in-person marriage applications; you must apply through the online portal. Self-Help Center computers at the courthouse can be used to apply and pay [6].
  • Fee: $85.50, plus $2.14 for a credit or debit card or $1.25 for an electronic check. The Clerk's Office can't take the payment, all fees are non-refundable, and the license only applies to weddings held in Kansas [6].
  • Good for (statewide): six months from the date the clerk issues it [8].
  • Certified copy timing: the county court says to wait about 2 days after you return the completed license before requesting a copy [7]. The statewide Kansas Self-Help page says to wait at least two weeks after the ceremony [8].
  • Court contact: Johnson County District Court, 150 W. Santa Fe St., Olathe, KS 66061. Court hours Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–5 p.m.; Clerk's Office 8 a.m.–noon and 1–4:30 p.m.; (913) 715-3300 [6].

Court records: Johnson County District Court Records Management

  • Contact: 150 W Santa Fe, Suite 1100, Olathe, KS 66061, (913) 715-3480. Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–noon and 1–4:30 p.m.; no in-person requests after 4:15 p.m. Online requests are also available [10].
  • Fees: the court says to call for a price quote before submitting a request. Searches on cases before 1980 require a $12.00 prepayment, no personal checks [10][11]. We didn't find a per-page copy or certification price.

Recorded documents: Johnson County Register of Deeds

  • Fees: recording a deed, mortgage or other instrument is $21.00 for the first page and $17.00 for each additional page. Copies are $0.25 per document page and certification of a copy is $13.00. The county says fees are set by K.S.A. 28-115 and include Heritage Trust Fund and technology fees [13].
  • Paying: cash, Visa, MasterCard or Discover (2.25% processor fee), check, money order or cashier's check, payable to Johnson County Register of Deeds [13].
  • Where: County Administration Building, 111 S. Cherry St., Suite 1200, Olathe, KS 66061, (913) 715-0775 [12][13][14].
  • Searching: the office has a free public computer terminal; printed copies are $0.25 a page. Email RegisterofDeeds@jocogov.org for an appointment to use it. Office hours are Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–5 p.m. [14].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [18]. The Register of Deeds accepts money orders [13]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • Bank event: INTRUST Bank's 2026 free shred list included Olathe, 18225 W 106th, on October 17, 9 a.m.–noon, with a limit of 2 boxes per customer [19]. That date has passed, and it was the last Olathe date on the list.
  • City events: the City of Olathe's website blocked our automated check, so we couldn't confirm its shredding events.
  • The UPS Store: the 119 N Parker St store offers shredding through Iron Mountain, charged per pound [3]. We didn't find its price.

For paid per-pound shredding, see where to shred documents.

Questions

Where do I get a marriage license in Olathe?

From the Johnson County District Court, online only. The license is $85.50 [6].

Where can I apply for a passport in Olathe?

The State Department's locator lists the Olathe Main Post Office and Olathe East Station [4].

How much is a copy of my deed?

The Johnson County Register of Deeds charges $0.25 per page and $13.00 to certify a copy [13].

How much can a Kansas notary charge?

We didn't find a dollar cap in the Secretary of State's guidance. A notary who charges must tell you first, get your agreement and record the fee in the journal [16].
