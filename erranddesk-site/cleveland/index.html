Cleveland, Ohio document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Cleveland, Ohio, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

This is general information, not legal advice. Prices and hours change; check the linked source before you go.
Short version: the Cleveland Public Library gives everyone a $1.00 printing credit each day, then charges $0.10 a page in black and white and $0.25 in color; self-service faxing and scanning are free [1]. The library also accepts passport applications [2][1]. The Cleveland Department of Public Health sells certified birth and death certificates for $25 each at City Hall, with same-day service in person [3]. A Cuyahoga County marriage license is $60 [4]. City residents can shred documents for free year-round [5].
One county: the Census places all of Cleveland city in Cuyahoga County [6], so the county offices on this page are Cuyahoga County offices.

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Cleveland Public Library (CPL)

  • Self-service printing and copying: every patron gets a $1.00 printing credit per day. After that, letter and legal pages are $0.10 in black and white and $0.25 in color; 11" x 17" pages are $0.20 in black and white and $0.50 in color. Ohio sales tax is included [1]. The fee schedule is dated effective July 1, 2023 [1].
  • Printouts made by staff: $0.50 a page for standard black-and-white and letter or legal color pages [7].
  • From your device: with a CPL card, send print jobs from your computer or phone through the library's web print site, or email an attachment to bwprinting@cpl.org (black and white) or colorprinting@cpl.org (color). You must be on CPL wifi to print from a mobile device [8].
  • Fax and scanning: self-service faxing from the library's multifunction devices and self-service scanning to email or your own device are free [1]. The library says printing, scanning, copying and faxing are available at all its branches [8].
  • Hours: the library's pages list service hours of Monday–Saturday 10:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m. [7]. The Main Library is at 325 Superior Ave. N.E. [2], which the Census Geocoder places in Cleveland city [9].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 2 locations with Cleveland addresses: 11459 Mayfield Rd and 7 St Clair Ave. Its city page says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [10]. Prices aren't listed online: its 7 St Clair Ave store page shows no prices and says products, services, pricing and hours of operation may vary by location [11], so call the store for prices (last checked Nov 1, 2026). The Census Geocoder places both stores in Cleveland city [10][9].

Notary in Cleveland

  • Cleveland Public Library: we didn't find notary service on the library's printing, fee or photoduplication pages [8][1][7]. Call (216) 623-2800 to ask.
  • The UPS Store: its Cleveland page lists notarizing [10]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store [11].
  • How notaries are commissioned (statewide law): the Ohio Secretary of State issues all notary commissions. Applicants must be at least 18 and Ohio residents (or attorneys admitted in Ohio who practice here), and most must submit a criminal records check from the past six months and complete an education program and test [12]. A commission lasts five years unless revoked; attorneys keep theirs as long as they qualify [13]. Ohio notaries are commissioned "for the state" [12] and have statewide jurisdiction [14].
  • Fees (statewide law): since April 3, 2025, an Ohio notary may charge up to $5 for a notarial act that isn't online, or up to $30 for an online notarization. Fees can't be charged per signature. A notary may also charge a reasonable travel fee agreed before the notarial act, and a technology fee of up to $10 for an online notarization [15].
  • Ohio Secretary of State pages: we tried to read the Secretary of State's notary pages on Nov 1, 2026, but they blocked our check, so the notary rules above come from the Ohio Revised Code.

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates (Cleveland Department of Public Health)

  • What it issues: the Office of Vital Statistics issues certified copies for births in Ohio from Dec. 20, 1908, to the present, deaths in all of Ohio from 1971 to the present, and deaths in Cuyahoga County (except Lakewood and Parma) from Dec. 20, 1908, to 1970. For Cuyahoga County births from 1849 to Dec. 19, 1908, contact the Cuyahoga County Archives at (216) 443-7250 [3].
  • Fee: $25.00 for each certified copy, and the first copy for a newborn isn't free [3]. That's more than the state's $21.50 in the statewide section below.
  • In person (same day): City Hall, Room 122, 601 Lakeside Ave., Monday–Friday 9:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m. Pay by credit card, Apple Pay, cash, check or money order payable to Cleveland Vital Statistics; in-person orders are provided the same day [3]. The Census Geocoder places 601 Lakeside Ave in Cleveland city [9].
  • By mail: send the application with a self-addressed stamped envelope and a check or money order payable to Cleveland Vital Statistics to Office of Vital Statistics, 601 Lakeside Ave. Room 122, Cleveland, Ohio 44114. Allow one to two weeks [3]. The form says not to send cash [19].
  • Online or by phone: through VitalChek, online or at (866) 691-1914, by credit card. There are added fees for credit card processing [3].
  • Questions: (216) 664-2317 or vitals@clevelandohio.gov [3].

Birth and death certificates from the state (statewide)

  • State fee (statewide): the Ohio Department of Health (ODH) charges $21.50 for a certified birth or death record. Since Jan. 1, 2025, that $21.50 is a search fee, charged whether or not a record is found [20]. State law says the state fee can't be less than $12, and that local offices and the state add a $5 fee to each certified copy [21]. Local offices can charge more than the state: compare the city fee above.
  • Any Ohio birth, at local offices (statewide): ODH says more than 100 local offices offer same-day certified copies of Ohio vital records in person or in other ways [20]. State law lets a local registrar issue a certified copy to any applicant, unless the record is no longer public [22].
  • How to order from the state (statewide): online by credit card (processed within five business days; about three weeks for delivery by mail; no expedited service), or by mail to ODH Bureau of Vital Statistics, PO Box 15098, Columbus, Ohio 43215-0098, with a check or money order (four to six weeks) [20]. Online birth certificates are abstract copies that ODH says are valid for all U.S. legal uses, including passports [20].
  • State office walk-in (statewide): 4200 Surface Road, Columbus, Monday through Friday 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., only for central Ohio customers who need an expedited copy of their birth record and pay by credit card [20].
  • Deaths before 1971 (statewide): the state's records are with the Ohio History Connection. ODH says to get certified copies from the local health department where the death happened [20].

Marriage licenses (Cuyahoga County Probate Court)

  • Where to apply (statewide law): both of you appear in person at the probate court of the county where either of you lives. If neither of you lives in Ohio, apply in the county where you plan to marry [23].
  • Waiting period and validity (statewide law): a probate judge may grant the license "at any time after the application is made" [23], and the license expires 60 days after it's issued [24]. A passport is one of the accepted proofs of age [23]. Ohio law doesn't set one statewide license fee; check the county fee below.
  • Cuyahoga County fee: $60.00. The court accepts cash, money order, cashier's check, and MasterCard, Visa, Discover and American Express credit or debit cards, with a $2.95 convenience fee for cards [4]. The court's fee page also says it doesn't accept personal checks [25].
  • Residency: one of you must be a current Cuyahoga County resident and show evidence of current residence [4].
  • How: online pre-registration is mandatory, and the registration is kept for 90 days. Then you both go to the Marriage Department together; no appointment is needed. Each of you needs a Social Security number and valid government photo ID. If either of you is divorced, bring a certified copy of the last divorce decree [4][26]. The license is valid for 60 days from issuance [4].
  • Where and when: 1 Lakeside Avenue West, Room 129 (1st Floor), Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m.; (216) 443-8920. The department won't process payment after 4:15 p.m. [4]. The Census Geocoder didn't match this address.
  • Copies: a certified copy is $2.00. By mail, send a self-addressed stamped envelope with a money order or cashier's check; mail requests take about two weeks [4].

Recorded documents (Cuyahoga County Fiscal Officer)

  • Recording fees (pages differ): the Transfer and Recording page says the standard recording fee rose to $39 for the first two pages and $8 for each page after that, effective Oct. 24, 2024 [27]. The Fees and Filings page's page-count table also starts at $39.00 for one or two pages, but the same page's basic recording fee list still shows $34.00 for the first two pages [28]. Ask the office which applies.
  • Copies: a copy of a recorded instrument is $2.00 a page; a certified copy adds $2.00 [28].
  • Where: 2079 E. Ninth Street, 4th floor; Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m., with recording from 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. [27]. The Census Geocoder places it in Cleveland city [9].

Court records (Cuyahoga County Clerk of Courts)

  • Copy fees (pages differ): the clerk's court costs page says certified copies are $1 a page, including the certificate and seal, and copies without them are $.03 a page [29]. The clerk's public records policy says Legal Division copies are $.03 a page if over 33 pages, with no cost under 33 pages [30].
  • Card fees: $2.00 for card payments up to $50.00, with higher fees for larger payments [29].
  • Where: the clerk is in the Justice Center, 1200 Ontario Street [16][30], which the Census Geocoder places in Cleveland city [9].

  • Not checked: Ohio apostilles come from the Ohio Secretary of State. We tried to read its apostille pages on Nov 1, 2026, but they blocked our check [31], so we didn't confirm the fee, what it accepts, how to submit or how long it takes. Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [32]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • Free year-round shredding for residents: the Cuyahoga County Solid Waste District says the City of Cleveland offers free, year-round document shredding for residents at the Division of Waste Collection, 5600 Carnegie Avenue, Monday–Friday 9:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m.; call (216) 664-3711 [5]. The Census Geocoder places it in Cleveland city [9].
  • Shredded paper: the district says not to put it in curbside recycling, because shreds are too small to be sorted at the recycling facility [5].
  • The UPS Store: its Cleveland page lists shredding [10]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store [11].

For paid per-pound shredding, see where to shred documents.

Questions

Where do I get a birth certificate in Cleveland?

At the Cleveland Department of Public Health's Office of Vital Statistics, City Hall Room 122, 601 Lakeside Ave., for $25.00 per copy. In-person orders are provided the same day [3].

How much is a marriage license in Cleveland?

$60.00 at the Cuyahoga County Probate Court, plus a $2.95 fee if you pay by card. One of you must live in Cuyahoga County, and you must pre-register online first [4].

Can I get a passport at the Cleveland Public Library?

Yes. The Main Library is in the passport locator, and the library's fee schedule lists a $35.00 application processing fee and $10.00 passport photos [2][1].

Can I send a fax at the Cleveland Public Library?

Yes. Self-service faxing from the library's multifunction devices is free [1].
