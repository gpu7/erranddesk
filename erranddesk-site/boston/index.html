Boston, Massachusetts document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Boston, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City of Boston, Boston Public Library, Suffolk County Registry of Deeds, the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, USPS, FedEx, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. Fees change; confirm with the office before you go. Several Boston.gov Registry and City Clerk pages were Temporarily Unavailable when we checked on Nov 5, 2026; those facts come from Internet Archive captures of the official pages, and we still link the live Boston.gov URLs.
Short version: the locator lists 6 passport acceptance facilities in Boston, all post offices [1]. The City Clerk notarizes documents for $5 [2]. Birth, death and marriage certificates are $12 in person at City Hall Room 213 [3][4][5]. Massachusetts does not cap ordinary notary fees (statewide) [6].
Counties in Massachusetts (statewide): Census geography still uses counties, but Berkshire, Essex, Franklin, Hampden, Hampshire, Middlesex and Worcester counties are no longer legal governmental units [7]. Land records are kept by county Registries of Deeds under the Secretary of the Commonwealth. Suffolk County and Boston city are governmentally consolidated [7].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

  • Boston Public Library (700 Boylston St.): black-and-white prints and copies are $0.20 per side, color $0.60 per side, and scanning $0.05. Library-card accounts start each month with $20 of free printing [8]. We didn't find a BPL fax price on the printing page (last checked Nov 5, 2026).
  • FedEx: FedEx's Boston page lists 17 locations, including FedEx Office Print & Ship Centers at 10 Post Office Sq., 131 Dartmouth St. and 2 Center Plaza [9].
  • The UPS Store: 8 Boston stores, including 71 Commercial St. ((617) 670-1121), 139 Charles St. ((617) 573-0005), 292 Newbury St. ((617) 437-9303) and 1363 Boylston St. ((617) 738-4461) [10].

Notary in Boston

  • Boston City Clerk ($5): Room 601, 1 City Hall Square. Notary hours 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Monday–Friday, no appointment. $5 per document. Bring government photo ID; don't sign before you arrive. Vital records can't be copied or notarized here [2][11].
  • Boston Public Library: notary at the Central Library in Copley Square and select branches. The BPL page does not list a BPL fee amount; it points to the City Clerk's $5 walk-in service [12]. We didn't find a BPL notary fee on that page (last checked Nov 5, 2026).
  • The UPS Store: notary rules and fees vary by state [13].
  • Notary fees (statewide): Massachusetts does not set a maximum fee for ordinary notarial acts such as acknowledgments and jurats. G.L. c. 262, § 41's $1.25 figure applies only to "noting" a dishonored negotiable instrument [14]. The Supreme Judicial Court held that cap does not limit fees for other notarial acts [6]. Ask the fee before you sit down.

Birth, death, marriage and land record copies

Boston Registry of Birth, Death and Marriage

  • Where and when: City Hall, 1 City Hall Square, Room 213. Open Monday–Friday 9 a.m.–4 p.m. Phone 617-635-4175 [3][11].
  • Fees: birth, death and marriage certificates are $12 in person and $14 by mail or online (shipping included) [3][4][5]. Cash, credit, pinless debit, check or money order to the City of Boston [3].

Birth, death and marriage copies (statewide rules)

  • City or town clerk (statewide): you can usually get a certified copy from the city or town where the event happened (or where marriage intentions were filed). Fees vary by city or town [18].
  • State Registry of Vital Records and Statistics (statewide): 150 Mt. Vernon St., 1st Floor, Dorchester. Counter hours Monday–Friday 8:45 a.m.–4:45 p.m. State fees: $20 in person, $32 by mail, or $54 for the first copy ordered online or by phone ($42 for each additional copy in that order) [19].
  • Need an apostille? (statewide): bring a certified copy with an original signature of a Massachusetts notary, city/town clerk, court clerk or Registrar of Vital Records to the Secretary of the Commonwealth [20].

Marriage licenses (Boston Registry)

  • Where: City Hall, Room 213, windows 24–25. Appointments required; walk-ins not currently taken [21].
  • Fee: $50 application fee; cash, debit/credit or money order to the City of Boston [21].
  • Waiting period: 3-day wait after filing; the license expires 60 days from the filing date [21]. A Marriage Without Delay Waiver costs $195 [21].
  • State rules (statewide): both of you apply in person at any Massachusetts city or town clerk. There is a 3-day waiting period; the license is valid for 60 days and only inside Massachusetts. You must both be 18 or older. License fees vary by town [18].

Land records (Suffolk County Registry of Deeds)

  • Office: 24 New Chardon St., Boston. Research 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m.; recording 8:30 a.m.–4:15 p.m. Monday–Friday [22]. Suffolk County and Boston city are governmentally consolidated (statewide) [7].
  • Recording fees (Suffolk): deed $155, mortgage $205, mortgage discharge $105, declaration of homestead $35. Cash, check or money order to the Commonwealth of Massachusetts; no cards [23].
  • Copies: $0.50 per page, or $1.00 per page certified [24][23].
  • Recording fees (county registries): each Registry of Deeds posts its own fee schedule (often a flat amount per document). See the city section for that county's posted fees.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who issues them (statewide): only the Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth. Walk-in at Commissions Section, One Ashburton Place, Room 1719, Boston, or regional offices in Springfield and Fall River [20][25].
  • Fee (statewide): $6.00 per document; check or money order payable to the Commonwealth of Massachusetts [20][25].
  • Mail (statewide): mailed requests take about two to three weeks; include the destination country and a prepaid return envelope [20].
  • Counter limit (statewide): up to three documents per customer at the Boston counter; four or more are ready the next business day after 3 p.m. [20].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [26]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Where can I get a $5 notary in Boston?

Boston City Clerk, Room 601, 1 City Hall Square, 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m. weekdays, no appointment [2]. BPL also offers notary at Central and some branches; its page doesn't list a BPL fee [12].

How much is a birth certificate in Boston?

$12 in person or $14 by mail/online at City Hall Room 213 [3]. Statewide RVRS copies are $20 in person, $32 by mail, or $54/$42 online or by phone [19].

How much can a Massachusetts notary charge?

There is no statewide maximum for ordinary notarial acts. The $1.25 figure in G.L. c. 262, § 41 applies only to "noting" a dishonored instrument (statewide) [14][6]. Ask the fee first.

Can I get an apostille on a Boston birth certificate?

Bring a certified copy with an original clerk or RVRS signature to the Secretary of the Commonwealth. The apostille is $6 per document (statewide) [20][25].

Where do I record a deed in Boston?

Suffolk County Registry of Deeds, 24 New Chardon St. A deed costs $155 to record [22][23].
