Minneapolis, Minnesota document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Minneapolis, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with Hennepin County, Hennepin County Library, the University of Minnesota, the City of Minneapolis, the Minnesota Department of Health, the Minnesota Secretary of State, the Minnesota Judicial Branch, the State of Minnesota, USPS, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. This is general information, not legal advice. Prices and hours change; check the linked source before you go.
Which offices serve Minneapolis: the Census Bureau's 2020 place-by-county file lists Minneapolis only in Hennepin County [1], so county services (birth and death certificates, marriage licenses, notary, recording) come from Hennepin County's seven service centers and its Recorder [2][3]. The public library is Hennepin County Library, which runs the Minneapolis libraries [4].
Short version: Hennepin County Library prints and copies are 20¢ a page in black and white (50¢ color for prints), cash only, and cardholders get up to $5 of printing free each month; faxing is free, send only, up to 25 pages a month [4]. Hennepin County notarizes for $5 at all 7 service centers [5]. The passport locator lists 3 Minneapolis facilities [6]. A certified birth certificate is $26 [7] and a marriage license $125, or $50 with premarital education [8].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Hennepin County Library

  • Free monthly allowance: library cardholders can print or copy up to 25 black-and-white pages or 10 color pages, "or any combination up to $5 in value," each month at no charge [4].
  • Prints: after that, 20¢ a page in black and white and 50¢ in color, cash only, from library computers or your own device, at all Hennepin County libraries [4].
  • Copies: coin-operated copiers, black and white only, at all libraries, 20¢ a page, cash only [4].
  • Scanning: scanners are at all Hennepin County libraries; we didn't find a scanning price on the page [4].
  • Fax: free at all Hennepin County libraries, with a request to limit faxing to 25 pages a month. You can send faxes but not receive them [4].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 4 locations in Minneapolis: 40 S 7th St, Ste. 212, (612) 332-4117; 2801 Hennepin Ave S, (612) 822-0022; 3109 W 50th St, (612) 925-0555; and 1730 New Brighton Blvd, #104, (612) 789-6904. Its city page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [9]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [10].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Minneapolis

  • Hennepin County service centers ($5): notaries are at all 7 licensing service center locations, $5 per notarization. Sign the document in front of the notary and bring photo ID. Schedule an appointment or walk in; wait times depend on staffing. The county won't notarize a last will and testament, I-9 forms, or birth, death or marriage certificates. Questions: 612-348-8240 [5].
  • Where the service centers are: Government Center, 300 South 6th Street, Suite A-025; Midtown Exchange, 2929 Chicago Avenue South, Lower Level; and North Minneapolis, 1001 Plymouth Avenue North, are in Minneapolis. The others are in Brooklyn Center, Maple Grove, Minnetonka (Ridgedale) and Edina (Southdale). All are open Monday–Friday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m.; appointments can't be booked more than four weeks ahead. Cards add a 2.49% charge for non-DVS services [2].
  • Library: we didn't find notary service on the Hennepin County Library page we checked [4].
  • The UPS Store: its Minneapolis page lists notarizing [9]. We didn't find store notary prices; the Minnesota maximums are below.
  • Fee limits (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 357.17 sets "the maximum fees to be charged and collected by a notary public": $5 for each protest and copy, $5 for making and serving each notice of nonpayment or nonacceptance, $5 per folio for "any affidavit or paper for which provision is not made herein" and $1 per folio for copies, $5 for each oath administered, $5 per folio for recording each instrument the notary is required by law to record, and for acknowledgments of deeds and other services, "the legal fees allowed other officers for like services" [11]. The Secretary of State's Notary FAQ quotes the same maximums [12]. Offices often post a flat price per signature or stamp; the local prices above are what each office lists.
  • Remote online notarization (statewide): the Secretary of State says a remote online notary public "must be physically located in the state of Minnesota to perform a remote online notarial act" and must register with its office under Minn. Stat. § 358.645 [13]. We couldn't confirm a current fee limit for remote online notarization on Oct 30, 2026, so ask the notary what it charges.

A $35 acceptance fee is paid separately to the acceptance facility, and expedited service is $60 more [16].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Minnesota Department of Health (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): the Minnesota Department of Health fee table lists a certified birth certificate at $26, with additional copies of the same certificate ordered at the same time $19 each; a noncertified (informational) birth record at $13, plus $6 for each additional copy; a certified death certificate at $13, plus $6 for each additional copy; a noncertified death record at $13, plus $6; and a verification of birth at $9. A birth certificate for a homeless youth or for the Veterans Administration, and a death certificate for the VA, are $0. Optional rush processing is $20 and rush shipping $21. The table marks these as available at "All vital records offices" [17].
  • In state law (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 144.226 sets the vital record fees. It waives the certified birth record fee for a homeless youth (subd. 8) and lets the state registrar and its agents charge a convenience fee of up to three percent for electronic, telephone or internet transactions (subd. 7) [18].

Birth and death certificates: Hennepin County

  • Birth fees: for any birth in Minnesota, a certified copy is $26 (each extra $19) and a non-certified copy $13; non-certified copies can't be used for legal purposes. A verification of birth is $9 [7].
  • Death fees: a certified copy is $13 (each extra $6) and a non-certified copy $13, for deaths in Hennepin County from 1870 and in any Minnesota county from 1997 [19].
  • How: in person at a service center, where most copies are issued the same day; or by mail, drop-off or fax. Mailed requests for certified copies must be notarized (e-notarization is OK). Faster processing (ready within 1 week) is $20 extra; applying by fax is $9.50 extra. Phone 612-348-8919 [7][19].

Marriage licenses: Hennepin County

  • Fee: $125, or $50 if you've completed 12 hours of premarital education and submit the educator's statement. Fees are non-refundable. The license is valid for 6 months, and you must be at least 18 at the time of the wedding [8].
  • How: both of you at a service center; one of you, with a notarized party-not-appearing form for the other; or nobody in person, by mailing the application, notarized supplemental forms, proof of age and a check or money order to Director of Licensing – Remote Marriage, 300 South 6th Street, MC 678-B, Minneapolis, MN 55487-0678 [8].
  • Fee and premarital education discount (sources differ): Minn. Stat. § 517.08, subd. 1b, says the local registrar collects $125 for a civil marriage license, and that the fee "for parties who have completed at least 12 hours of premarital education is $50." To get the reduced fee, you bring a statement from the educator, on letterhead, "signed, dated, and notarized or marked with a church seal." The educator must be a licensed or ordained minister or the minister's designee, a person authorized to solemnize marriages, or a licensed marriage and family therapist. Subdivision 1c(b) of the same section refers to "the $40 fee under subdivision 1b, paragraph (b)" [20]. We don't say which number governs; the county notes above give the county's own figures.
  • Term and oath (statewide): the license is valid for six months. If you can't use it within that time "due to illness or other extenuating circumstances," you can surrender it and get a new one "without fee." The registrar may take your oath in person, by telephone, by web conference or by a verified statement signed by both of you, and may accept applications by mail, fax or electronic filing [20]. Each county decides which of these it offers; see the county notes above. We didn't find a waiting period in § 517.08.

Recorded documents: Hennepin County Recorder

  • Recording: $46 per document; plats $56. Appointments 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. on the 7th floor of the Government Center's A-tower, 300 South 6th Street; walk-ins go on a wait list. Online submissions through Virtual Recorder add a $3 service fee. Phone 612-348-5139 [3].
  • Copies: online through Virtual Recorder, a certified document or certificate of title is $10 and a plat $15, plus a $3 service fee per order. By mail, a non-certified document copy is $1 a page and a certified copy $10; check payable to Hennepin County Recorder, no cards. Property owners can get a free electronic copy of their deed and certificate of title by emailing recordsrequest@hennepin.us. Self-service kiosks are in room SK-0353 on the Government Center skyway level, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. [21].
  • Recording fees (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 357.18, subd. 1, sets county recorder fees: $46 to index and record a deed or other document, $56 to record a plat, $10 for a certified copy of any record, and $2 for a noncertified copy of a document submitted for recording when you bring a copy or duplicate original with it. The county board sets the price of other noncertified copies [22].

Court copy fees (statewide)

  • District court copies (statewide): the Minnesota Judicial Branch's district court fee chart lists a certified copy of any instrument from a civil or criminal proceeding at $14.00, an exemplified and certified copy at $28.00, and an uncertified copy at $0.00. A scanning fee of $25 applies "for each 50 pages, or part thereof of the filing," and faxes carry a "$25 transmission fee for each 50 pages, or part thereof." These are Minnesota base fees; they "do not include county law library fees." Checks are payable to District Court Administrator [23]. The chart doesn't list a per-page copy fee, and we didn't find a general records search fee on it (it does list a $5 judgment search fee for each name certified) [23].
  • In state law (statewide): Minn. Stat. § 357.021, subd. 2, sets the $14 certified copy fee and says "No fee may be charged to view or download a publicly available instrument from a civil or criminal proceeding or for an uncertified copy of that instrument" [24]. Court users who can't afford a fee can ask to have it reduced or waived [23].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: the Minnesota Secretary of State authenticates "the signature of a state public official or Minnesota Notary Public on documents to be used outside the United States of America" [25]. Its office doesn't notarize documents [26].
  • Fee: "$5.00 fee for each apostille certificate," in person or by mail [26].
  • In person, by appointment only: at the Secretary of State, First National Bank Building, 332 Minnesota Street, Suite N201, Saint Paul, MN 55101. Walk-ins aren't accepted. Appointments open 14 days in advance and cover up to 10 documents; book online or call 651-296-2803, option 2. In person you can pay by credit card, debit card, cash, check or money order [26].
  • By mail: send the certified or notarized documents, the fee and the completed Authentication Request Form to the same Suite N201 address, with a check or money order made out to MN Secretary of State. Documents come back by regular USPS mail at no charge unless you include a prepaid label [26][25]. The form lists common reasons for rejection: improper notarization, a private document that isn't notarized, a missing or short fee, a document that originated outside Minnesota, and no original signature [25].
  • Processing time: not posted. The office says documents "are processed in the order that they are received"; if appointments are booked and your need is urgent, email apostille.oss@state.mn.us [26].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [27]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Can I fax at the library in Minneapolis?

Yes. Faxing is free at all Hennepin County libraries, send only, with a request to keep it to 25 pages a month [4].

How much does printing cost at the library?

20¢ a page in black and white and 50¢ in color, cash only, after a free monthly allowance of up to $5 for cardholders [4].

Where can I get something notarized in Minneapolis?

At any of Hennepin County's 7 service centers, $5 per notarization, with photo ID [5].

Where can I apply for a passport in Minneapolis?

The locator lists 3 Minneapolis facilities: the Loring Station and main post offices and the University of Minnesota Passport Office, which requires appointments [6][14].

How much is a marriage license in Hennepin County?

$125, or $50 with 12 hours of premarital education and the educator's statement [8].

Where do I get a Minneapolis birth certificate?

At a Hennepin County service center, where most copies are issued the same day, or by mail with a notarized application; a certified copy is $26 [7].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Minnesota Secretary of State in Saint Paul: $5.00 per apostille certificate (statewide), in person by appointment only (no walk-ins) or by mail with the Authentication Request Form [26].
