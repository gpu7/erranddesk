Winston-Salem, North Carolina document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Short version: Forsyth County Public Library gives 10 free black-and-white prints a day, then 15 cents a page [1]. Forsyth County birth and death certificates are $10 each at the Register of Deeds [2]. A marriage license is $60 [2][3]. North Carolina notaries can charge at most $10 per signature (statewide) [4]. The State Department's locator lists 3 passport offices with a Winston-Salem address [5].
City and county: this page covers the city the Census Bureau calls "Winston-Salem city" [6], which is in Forsyth County [7]. We use the Census Geocoder to check which addresses are inside the city [8]. A Winston-Salem mailing address doesn't always mean the city itself, so we note where the Geocoder disagrees.

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Forsyth County Public Library

  • Printing: public computers print black-and-white; the first 10 pages a day are free, then 15 cents a page. Printing isn't available for wireless users [1].
  • Copies: Central Library lists copiers and scanners [9], but we didn't find a copier price on forsyth.cc [1][9] (last checked Nov 3, 2026).
  • Fax (sources differ): the library's technology page says a send-only FAX24 machine is at Carver School Road, Central, Clemmons, Paddison Memorial and Southside. The same page's branch list marks Paddison Memorial, Rural Hall and Southside as having public fax [1]. An undated library news item says sending a U.S. fax costs $1.50 for the first page and $1.00 for each additional page [10]. We didn't find a current price on the technology page, so check before you go.
  • Register of Deeds copies: uncertified copies of recorded documents are 25 cents a page; certified copies are $5 for the first page and $2 for each additional page [2].
  • The UPS Store: its Winston-Salem page lists 8 locations, one marked "Coming Soon" (3581 Parkway Village Ct), and says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [11]. The Census Geocoder places all 7 open stores in Winston-Salem city [8]. Prices aren't listed, so call the store for prices (last checked Nov 3, 2026).

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Winston-Salem

  • Library: we didn't find a notary service on the Forsyth County Public Library pages on forsyth.cc [1][9] (last checked Nov 3, 2026).
  • Register of Deeds: the Notary Oath is given at the Register of Deeds for $10 [12]. The fee list also shows "Notarial acts $5 ea. signature" [2]; call 336-703-2700 to ask when that applies.
  • Winston-Salem State University: the locator says notary services are available at its passport office [5].
  • The UPS Store: its Winston-Salem page lists notarizing [11]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store. Notary rules vary by state [13].
  • How notaries are commissioned (statewide): the Secretary of State commissions North Carolina notaries for a five-year term. A new applicant takes an approved classroom course of at least six hours and a written exam, pays a $50 nonrefundable application fee, and then takes the oath before the county Register of Deeds within 45 days [4]. The Register of Deeds fee for that oath is $10 [14].
  • Fees (statewide): at most $10 per notarized signature for an acknowledgment, jurat, verification or proof, and $10 per person for an oath or affirmation without a signature. Remote notarization is at most $25 per signature. Travel can be charged only as actual mileage at the federal business rate, and only if you agree in writing before the trip. A notary who charges must post or show an English fee schedule, and doesn't have to charge at all [4].
  • Electronic notarization (statewide; two official pages differ): the statute lists $15 per signature for an electronic acknowledgment or jurat [4]. The Secretary of State's FAQ says $10.00 per signature for an electronic acknowledgment [15]. Ask the notary which fee applies before you start.
  • No certified copies (statewide): the Secretary of State says a North Carolina notary can't certify a document as a true copy [15]. For a certified copy of a recorded or vital record, go to the office that holds it (below). The Secretary of State's site has a "Find a Notary Near You" search [16].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death and marriage certificates (Forsyth County Register of Deeds)

  • Forsyth County records: certified copies of birth and death certificates are $10 each; uncertified copies are 50 cents. A wallet-size laminated birth certificate is $11 [2].
  • Where and when: 201 N Chestnut St., 2nd floor of the Forsyth County Government Center, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Monday–Friday [12]. The Census Geocoder places it in Winston-Salem city [8].
  • Payment: cash, bank check, business check, money order or card; personal checks aren't accepted. Cards add 3% or at least $1 [2][12].
  • Other North Carolina births: the office issues birth certificates for anyone born in North Carolina from 1971 on for $24 [12][2].
  • Online: online orders add a $4 service fee plus a card fee [19].

Birth, death and marriage certificates from the state (statewide)

  • County first (statewide): NC Vital Records says birth, death and marriage certificates can also be ordered from the Register of Deeds in the county where the event happened, usually the same day [20]. State law sets the Register of Deeds fee at $10 for a certified copy of a birth or death certificate or marriage license [14].
  • State fees (statewide): $24 for a search of one three-year period, including one copy if the record is found. The search fee isn't refunded if no record is found. Extra copies of the same certificate are $15 each, expedited processing is $15, and VitalChek adds a $13.95 processing fee [21].
  • In person or by mail (statewide): in-person service is by appointment only, Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m.–3 p.m. (last appointment 2:30 p.m.), at the Cooper Memorial Health Building, 225 N. McDowell St., Raleigh. Book at 919-733-3000, option 3; the $15 expedited fee applies to in-person service. Mail goes to North Carolina Vital Records, 1903 Mail Service Center, Raleigh, NC 27699-1900 [22].
  • Divorce (statewide): the state has divorce certificates from 1958 on [23]; the divorce decree itself is kept by the Clerk of Court in the county where the divorce was filed [20].

Marriage licenses (Forsyth County Register of Deeds)

  • State law (statewide): unmarried people 18 or older may marry [24]. The Register of Deeds fee for a license is $60, and $10 for each certified copy [14]. The license form in the statute authorizes the marriage "at any time within 60 days" [24].
  • Forsyth County fee and hours: the license is $60, or $70 with a certified copy [3]. Apply 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Monday–Friday; licenses aren't issued after 4:30 [12]. Return the completed license within 10 days after the ceremony [3].
  • Ceremonies: you can't marry in the Register of Deeds office. A magistrate ceremony is $50, cash only [3].

Recorded documents (Forsyth County Register of Deeds)

  • State law (statewide): registers of deeds charge $5 for the first page of a certified copy of a recorded instrument and $2 for each additional page [14]. Uncertified copy prices are set by each office [14].
  • Forsyth County fees: certified copies are $5 for the first page and $2 for each additional page; uncertified copies 25 cents a page [2].

Court record copies (Forsyth County Clerk of Superior Court)

  • Court copy fees (statewide): the Clerk of Superior Court charges $2.00 for the first page of each document copied and $0.25 for each additional page, $3.00 for a certificate under seal and $10.00 for exemplification of records. These are state fees under G.S. 7A-308, shown in the NC Judicial Branch costs chart in effect Jan. 1, 2026 [25][26][27] (last checked Nov 3, 2026).
  • Where and when: Forsyth County Courthouse, 175 N. Chestnut Street, Winston-Salem, phone (336) 779-6300. The clerk's office is open 8:30 a.m.–5 p.m. [28]. The Census Geocoder places it in Winston-Salem city [8].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the North Carolina Secretary of State's Authentication Office, for North Carolina documents used in foreign countries, such as birth and marriage certificates, statements of marital status, powers of attorney, diplomas and transcripts [29].
  • Fee (statewide): $10.00 per document; for adoptions, a duplicate original ordered at the same time is $5.00. Pay by check or money order to "North Carolina Secretary of State" [30][31].
  • How to send (statewide): mail a cover letter, payment, the original documents with all certifications attached and a prepaid return envelope or courier label [30] to North Carolina Secretary of State, Authentication Office, P.O. Box 29622, Raleigh, NC 27626-0622. The office is at 2 South Salisbury Street, Raleigh [29]. It no longer takes appointments; you can bring documents in without one [32].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [33]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Where do I get a birth certificate in Winston-Salem?

At the Forsyth County Register of Deeds, 201 N Chestnut St., for $10 for a Forsyth County birth [2][12]. Births elsewhere in North Carolina from 1971 on are $24 [12].

How much does it cost to print at the Forsyth County library?

The first 10 black-and-white pages a day are free, then 15 cents a page [1].

How much is a marriage license in Forsyth County?

$60, or $70 with a certified copy [3].

Can I get a passport at the Forsyth County Register of Deeds?

We didn't find passport services there. The locator lists Winston-Salem State University and two post offices [5].

Can a North Carolina notary certify a copy?

No. The Secretary of State says a North Carolina notary can't certify a document as a true copy (statewide) [15].
