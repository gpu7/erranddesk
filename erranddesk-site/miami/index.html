Miami, Florida document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Miami, Florida, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide, countywide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City of Miami, Miami-Dade County, the Miami-Dade Clerk of the Court and Comptroller, the Florida Department of Health, the Miami-Dade Public Library System, the Florida Department of State, the State of Florida, USPS, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. This is general information, not legal advice. Prices and hours change; check the linked source before you go.
Short version: the Miami-Dade Public Library System gives you 25 free print/copy pages a day, then charges 10¢ a page [1]. The Florida Department of Health in Miami-Dade County sells birth and death certificates for $20 each, $16 for each additional copy [2][3]. A Miami-Dade marriage license is $86, or $61 with a premarital course [4]. Miami City Hall takes passport applications by appointment only, on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, for a $35 fee [5].
One county, many cities: the Census places all of Miami city in Miami-Dade County [6], so the county offices on this page are Miami-Dade County offices. Many places with a "Miami" mailing address are outside Miami city, in unincorporated Miami-Dade County or in another city. We note where the Census Geocoder places each address [7].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Miami-Dade Public Library System (MDPLS)

  • Prices: you get 25 free print/copy pages a day. After that it's 10¢ a page [1]. The county's library fee schedule, effective Oct. 1, 2026, lists black-and-white printing and photocopies at $0.10 a page and color at $0.25 a page, after the first 25 free [8]. Mobile printing is 10¢ per black-and-white print and 25¢ per color print [9].
  • Copiers and scanners: public copy machines are at every library location except the UHealth | Jackson Station Library Kiosk. Every branch except that kiosk has at least one public computer with a free scanner. If you don't have a library card, you can show any form of ID at the service desk for a courtesy guest pass [1].
  • Fax (pages differ): the library's computers page says "Fax machines are not available" [1]. The county fee schedule's 10¢ line is for "Computer/Mobile Printing, Faxing and Photocopies" [8]. Call the branch before you go.
  • Where: the Main Library is at 101 West Flagler Street, tel. 305-375-2665 [10]. For the week of Nov. 2, 2026, its branch page listed Monday–Saturday 9:30 a.m.–6:00 p.m. and closed Sunday [10]. The Census Geocoder places it in Miami city [7].
  • Miami-Dade Clerk: copies of recorded documents are $1 a page, plus $2 to certify the document [11].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 31 locations with Miami addresses. Its city page says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [12]. The Census Geocoder places these 9 in Miami city: 5727 NW 7th St; 936 SW 1st Ave; 153 E Flagler St; 3692 Grand Ave; 3725 W Flagler St; 1951 NW 7th Ave, Ste 160; 2045 Biscayne Blvd; 6815 Biscayne Blvd, Ste 103; and 2520 SW 22nd St, Ste 2 [12][7]. It places 18 others in unincorporated Miami-Dade County and three in other cities (18495 S Dixie Hwy in Cutler Bay, 11231 NW 20th St in Sweetwater and 13611 S Dixie Hwy in Palmetto Bay), and it didn't match 6619 S Dixie Hwy [12][7]. Prices aren't listed online: its 153 E Flagler St store page shows no prices and says products, services, pricing and hours of operation may vary by location [13], so call the store for prices (last checked Nov 3, 2026).

Notary in Miami

  • Library: we didn't find notary service on the Miami-Dade Public Library System's computer, printing or passport pages [1][14].
  • The UPS Store: its Miami page lists notarizing [12]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store [13]. Notary rules vary by state [15].
  • How notaries are appointed (statewide law): Florida notaries are appointed by the Governor for 4 years. They must be at least 18 and legal residents of Florida, and can act only while they are within the state [16].
  • Fees (statewide law): a Florida notary may charge no more than $10 per notarial act [17]. For an online notarization, the limit is $25 per act; fees for other services, including a remote online notarization (RON) service provider's fees, aren't covered by that limit [18]. Witnessing a vote-by-mail ballot is free [17].
  • ID (statewide law): a notary may rely on an ID that is current or issued within the past 5 years and has a serial or other ID number, such as a Florida ID card or driver's license or a U.S. passport. The law lists other accepted IDs too [17].
  • Check a notary (statewide): the Florida Department of State has an online Notary Search of commissioned notaries [19]. Its Notary Section is at P.O. Box 6327, Tallahassee, FL 32314-6327 [19]; phone 850.245.6975 [20].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates (Florida Department of Health in Miami-Dade County)

  • Birth certificate fee: $20 for the first copy and $16 for each additional copy, plus $10 for rush and $3 for a protective sheath [2]. That's more than the state office's $9, listed in the statewide section below.
  • Death certificate fee: the same: $20, $16 for each additional copy, a $10 rush fee and $3 for a sheath. This office has Florida deaths from 2009 to the present. For earlier deaths, use the state Bureau of Vital Statistics in Jacksonville or VitalChek. Only a family member or legal representative can request the cause of death [3].
  • ID: bring a valid, unexpired photo ID such as a driver's license, state ID, passport or military ID. For mail requests, send a clear photocopy [2].
  • In person: the Downtown, North Dade and West Perrine offices are open Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m., no appointment needed. Questions: 305-324-2400 [2].
    • Downtown: the Health District Center, 1350 NW 14th St., offers birth and death certificates; its Vital Records line is 305-575-5030. Its location page lists building hours of 7:30 a.m.–5 p.m. on weekdays [24], which differ from the 8 a.m.–4 p.m. on the certificate pages [2]. The Census Geocoder places it in Miami city [7].
    • North Dade: 18680 NW 67th Ave., Hialeah, 305-628-7230, 8 a.m.–4 p.m. weekdays [25].
    • West Perrine: 18255 Homestead Ave., 305-234-5400 [26].
    • The Census Geocoder places the North Dade and West Perrine offices in unincorporated Miami-Dade County, outside Miami city [7].
  • Older births: Florida births before 1930 must be requested from the state Bureau of Vital Statistics in Jacksonville [2]. Records of Florida marriages before June 6, 1927 come from the Clerk of Court in the county that issued the license [27].

Birth and death certificates from the state (statewide)

  • State birth certificate fees (statewide): the Florida Bureau of Vital Statistics charges $9 for the first computer-generated birth certification, which includes a non-refundable $9 search fee, and $4 for each additional copy. A photocopy certificate is $14 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy. Rush service is $10 more, and shipping is $1 [28].
  • Who can get a birth certificate (statewide): births less than 125 years old are confidential. Only the person named (if 18 or older), a parent on the record, a legal guardian, a legal representative or someone with a court order can get one, with a valid photo ID [28].
  • State death certificate fees (statewide): $5 for a one-year search and one certified copy (the $5 search fee isn't refundable), $4 for each additional copy, $2 for each extra year searched (up to $50), $10 for rush service and $1 for shipping. Records before 2009 need the $10 rush fee for same-day walk-in service, when available [29].
  • Who can get a death certificate (statewide): anyone 18 or older can get a certified copy without the cause of death. Records less than 50 years old that show the cause of death are confidential and limited to family and others the department lists [29].
  • State office walk-in (statewide): 1217 N Pearl St., Jacksonville, FL 32202; lobby hours Monday through Friday, 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Pay by cash, check, money order, Visa or Mastercard; contactless and tap-to-pay aren't accepted [28][29].
  • By mail (statewide): Florida Department of Health, Vital Statistics, P.O. Box 210, Jacksonville, FL 32231-0042. Computer-generated birth certificates normally take 3–5 business days plus shipping. Mark the envelope RUSH and add $10 for priority processing [28].
  • Online (statewide): VitalChek is the only vendor the state contracts with. A birth certificate through VitalChek is $19 for the first copy (including the $9 search fee and $10 rush fee), plus a $7 VitalChek processing fee [28].
  • County health departments (statewide): they issue Florida birth records from 1917 to the present, and the state says fees vary by county [28]. Compare the county fee above.

Marriage licenses (Miami-Dade Clerk of the Court and Comptroller)

  • Who issues licenses (statewide law): a county court judge or the clerk of the circuit court [30].
  • Waiting period (statewide law): if you don't give the clerk certificates showing you finished a premarital preparation course, the license takes effect 3 days after you apply. The clerk must make exceptions for people who don't live in Florida and for couples who claim hardship, and a county judge may waive the wait for Florida residents with good cause [31].
  • Course discount (state law has two figures): the course must be at least 4 hours, and section 741.0305 says it cuts your license fee by $32.50 [32]. Section 741.01 says the fee is reduced by $25 for couples with course certificates [30]. Check the county fee below for what you'd actually pay.
  • Validity (statewide law): a Florida marriage license is valid for 60 days after it's issued [33].
  • Miami-Dade fee: $86, or $61 if you complete a premarital preparation course [4]. The course must be at least 4 hours, from a Miami-Dade registered provider, and completed no more than one year before you apply. It also waives the three-day waiting period for Florida residents. Non-Florida residents have no waiting period [4].
  • How: complete the online pre-application before you visit. The office is open Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m., except legal holidays. Appointments are available and walk-ins are accepted, though waits may be longer. Both applicants must be there in person [4].
  • Where, payment and validity: the Central Marriage License Bureau is at 601 NW 1st Court, Room 1900 [4]. The Census Geocoder places it in Miami city [7]. Payment can be cash, cashier's check, money order, Visa, Discover, MasterCard or American Express. A license is valid for 60 days. A Clerk ceremony costs $30.00 [4].
  • Certified copies (pages differ): the certified-copies page says $9 for the first copy and $7 for each additional copy. If you don't know the year, add $2 per year searched [34]. The marriage page says additional certified copies cost $9.00 each. Its fee list shows "Certified copy - $7", "Additional certified copy (each) - $7" and "Marriage status search and certificate - $9" [4]. Confirm the price when you order.
  • Ordering copies: licenses from 1971 to the present can be ordered online; earlier ones by mail or in person. The mail address is Marriage License Bureau, Overtown Transit Village South, 601 NW 1 Court, Suite 1900, Miami, FL 33136. Personal checks aren't accepted. For a license from another county, contact Florida Vital Statistics at 904-359-6900 [34].

Recorded documents (Miami-Dade Clerk of the Court and Comptroller)

  • State base fees (statewide law): state law lists recording fees of $5.00 for the first page and $4.00 for each additional page, and requires additional service charges on top [35]. The county total below is what you pay.
  • Miami-Dade recording fees: $10 for the first page and $8.50 for each additional page. A lis pendens is $5 for the first page and $4 for each additional page [11].
  • Taxes on deeds: documentary stamps are $0.60 per $100 of consideration ($6 per $1,000). There's also a surtax of $0.45 per $100 ($4.50 per $1,000) on transfers of real property other than a single-family residence [11].
  • Payment: cash (in person only), cashier's check, money order, or a check from a title company, attorney or mortgage company. Credit cards are accepted in person only, with a $5,000 daily limit [11].
  • Certified copies: $1 a page and $2 per document for the certification, plus a $2 search fee per name and year. Records older than 1974 are only available in person or by mail. The mail address is Miami-Dade County, P.O. Box 011711, Flagler Station, Miami, FL 33101 [36].
  • Where (pages differ on the street): the Osvaldo N. Soto Miami-Dade Justice Center. The recording page gives the County Recorder's address as 20 NW 1st Avenue, Suite 5.246 [11]. The certified-copies page says 20 NW 1st Street, Suite 5.246, and its footer says 20 NW 1st Avenue, 305-275-1155 [36]. The Census Geocoder places 20 NW 1st Ave in Miami city [7].

Court records (Miami-Dade Clerk of the Court and Comptroller)

  • State fee limits (statewide law): Florida law sets clerk charges of $2.00 for certifying a copy of a court record and $1.00 a page for copies up to 14 by 8½ inches [35].
  • Miami-Dade court copies: $1 a page, plus $2 per document to certify [37][38]. If you don't know the year or case number, there's an added search fee of $2 per year, per name, for every year searched [39][37]. Civil copies in person: Civil & Probate Courthouse, Civil Department, 20 NW 1st Avenue, Suite 6.240 [37].
  • Exemplified copies: we didn't find a price for exemplified copies on the Clerk's certified-copies, civil certified-copies or records library pages [39][37][38].
  • Payment: online by Visa, Discover, Mastercard or American Express. By mail, use a cashier's check or money order. In person, use cash, a card, a cashier's check or a money order. Personal checks aren't accepted [39][37].

Apostilles and notarial certifications (statewide)

  • What it is (statewide): the Florida Secretary of State is the only authority in Florida that issues apostilles and notarial certifications [40]. Countries that aren't in the 1961 Hague Convention get a notarial certification instead of an apostille [41].
  • Fee (statewide): $10.00 per document, or $20.00 per document certified by any Florida county Clerk of the Court ($10 for the apostille and $10 for a certificate of incumbency) [40]. State law caps the apostille fee at $10 per apostille [42].
  • Payment (statewide): a check or money order payable to the Florida Department of State, in U.S. currency from a U.S. bank. Cash and credit cards aren't accepted [40].
  • By mail (statewide): send the request form, the document (an original certified copy, not a photocopy) and a self-addressed stamped envelope or prepaid airbill [40] to Department of State, Division of Corporations, Apostilles Section, P.O. Box 6800, Tallahassee, FL 32314; phone 850.245.6053 [20]. Requests are processed in the order received, and there's no expedited service [41].
  • Walk-in (statewide): 2415 N. Monroe Street, Suite 810, Tallahassee, Monday through Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m., except state holidays. Most requests can be completed while you wait [41].
  • Birth, death and marriage records (statewide): birth and death certificates must be signed by the State Registrar, whether issued by the Bureau of Vital Statistics or a county health department. Marriage and divorce certificates must be signed by the State Registrar or the Clerk of the County Courts [43].
  • Elsewhere (statewide): federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State, and documents from another state go to that state [40]. The office doesn't translate, and the translator can't be the notary [41].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [44]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • Free shredding (countywide, upcoming): Miami-Dade County's 2026 Home Chemical Drop-Off Mobile Events include free document shredding for county residents, subject to availability. The limit is two bankers boxes or the equivalent per person [45]. The last 2026 event is Saturday, Nov. 14, 8:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m., at the Sunset Kendall Trash and Recycling Center, 8000 SW 107th Ave. Bring a Florida driver's license or ID card to show Miami-Dade residency [46]. The Census Geocoder places the site in unincorporated Miami-Dade County, outside Miami city [7]. The earlier 2026 events, February through Oct. 3, are past [46].
  • The UPS Store: its Miami page lists shredding [12]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store [13].

Questions

Where do I get a birth certificate in Miami?

At the Florida Department of Health in Miami-Dade County, for $20 [2]. Its downtown office is the Health District Center, 1350 NW 14th St. [24]. The state Bureau of Vital Statistics in Jacksonville charges $9 for a computer-generated birth certification [28].

How much is a marriage license in Miami?

$86 at the Miami-Dade Clerk of the Court and Comptroller, or $61 with a premarital course [4].

Can I get a passport at Miami City Hall?

Yes, by appointment only, on Tuesday and Wednesday from 9:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. The fee is $35. City Hall doesn't process DS-82 renewals [5].

How much does a Florida notary charge?

No more than $10 per notarial act under state law, or $25 for an online notarization [17][18].

How much does printing cost at the Miami library?

You get 25 free print or copy pages a day. After that, the county fee schedule effective Oct. 1, 2026 lists black-and-white printing and photocopies at $0.10 a page and color at $0.25, and mobile printing is 10¢ in black and white and 25¢ in color [1][8][9].
