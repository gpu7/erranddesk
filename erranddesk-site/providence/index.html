Providence, Rhode Island document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Providence, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City of Providence, Community Libraries of Providence, Providence Public Library, the State of Rhode Island, USPS, FedEx, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. Fees change; confirm with the office before you go.
Short version: the locator lists 2 passport acceptance facilities in Providence, both post offices [1]. Community Libraries of Providence offers free notary service and free fax sending [2][3]. Birth certificates are $22 walk-in at City Hall [4]. Rhode Island notaries can charge up to $25 per document, so ask first (statewide) [5].
No county clerk in Rhode Island (statewide): Rhode Island has no county government [6]. Its counties were abolished as governmental units and survive only as geographic areas [7]. Land records are recorded in the city or town where the property is, and city and town clerks issue birth, death and marriage copies.

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

  • Community Libraries of Providence: printing is 20¢ a page black and white and 50¢ color. Every location has a fax machine, usually at the circulation desk. Sending a fax is free, but the libraries don't receive incoming faxes [3].
  • Providence Public Library (150 Empire St.): printing is 20¢ a page black and white and 35¢ color. You can print from your own device through Princh or by emailing the library's print address, then release the job at a print terminal [8].
  • FedEx: FedEx's Providence page lists 8 locations, including a FedEx Office Print & Ship Center at 236 Meeting St. [9].
  • The UPS Store: 2 Providence stores, at 22 Parsonage St. ((401) 369-7029) and 11 S. Angell St. ((401) 751-6245) [10].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Providence

  • Community Libraries of Providence (free): the service is free of charge when a notary is in. Call ahead or book online. Mt. Pleasant Library has bookable notaries; at South Providence Library, call Emily LeMay at 401-467-2619 [2].
  • Providence Public Library ($5): $5.00 to start, plus $5.00 for each 5 pages over 10, up to 20 pages. Walk in or book; call 401-455-8000 to confirm a notary is there. No copy certifications [11].
  • The UPS Store: notary rules and fees vary by state [12].
  • Notary fees (statewide): a Rhode Island notary may charge up to $25 per document or notarization, including remote online notarization, and must tell you the fee before notarizing [5][13]. That cap is higher than in most nearby states, so ask first.

Birth, death, marriage and land record copies

Providence Vital Records

  • Where and when: City Hall, 25 Dorrance St., Room 104 [17]. Open 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Monday–Friday (closes at 4 p.m. in July and August). Phone 401-680-5213. No cash [4].
  • Fees: birth, death and marriage certificates are $22.00 walk-in and $25.00 by mail [4]. Extra copies of the same record bought the same day are $18.00 each [18].

Birth, death and marriage copies (statewide rules)

  • Any city or town hall (statewide): births from 1960 on, and deaths and marriages from 2022 on, can be ordered at any Rhode Island city or town hall. Older records come from the city or town where the event happened or where you lived [19].
  • Fees (statewide): $22 for the first copy in person and $25 by mail, plus $18 for each extra copy of the same record bought at the same time [20].
  • State office (statewide): RIDOH Center for Vital Records, Simpson Hall, 6 Harrington Rd., Cranston, weekdays 7:30 a.m.–3:30 p.m. Mail orders take 4–6 weeks; rush orders cost $7 more [20].
  • Need an apostille? (statewide): order the copy from the state office, not a city hall. RIDOH says vital records issued by a city or town can't be apostilled [19].

Marriage licenses (Providence Vital Records)

  • When: in person, both of you, Monday–Friday 9 a.m.–3:30 p.m. (9 a.m.–3 p.m. in July and August) [4].
  • Fee (sources disagree): the Vital Records page lists "$24.00 (+ a $3.00 processing fee)" [4]. The city's checklist lists $24.00 by check or money order and $25.00 by debit or credit card, with no cash [17]. Ask which applies to your payment.
  • Who can apply here: Providence residents can marry anywhere in Rhode Island. Out-of-state couples must hold the ceremony in Providence. Both must be 18 or older, and the license expires 3 months after it's issued [17].
  • State rules (statewide): both of you apply in person at a city or town clerk. Apply where either of you lives if one of you lives in Rhode Island; if neither does, apply where the ceremony will be. RIDOH lists the fee as $24, and the license is good for 3 months [21][22].
  • Sources disagree (statewide): R.I. Gen. Laws § 15-2-9 sets the clerk's license fee at $14.00 [23], while RIDOH lists $24 [22]. We didn't find which law adds the other $10 (last checked Nov 4, 2026). Clerks charge the $24.

Land records (Recorder of Deeds)

  • Office: City Hall, 25 Dorrance St. Bring documents for recording to Room 508, 8:30 a.m.–3:30 p.m. (3 p.m. in summer). Title research is in Room 506. Phone 401.680.5545 [24].
  • Fees: the city posts the statewide fee list dated December 2012, such as $84 plus $1 for each extra page for a lis pendens [25]. The statewide law is below.
  • Recording fees (statewide law): R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-13-7 sets a base fee by document type, such as $80 for a deed and $60 for a mortgage, plus $1 for each extra page [26]. A $4 Historical Records Trust charge is added to each recorded document [27]. That's why clerks post $84 and $64.
  • Copies (statewide law): § 34-13-9 sets copies of recorded documents at $1.50 a page and $3.00 to certify [28].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Rhode Island Department of State, Business Services Division, 148 West River Street, Providence. Bring or mail the document with a request form; checks and money orders go to the RI Department of State [29].
  • Fee (statewide): $5 per document [29]. Ordering online adds a $0.75 enhanced access fee per document; you still mail in the original [30]. For related documents in one matter filed together, the total is capped at $150 [31].
  • Returns (statewide): orders come back by USPS first-class mail unless you include a prepaid DHL, FedEx or UPS label [29].
  • Vital records (statewide): only state-issued copies can be apostilled [19]. You can ask RIDOH to send the record directly to the Department of State [29].

More on certified copies: how to get a certified copy.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [32]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Where can I get a free notary in Providence?

Community Libraries of Providence offers free notary service; call ahead or book [2]. Providence Public Library charges $5.00 [11].

Where can I send a fax for free in Providence?

Any Community Libraries of Providence location. Incoming faxes aren't accepted [3].

How much is a birth certificate in Providence?

$22.00 walk-in or $25.00 by mail [4], and $18.00 for each extra copy bought the same day [18].

How much can a Rhode Island notary charge?

Up to $25 per document or notarization, disclosed in advance (statewide) [5].

Can I get an apostille on a Providence birth certificate?

Not on a city-issued copy. Order the certificate from the state Center for Vital Records instead (statewide) [19]. The apostille itself is $5 per document [29].
