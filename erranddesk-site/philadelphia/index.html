Philadelphia, Pennsylvania document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the City of Philadelphia, the Philadelphia Register of Wills, the Department of Records, the First Judicial District of Pennsylvania, the Free Library of Philadelphia, the Pennsylvania Department of State, the Pennsylvania Department of Health, USPS, The UPS Store or the U.S. Department of State. Fees change; confirm with the office before you go.
Short version: the Register of Wills issues marriage licenses at City Hall Room 413 for $90 (no cash) [1]. The Department of Records copies recorded deeds for $2 a page plus $2 to certify [2]. The locator lists 8 passport facilities in the city, including the Office of Judicial Records in City Hall [3]. Pennsylvania caps most notary fees at $5 [4].
One city, one county: Philadelphia is a consolidated city and county, so the city of Philadelphia and Philadelphia County cover the same area [5][6]. City offices do the county jobs: the Register of Wills issues marriage licenses, the Department of Records records and copies deeds, and the Office of Judicial Records keeps Court of Common Pleas records [7][8][9].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

  • Free Library of Philadelphia: its Printing Services page lists 47 locations with printing but no prices [10], so we didn't find its print, copy, scan or fax prices on an official page (last checked Nov 4, 2026). Its only fax mention we found was a link to a third-party service, not a library service.
  • The UPS Store: its Philadelphia page lists 18 locations and says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [11]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store.
  • Court copies: the Office of Judicial Records fee schedule lists photocopies of records at $0.25 a page [9].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Philadelphia

  • Office of Judicial Records: its passport locator entry says photocopy and notary services are available [3]. We didn't find its notary fee or notary hours on the office's official pages (last checked Nov 4, 2026); the statewide caps below apply.
  • The UPS Store: its Philadelphia page lists notarizing [11]. Notary rules vary by state [12].
  • Notary fees (statewide): the Pennsylvania Department of State sets notary fees by regulation, and a notary can't charge more. The maximums are $5 per acknowledgment (plus $2 for each additional name on the same certificate), $5 per oath or affirmation, $5 per verification, $5 per signature witnessed, $5 per certified copy and $3 a page to note a protest. Electronic and remote notaries may add up to $20 per act [4].
  • Extra charges (statewide): a notary may waive the fee. Clerical or administrative fees, such as copying, postage or travel, aren't set by the Department; they must be reasonable, disclosed before the notarization and itemized on the receipt [4].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates from the state (statewide)

  • Fee (statewide): $20 per birth certificate and $20 per death certificate from the Pennsylvania Department of Health; online orders through VitalChek, the state's only authorized online vendor, add a $10 fee [16][17].
  • Who can order (statewide): birth certificate applicants must be at least 16 for in-person and mail requests; death certificate applicants must be at least 18 [16][17].
  • In person (statewide): the Department of Health has six Vital Records public offices: Erie, Harrisburg, New Castle, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh and Scranton. A request received before 2:30 p.m. may be filled the same day [18].
  • Philadelphia state office: 110 N. 8th Street, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m., by appointment only since October 6, 2025 [18].

Marriage licenses (Register of Wills)

  • Fees: $90 for a regular license, $100 for a Quaker or self-uniting license; Visa, Mastercard or money order only, no cash or personal checks [1]. The fee schedule lists the same $90 and $100 [19].
  • Copies and replacements, two official sources: the city's marriage license page lists $15 per extra certified copy at application, $40 per copy afterward and $10 to replace a lost license [1]. The Register of Wills fee schedule (PDF dated Nov 18, 2024) lists a $25 certified copy from the Marriage Record Department, a $25 replacement license, a $50 exemplified copy, a $45 search and $45 same-day service [19]. We show both; ask which applies.
  • How: walk-ins Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–3:15 p.m. at City Hall, Room 413. The license is valid on the third day after you apply and for 60 days from issue. Questions: (215) 686-2233 [1].

Deeds and recorded documents (Department of Records)

  • Copies: $2 a page for recorded legal documents, plus $2 per document to certify [2].
  • In person or by mail: City Hall, Room 154, Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m.–3:30 p.m. Cash, money order, business or certified check, or card (3.5% fee); no personal checks. Page counts: (215) 686-2292 [8].
  • Online: Philadox subscriptions are $15 a day, $60 a week, $125 a month or $750 a year. New recordings aren't searchable for two to four weeks [8].

Court records and divorce decrees (Office of Judicial Records)

  • Fees (effective Nov 12, 2025): $41.58 for a certification, $58.52 for a name search and $0.25 a page for photocopies [9].
  • Where, sources differ: the Civil page lists OJR's offices in Room 266, City Hall, Monday–Friday 9 a.m.–5 p.m., and the Civil Filing Center in Room 296, Monday–Friday 9 a.m.–4:30 p.m. [20]. The office's overview page lists Civil in Room 284, City Hall [21]. Court offices are open 9 a.m.–5 p.m. weekdays unless stated otherwise [22] (last checked Nov 4, 2026).
  • Passport hours, sources differ: the Passports page says 10 a.m.–2:30 p.m. weekdays, by appointment only [23]; the Civil page says 10 a.m.–3 p.m. [20].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Pennsylvania Department of State, North Office Building, Room 201, 401 North Street, Harrisburg. The fee is $15 per document (not per page). Walk-in service no longer takes cash; checks, money orders and credit cards are accepted. The building's front doors lock after 3 p.m. Mail requests typically take two to three weeks, not counting mailing time [24].
  • County documents (statewide): marriage certificates must be signed by the register of wills or clerk of the orphans' court, not only the officiant [24].

More on certified copies: how to get a certified copy.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [25]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • The UPS Store: its Philadelphia page lists shredding [11]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store.
  • Free events: we didn't find an upcoming free shred event on a City of Philadelphia page (last checked Nov 4, 2026).

Questions

How much is a marriage license in Philadelphia?

$90, or $100 for a Quaker or self-uniting license, with no cash accepted [1].

How do I get a copy of a deed in Philadelphia?

From the Department of Records, City Hall Room 154: $2 a page, plus $2 to certify [8][2].

Where do I apply for a passport in Philadelphia?

The locator lists 8 facilities, including the Office of Judicial Records in City Hall and the post office at 3000 Chestnut Street [3].

How much can a Pennsylvania notary charge?

Statewide, $5 per acknowledgment (plus $2 for each extra name), signature witnessed or oath [4].
