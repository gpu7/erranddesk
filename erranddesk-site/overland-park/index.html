Overland Park, Kansas document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Overland Park, Kansas, the largest city in Johnson County, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those. Most county offices that serve Overland Park are in Olathe, the county seat.

Which offices serve Overland Park: this page is about Overland Park, Kansas, in Johnson County. It's not about Kansas City, Missouri. Its library system is Johnson County Library, whose Central Resource Library is at 9875 W. 87th St. in Overland Park [4]. The Johnson County Register of Deeds records property documents at the County Administration Building, 111 S. Cherry St., Olathe [13]. Marriage licenses come from the Johnson County District Court, 150 W. Santa Fe St., Olathe, not a county clerk [7]. Birth, death and marriage certificates come from the state KDHE Office of Vital Statistics in Topeka [8][11].
Short version: Johnson County Library prints and copies for 15¢ a page in black and white, and cardholders get $1.50 of free printing a day [1]. The library doesn't fax or notarize [2][3]. The State Department's locator lists one Overland Park passport facility, the Brookridge post office [5]. A marriage license is $85.50 and must be applied for online [7]. Kansas certified birth, death and marriage copies are $20 each (statewide) [10].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Johnson County Library

  • Where and when: the Central Resource Library, 9875 W. 87th St., Overland Park, KS 66212, 913-826-4600, is open Mon–Thu 9 a.m.–8 p.m., Fri 9 a.m.–6 p.m., Sat 9 a.m.–5 p.m. and Sun 1–5 p.m. It lists a copier (scan to print) and a scanner among its amenities [4]. You can print and copy at all of the library's locations [1].
  • Printing and copying: 15¢ per single-sided page and 30¢ double-sided in grayscale; 50¢ single-sided and $1 double-sided in color. Standard 8.5" x 11" white paper only (no legal size), and you can't load your own paper [1].
  • Free daily allowance: library cardholders can print up to $1.50 a day at no cost. Upload or email print jobs and pick them up by the end of the same business day [1].
  • Scanning: all Johnson County Library branches offer scanning [2]. We didn't find a scanning price.
  • Fax: the library says it "does not offer faxing services." Its help page names nearby businesses that do, including, near Central, the FedEx Office at 13450 W. 87th St. Pkwy. and The UPS Store at 9218 Metcalf Ave., and says to call them for availability and pricing [2].

The UPS Store

The UPS Store #4279 at 9218 Metcalf (Regency Park Shopping Center, ZIP 66212), (913) 649-2925, lists printing, faxing and scanning, shredding and notary services. Its page lists hours of Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–6 p.m. and Sat 8 a.m.–4 p.m. [19]. We didn't find prices on its page. The UPS Store has other Overland Park locations; this is the one nearest the Central library we checked.

Notary in Overland Park

  • Library: "The Johnson County Library does not offer notary services," according to its help page, which says limited notary service may be available at Olathe Libraries and that many banks offer notary services if you have an account [3].
  • The UPS Store: the 9218 Metcalf store lists notary service [19]. We didn't find its notary hours or price.
  • Fees (statewide): the Kansas Secretary of State says "State law does not prohibit a notary from charging a fee for a notarial act." We didn't find a dollar limit in its notary page or handbook. Under K.A.R. 7-43-16 a fee must be disclosed to you and agreed to before the notarization, you must be told that the fee is permitted but not required by state law, and it must be collected at the time and recorded in the notary's journal [15][16]. Other states we cover set dollar caps instead: Nebraska's $5 acknowledgment cap, Utah's $10 per signature, Wyoming's $10 per notarial act and Colorado's $15 per document. Ask the price before you start.
  • Spouses (statewide): a Kansas notary may not notarize a record in which the notary or the notary's spouse is a party or has a direct financial or beneficial interest [15][16]. We didn't find a broader Kansas rule on other relatives on the pages we checked.
  • ID and journal (statewide): a notary may accept a driver's license, passport or other government ID that expired not more than three years before the notarization, and since January 1, 2022 Kansas notaries must keep a journal [15].
  • Banks (national policy): Bank of America says notary service is available "at no cost to our customers" and "in many of our financial centers." Schedule an appointment. All signers must be present [17]. We didn't check which Overland Park branches offer it. For other banks, see free notary options.

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death and marriage certificates: KDHE Office of Vital Statistics

  • No local counter found: the district court says certified marriage certificates after May 1, 1913 are available only from the Office of Vital Statistics in Topeka, and its records office refers birth, marriage and death certificate requests to Kansas Vital Statistics at 785-296-1400 [8][11]. We didn't find an Overland Park or Johnson County office that issues them.
  • Fees (statewide): under K.A.R. 28-17-6, as amended July 1, 2022, certified copies of birth, death, stillbirth, marriage and divorce certificates are $20.00 for the first copy and $20.00 for each additional copy of the same record ordered at the same time [10].
  • Who can order a marriage certificate: a person named on it, parents or immediate family named on it, a legal representative, or someone who can show a direct interest in writing [8].
  • Ordering (statewide): KDHE's Office of Vital Statistics is at 1000 SW Jackson, Suite 120, Topeka, 785-296-1400. Walk-in service is 9 a.m.–4 p.m. and phone service 8 a.m.–5 p.m., Mon–Fri except holidays. Copies are $20 each in person or by mail (check or money order, 7 to 10 business days). Online through VitalChek, or by phone at 877-305-8315, they're $20 each plus a $15 expedited service fee; KDHE says online orders go out within 3 to 5 business days after it receives them. The IKAN mobile app charges $20 plus a $5 processing fee, one copy per order. We didn't find VitalChek's shipping prices on KDHE's pages [22]. Walk-in details are in our Topeka guide.

Marriage licenses: Johnson County District Court

  • Online only: the court says it isn't accepting in-person marriage applications; you must apply through the online portal. Self-Help Center computers at the courthouse can be used to apply and pay [7].
  • Fee: $85.50, plus $2.14 for a credit or debit card or $1.25 for an electronic check. The Clerk's Office can't take the payment, all fees are non-refundable, and the license only applies to weddings held in Kansas [7].
  • Good for (statewide): six months from the date the clerk issues it [9].
  • Certified copy timing: the county court says to wait about 2 days after you return the completed license before requesting a copy [8]. The statewide Kansas Self-Help page says to wait at least two weeks after the ceremony [9].
  • Court contact: 150 W. Santa Fe St., Olathe, KS 66061. Court hours Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–5 p.m.; Clerk's Office 8 a.m.–noon and 1–4:30 p.m.; (913) 715-3300 [7].

Court records: Johnson County District Court Records Management

  • Contact: 150 W Santa Fe, Suite 1100, Olathe, KS 66061, (913) 715-3480. Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–noon and 1–4:30 p.m.; no in-person requests after 4:15 p.m. Online requests are also available [11].
  • Fees: the court says to call for a price quote before submitting a request. Searches on cases before 1980 require a $12.00 prepayment, no personal checks [11][12]. We didn't find a per-page copy or certification price.

Recorded documents: Johnson County Register of Deeds

  • Fees: recording a deed, mortgage or other instrument is $21.00 for the first page and $17.00 for each additional page. Copies are $0.25 per document page and certification of a copy is $13.00. The county says fees are set by K.S.A. 28-115 and include Heritage Trust Fund and technology fees [13].
  • Paying: cash, Visa, MasterCard or Discover (2.25% processor fee), check, money order or cashier's check, payable to Johnson County Register of Deeds [13].
  • Searching: the office has a free public computer terminal; printed copies are $0.25 a page. Email RegisterofDeeds@jocogov.org for an appointment to use it. Office hours are Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–5 p.m. [14].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [18]. The Register of Deeds accepts money orders [13]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • City event: the most recent City of Overland Park shredding event we found was on Sept. 19, 2026, 9 a.m.–noon, at 5800 W 107th St, with documents accepted for a suggested donation of $5 per box and no residency requirement [21]. We didn't find a later date on the pages we checked.
  • The UPS Store: the 9218 Metcalf store offers shredding through Iron Mountain, charged per pound [20]. We didn't find its price.

For paid per-pound shredding, see where to shred documents.

Questions

Can I fax at the Johnson County Library?

No. The library doesn't offer faxing and points to nearby businesses that do [2].

Is printing free at the library?

Cardholders get up to $1.50 of printing a day at no cost. After that, black-and-white pages are 15¢ [1].

Where do I get a marriage license in Overland Park?

From the Johnson County District Court in Olathe, online only. The license is $85.50 [7].

How much can a Kansas notary charge?

We didn't find a dollar cap in the Secretary of State's guidance. A notary who charges must tell you first, get your agreement and record the fee in the journal [16].
