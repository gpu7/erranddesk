San Diego, California document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in the City of San Diego, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the San Diego Public Library, the City or County of San Diego, the Superior Court of California, the California Department of Public Health, the California Secretary of State, the State of California, San Diego State University, USPS, The UPS Store, the U.S. Department of State or any business named here. This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
Which offices serve San Diego: the San Diego County Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk issues marriage licenses, records documents and sells birth, death and marriage copies. Its main Recorder/Clerk office is downtown in the County Administration Center, 1600 Pacific Highway, Suite 260; it also has Recorder/Clerk offices in Chula Vista, Santee and San Marcos. Its Kearny Mesa office doesn't offer Recorder/Clerk services [1]. State birth, death and marriage copies also come from the California Department of Public Health [2]. Apostilles come from the California Secretary of State [3].
Short version: San Diego Public Library prints are $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.30 in color, and faxing and scanning on library copiers are free [4]. The locator lists 10 San Diego passport facilities, including the City Clerk, a county office and 7 post offices [5]. California notaries may charge at most $15 per signature (statewide), and a 2026 law raises that to $20 [6][7]. A San Diego County public marriage license is $129 [8].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

San Diego Public Library

  • Printing: black-and-white prints are $0.15 a page and color $0.30 a page. You can pay online or pay cash at the library's front desk when you pick up; the library doesn't give refunds [4].
  • Remote printing: send jobs from a phone, tablet or computer with the Princh app or print.princh.com, using the printer ID of the library you'll visit, and pick them up within 24 hours with a 4-digit release code. Jobs are deleted after 24 hours, and staff can't see the release code. Old print cards aren't accepted and their balances can't be refunded [4].
  • Fax and scanning: free. You can send digital faxes to U.S. phone numbers from library copiers, and scan to a USB flash drive or email; ask staff for details [4].
  • Central Library: 330 Park Blvd., San Diego, CA 92101, 619-236-5800. Regular hours are Mon–Tue 11:30 a.m.–8 p.m., Wed–Fri 9:30 a.m.–6 p.m. and Sat 10 a.m.–4 p.m.; closed Sunday [9].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 38 locations in San Diego; see its city page for addresses and phone numbers. That page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [10]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [11].

Notary in San Diego

  • Library: we didn't find notary service on the San Diego Public Library pages we checked.
  • County: the Recorder/County Clerk says its office doesn't offer notary public services [12]. It does keep a list of Authorized Notary Publics who can issue confidential marriage licenses, sometimes outside office hours; confirm with the notary directly [8].
  • The UPS Store: its San Diego page lists notarizing [10]. We didn't find store notary prices; California's legal maximum is below.
  • Fees now (statewide): California Government Code § 8211 caps notary fees at $15 for each signature on an acknowledgment and $15 for an oath or affirmation with a jurat; certifying a copy of a power of attorney is also $15 [6]. The Secretary of State's January 2026 Notary Public Handbook lists the same maximums and says a notary may charge less or nothing [13]. Notarizing vote-by-mail ballot envelopes, and a veteran's application or claim for benefits, is free [6].
  • Scheduled change (statewide): AB 1597 (Chapter 39, Statutes of 2026), approved by the Governor on June 30, 2026, amends § 8211 to raise those caps to $20 [7]. The bill text doesn't print a start date; under the state constitution, a statute enacted at a regular session takes effect on January 1 after a 90-day period from enactment [14], which for this bill is Jan. 1, 2027. The published code section still shows $15 [6]. The Secretary of State's January 2026 handbook also still lists $15, and the Secretary of State notary and apostille pages we checked don't mention AB 1597 [13][15].
  • Extras and remote notarization (statewide): the handbook says any travel fee should be entered separately in the notary's journal; it sets no travel amount [13]. California notaries can't yet perform remote online notarizations: those parts of the Online Notarization Act start once the Secretary of State finishes its system, or by Jan. 1, 2030, whichever is earlier [13].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death and marriage records: California Department of Public Health (statewide)

  • State fees: since Jan. 1, 2026, CDPH Vital Records charges $31 for a certified copy of a birth record, $26 for a death record and $19 for a marriage record, under AB 64 (Chapter 662, Statutes of 2025) [2].
  • Paying by mail: make the check or money order payable to CDPH Vital Records, drawn on a U.S. bank; don't send cash. Requests without the right fee are returned. If no record is found, you get a Certificate of No Public Record and the fee is kept as a search fee [2].
  • Ordering from CDPH by mail: send the birth application (VS 111) or death application (VS 112) and a check or money order for $31.00 per birth copy or $26.00 per death copy (the order pages say to make it payable to "CDPH-VR") to California Department of Public Health, Vital Records - MS 5103, P.O. Box 997410, Sacramento, CA 95899-7410. The notarized sworn statement (VS 20), which is part of the application, is needed only for an authorized copy [18][19].
  • Online and in person: CDPH takes electronic orders only through a third-party vendor; for in-person copies it points you to the county office where the birth or death was registered [18][19].
  • Processing time: CDPH's average is 4 to 6 weeks for a certified copy, counted from when it receives the request. It says you can often get a certificate faster from the county office, and that county fees may differ from CDPH's [20].
  • County copies: county recorders also issue certified copies; the county office for this city is below.

Birth, death and marriage records: San Diego County Recorder/County Clerk

  • County fees: $34 for each birth certificate [21], $26 for each death certificate [22] and $19 for each marriage certificate [23]. The fee isn't refunded if no record is found; you get a "Letter of No Record" [21].
  • Paying: in person, cash, check or money order payable to San Diego Recorder/County Clerk, or a credit or debit card with a $2.50 surcharge. By mail, check or money order only [21].
  • In person: no appointment needed at the downtown San Diego, Chula Vista, Santee and San Marcos offices; not at Kearny Mesa. You sign the application in front of a County Clerk and show government-issued photo ID [21]. Offices are open Mon–Fri 8 a.m.–5 p.m. [1].
  • Timing: allow 3 weeks after a birth or death [21][22], and 10 business days after a marriage license is returned, before requesting a copy [23].
  • Online: VitalChek is the county's only authorized online vendor and adds a $12.95 handling fee, plus $19 for optional expedited delivery [21].
  • By mail: for a certified copy, the sworn statement on the application must be notarized. Mail to San Diego Recorder/County Clerk, ATTN: Vital Records, P.O. Box 121750, San Diego, CA 92112-1750 [21].
  • Confidential marriage records: only the spouses, or someone with a court order, can get a certified copy [23].

Marriage licenses: San Diego County Recorder/County Clerk

  • Fees: $129 for a public marriage license, $144 for a confidential license, $107 for a civil ceremony and $58 for a witness provided by the County Clerk. Cash, check, money order or card ($2.50 card surcharge); license fees aren't refunded [8].
  • How: by appointment only, booked up to 7 weeks ahead. Both people come together with valid government-issued photo ID, and both must be 18 or older. The license is valid for 90 days in California [8].

Recorded documents: San Diego County Recorder/County Clerk

  • Recording fees: the Jan. 1, 2026 fee schedule lists $14.00 for the first page of most documents, or $17.00 for titles that carry the real estate fraud fee (such as deeds of trust and reconveyances), plus $3.00 for each additional page and the $75.00 SB 2 Building Homes and Jobs Act fee unless exempt. Pages that aren't 8½ by 11 inches cost $3.00 more each [24].
  • Copies: $2 for the first page and $0.05 for each additional page, plus $1 per document to certify. You can order in person, online through the Official Records Index (mailed), or by mail [12].
  • Where and paying: record in person at the downtown San Diego, Chula Vista, Santee and San Marcos offices without an appointment. Fees can be paid by cash, card in person ($2.50 surcharge), check or money order [12].

Court records: California superior courts (statewide fees)

  • Fees (statewide): under Government Code § 70627, the court clerk charges $0.50 a page to prepare copies of a record on file, $1 a page to compare a copy you bring with the original (plus the certificate fee), and $15 for a records search that takes a court employee more than 10 minutes [25].

Court records: Superior Court of California, County of San Diego

  • Local fee schedule: the court's Sept. 1, 2026 fee schedule lists $40 to certify a copy, $0.50 a side for copies, $1 a page for comparison, $15 for a search over 10 minutes and $50 for an exemplification. Off-site file retrieval is $41 standard, $61 rush and $75 emergency [26].
  • Online case documents: $1 a page for the first five pages, then $0.40 a page, up to $50.00 [26]. The court says most civil, small claims and probate documents can be previewed (first page only) and downloaded for a fee [27].
  • Divorce records: the county recorder doesn't hold divorce decrees; they're with the Superior Court [23]. A certified copy of a dissolution record is $15 for the public [26].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: California apostilles come from the Secretary of State, not the county. The document must be signed by a California public official or be an original notarized or certified document; photocopies aren't accepted [3]. The office doesn't notarize, so get the notarization first [28].
  • Birth and death certificates: a certificate signed by a local health officer or registrar may first need certifying by the county clerk, or you can get a certified copy from the county recorder or CDPH [28].
  • Fee: $20.00 per apostille. Include a cover sheet naming the country where the document will be used. In person there is also a $6.00 special handling fee for each different public official's signature [3][15]. The fee list also shows $5.00 for affixing the Certificate and Seal of State [15].
  • In person, same day: Sacramento, 1500 11th Street, 3rd Floor (card, check, money order or cash) [3], Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. except state holidays, (916) 653-3595 [29]; or Los Angeles, 300 South Spring Street, Room 12513, (213) 897-5310 [3][30].
  • Los Angeles office details: in person only, same day, first come, first served, with no appointments, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. except state holidays. It handles apostilles and domestic partnership filings only, doesn't take mail requests, and charges the $6.00 special handling fee for each different public official's signature on an apostille request. Payment is Visa, Mastercard, check or money order; no cash. It is temporarily serving customers on the 1st floor in Suite 1215 because of an elevator outage [30].
  • By mail: Sacramento only, with a $20.00 check or money order per apostille payable to Secretary of State and a self-addressed return envelope. USPS mail goes to Notary Public Section, P.O. Box 942877, Sacramento, CA 94277-0001; FedEx, UPS or DHL to 1500 11th Street, 2nd Floor, Sacramento, CA 95814. Mail requests aren't expedited [3]. The office also runs pop-up apostille events around the state, including San Diego on Wednesday, Dec. 2, 2026 [3][31]. At pop-ups the fee is $20 per apostille plus $6 special handling for each different official's signature, paid by Visa, Mastercard, check or money order (no cash); bring the original document and a pop-up cover sheet, and expect waits of up to 2–3 hours [31]. See the apostille guide.
  • San Diego pop-up (local): on Wednesday, Dec. 2, 2026, 8 a.m.–4 p.m., the Secretary of State's Apostille Pop-Up Shop will be at the San Diego County Administration Center, 1600 Pacific Highway, 2nd Floor, Room 273, San Diego, CA 92101, with free parking [31] (the county's page calls the room Suite 273 [32]). Bring the original document and a completed pop-up cover sheet; payment is Visa, Mastercard, check or money order, with no cash, and waits can reach 2–3 hours [32][31]. The county says on-site notarization isn't guaranteed [32].
  • County authentication: the county charges $21 to authenticate a notary's or public health officer's signature [24].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [33]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Can I fax for free in San Diego?

Yes, at the San Diego Public Library. Library copiers send digital faxes to U.S. numbers for free, and scanning to USB or email is free too [4].

Where can I apply for a passport in San Diego?

At 10 locator-listed facilities, including the San Diego City Clerk at 202 C Street (appointments at sandiego.gov/passports), the county Clerk of the Board, the SDSU Passport Office and 7 post offices [5].

What does a notary cost in California?

At most $15 per signature under Government Code § 8211 as published now. AB 1597 raises the cap to $20, and under the state constitution's timing rule that takes effect Jan. 1, 2027 (statewide) [6][7][14].

How much is a marriage license in San Diego County?

$129 for a public license and $144 for a confidential license, by appointment only [8].

Where do I get an apostille in San Diego?

From the California Secretary of State, in Sacramento or Los Angeles or by mail to Sacramento, for $20 per apostille (statewide) [3]. The Secretary of State's pop-up shop comes to the County Administration Center on Dec. 2, 2026 [32].
