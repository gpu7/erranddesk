Green Bay, Wisconsin document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Green Bay, Wisconsin, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Short version: Brown County Library charges 20 cents a page for black-and-white printing and copies and 50 cents for color, scanning to email or USB is free, and outgoing fax is $1 for the first page [1]. The State Department's locator lists 3 passport facilities inside Green Bay city, including the Brown County Clerk, which also lists notary service [2][3]. The Brown County Clerk's marriage license is $125 when you apply 4 to 60 days ahead [4]. The Census lists Green Bay city in Brown County [5].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Brown County Library

  • Where: the library offers self-service printing, photocopying, scanning and faxing at all its locations. The Central Library is at 515 Pine Street, Green Bay [1]; the Census Geocoder places it inside Green Bay city [3].
  • Before you start: you need a valid library card or guest pass, and you must add money to your account before you print, copy or fax. Pay with cash, coins or a credit or debit card at a library print kiosk (at select locations) or at a service desk [1].
  • Printing and copies: black and white is 20 cents a page for letter or legal paper and 40 cents for 11x17. Color is 50 cents a page for letter or legal and $1 for 11x17 [1].
  • From your own device: you can print from a Windows or Mac laptop or an Android or Apple phone or tablet at the library, at the standard rates. You won't see the total cost before the job is released to the printer [1].
  • Fax: outgoing only. $1 for the first page and 75 cents for each page after that [1].
  • Scanning: free, to your email or a USB drive [1].

Brown County Clerk

  • Photocopies: the State Department's locator entry for the Brown County Clerk's Office says photocopy service is available as part of walk-in service [2]. We didn't find a copy price.
  • The UPS Store: it lists 2 locations with Green Bay addresses: 101 S Military Ave, (920) 498-1991; and 1794 Allouez Ave, Ste C, (920) 884-6681. Its city page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [6]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [7].
  • Green Bay address, other municipality: the Census Geocoder places the S Military Ave store inside Green Bay city and the Allouez Ave store in Bellevue village [6][3].

Notary in Green Bay

  • Brown County Clerk: the State Department's locator entry for the Brown County Clerk's Office, 305 E Walnut St, Room 120, says notary service is available as part of walk-in service [2]. The clerk's walk-in counter is open Monday–Thursday 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and Friday 7:30 to 11 a.m.; arrive at least 15 minutes before closing [8]. We didn't find a notary price on the county's pages.
  • Library: we didn't find notary service on Brown County Library's printing, copying, fax and scanning page [1].
  • The UPS Store: its Green Bay page lists notarizing [6].
  • Fees (statewide): Wisconsin's Department of Financial Institutions (DFI) says a notary "may charge no more than $5 for most in-person notarial acts." Ask for the price before the notary starts [9].
  • Statewide commission: DFI says a Wisconsin commission lets a notary act in all counties in the state [9].
  • No copies of vital records (statewide): DFI tells notaries they are "strictly prohibited" from making copies, certified or uncertified, of birth, death, divorce, marriage and other vital records. Get certified copies from a state or county vital records office instead [9].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death, marriage and divorce records (statewide rules)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS) says Wisconsin birth, death, marriage and divorce records from October 1907 to now are available from the Wisconsin Vital Records Office, and also through any Wisconsin county Register of Deeds office. Availability depends on the event type and date [13].
  • Fees (statewide): $20 for the first copy of each record and $3 for each additional copy of the same record [13]. State law sets the same $20 and $3 fees [14]. DHS says the long form birth certificate doesn't cost extra; write "Long Form" on the application [13].
  • Who can get a certified copy: state law limits who can get one; an uncertified copy is printed on plain paper and stamped as not usable for legal or identification purposes [13].
  • State office by mail: Wisconsin Vital Records Office, PO Box 309, Madison, WI 53701. It takes about 10 business days after the office gets your order, plus mail time. Pay by cash, check or money order in U.S. funds [13].
  • Online and phone: through VitalChek, online or at 877-885-2981; there is a fee for using VitalChek, and requests take about five business days [13].

Brown County Register of Deeds

  • Where and when: Northern Building, Room 260, 305 E. Walnut Street, Green Bay, (920) 448-4470. Vital records are issued Monday–Thursday 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and Friday 7:30 to 11 a.m. These hours started on August 12, 2024 (a past date). Mail goes to P.O. Box 23600, Green Bay, WI 54305-3600 [15]. The Census Geocoder places 305 E. Walnut Street inside Green Bay city [3].
  • Fees: we didn't find a fee on the Brown County Register of Deeds vital records page [15]. The statewide fee is $20 for the first copy and $3 for each additional copy [13]; confirm with the office.
  • Online: the county's marriage brochure points to Wisconsin Register of Deeds Online, wrdaonline.org, for buying certificates [16].

Marriage licenses

  • Marriage licenses (statewide law): in Wisconsin the county clerk issues marriage licenses. A license can't be issued within 3 days of the application unless the clerk, at its discretion, issues it sooner for an added fee of up to $25. Each applicant must show a certified copy of a birth record, or one of the other documents the law allows, and swear to the application before the clerk. State law sets the county clerk's fee for each license at $49.50; that is the statutory clerk fee, not necessarily the full price a county charges [17].
  • Brown County Clerk, in person: both applicants must come in person to the County Clerk's Office, Northern Building, Room 120, 305 E Walnut Street, (920) 448-4016 [4]. Licenses are issued on weekdays only, and you should arrive at least 15 minutes before closing [16].
  • Price: $125 if you apply 4 to 60 days before the wedding, and $150 if you apply less than 4 days before (that includes the waiver of the 3-day wait; no personal checks). All fees are non-refundable [4][16].
  • Card fees and checks (sources differ): the county's web page says debit cards add $2.50 and credit cards add a 4% to 6% fee, and it lists personal checks for the $125 license [4]. The county's brochure (revised May 14, 2025) says credit cards add 6% and lists a "local check" [16]. Confirm with the office.
  • Timing: after you apply there's a 3-day waiting period. Once it's ready, the license is valid for 60 days in Wisconsin. You can apply in any Wisconsin county whether or not you live in the state [4].
  • Bring: state-certified birth certificates (hospital keepsakes and birth registration notices aren't accepted), photo ID, Social Security cards if a number has been issued, proof of residency, and the wedding date, ceremony location and officiant's name, address and phone. If you were married before, bring the final divorce decree, death certificate or annulment [4].
  • Off-site applications: for medical or special circumstances, the brochure lists an extra $125 transportation fee for delivery within Brown County, such as to a hospital or nursing home, paid in cash or by card [16].
  • Ceremony: to schedule a marriage with a Circuit Court judge, call 920-448-4348. After the ceremony, the officiant has three business days to file the license with any Wisconsin county Register of Deeds [4].
  • Varies by county: the clerk's page notes that the license and waiver fees may vary from county to county [4].

Recorded documents and court copies

  • Recording fees (statewide law): Wisconsin law sets the register of deeds fee for recording a document at $30. Copies of recorded documents are $2 for the first page and $1 for each additional page, plus $1 to certify a copy [18].
  • Court copies (statewide fee table): the Wisconsin court system lists Clerk of Circuit Court copies at $1.25 a page, and certified copies at $5 to certify the document plus $1.25 a page [19].
  • Divorce judgments: copies come from the county where the divorce happened. In Brown County, contact the Clerk of Circuit Courts, Brown County Courthouse, 100 South Jefferson Street, Green Bay, 920-448-4155 [4]. We didn't check the court's copy fees or the Register of Deeds' real estate fees.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Two state offices: both the Wisconsin Secretary of State (SOS) and DFI can issue apostilles for Hague Convention countries. Authentications for countries outside the Hague Convention come only from the SOS [20].
  • DFI: standard service is $10 per document (3–7 business days) and expedited is $35 per document (1–2 business days), not counting mail time. DFI, 4822 Madison Yards Way, North Tower, Madison, WI 53705; (608) 266-8915 [21].
  • Secretary of State: standard service is $10 per document (an estimated 7–20 days from when the office gets the documents) and expedited is $35 per document (2–4 business days). Walk-in expedited service is generally available the same day at the office on the basement level of the State Capitol in Madison. It accepts card, cash, check or money order [20].
  • Notarized documents: the SOS accepts only wet-ink stamped or embossed notarizations by a Wisconsin notary, not electronic ones. It can't process federal documents or vital records from another state or country [20].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [22]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

How much is printing at Brown County Library?

20 cents a page for black and white and 50 cents for color on letter or legal paper [1].

Can I fax at the Green Bay library?

Yes, outgoing only: $1 for the first page and 75 cents for each page after [1].

Where can I get a passport photo in Green Bay?

The Brown County Clerk takes passport photos for $17 each [10], and the locator lists photos at all 3 facilities inside the city [2].

How much is a marriage license in Brown County?

$125 if you apply 4 to 60 days before the wedding, or $150 if less than 4 days [4].

How much can a Wisconsin notary charge?

No more than $5 for most in-person notarial acts (statewide) [9].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions or the Wisconsin Secretary of State, both in Madison: $10 per document standard or $35 expedited (statewide) [21][20].
