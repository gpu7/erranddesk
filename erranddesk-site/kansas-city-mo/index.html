Kansas City, Missouri document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Kansas City, Missouri, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those. Looking for the Kansas side? See Kansas City, Kansas.

Which offices serve Kansas City: the Census Bureau's 2020 place-by-county file lists Kansas City, Missouri in Jackson, Clay, Platte and Cass counties [1]. This page covers Jackson County offices (the Recorder of Deeds and the 16th Judicial Circuit); if you live in the Clay County part of the city, see Clay County offices below [2]; if you live in the Platte or Cass County part, check with that county for marriage licenses, recording and court copies. Birth and death certificates come from the city's Health Department Office of Vital Records [3]. The public library on this page is the Kansas City Public Library [4]. Many addresses with a Kansas City, MO mailing address are outside the city, so we checked facility addresses with the Census Geocoder [5].
Short version: Kansas City Public Library black-and-white prints are free for the first 50 pages a day, and national faxes are currently free [6][4]. The library notarizes free, subject to staffing [7]. The passport locator lists 8 facilities inside the city [8]. A birth certificate is $15 at the city's vital records office [3], and a Jackson County marriage license is $52 [9].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Kansas City Public Library

  • Prints: black and white is free for pages 1–50 and $0.10 a page for pages 51–150; color is $0.50 a page. The daily limit is 150 pages per patron. Pay by credit or debit card or cash. Log in to a public computer with your library card number and PIN, or send files from a phone or computer with a guest name through the remote printing service [6].
  • Copies, fax and scanning: all locations except Sugar Creek have equipment to copy, fax and scan. Copies cost the same as prints. Fax is "Currently FREE to national landline number. $1 per page international landline." Scanning is free to a USB drive, phone, email or cloud storage [4].
  • Computers: available at each location, first come, first served, for one hour at a time, until 15 minutes before closing. Wi-Fi is free [4].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 8 locations in Kansas City: 905 McGee St, (816) 249-1020; 1515 Wyandotte St, (913) 218-4731; 4741 Central St, (816) 561-7411; 107 W 63rd St, (816) 363-3456; 1221 W 103rd St, (816) 942-0388; 5231 NE Antioch Rd, (816) 454-8560; 6324 N Chatham Ave, (816) 587-4700; and 5563 NW Barry Rd, (816) 587-7100. Its city page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [10]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [11].

Notary in Kansas City

  • Library (free): Kansas City Public Library notary services are "free of charge, subject to staffing availability," and end one hour before closing. They're currently unavailable at the Trails West Branch. Call 816.701.3400 or your branch to make sure a notary is available. Bring government-issued photo ID and every page of your document, unsigned. The library notarizes absentee and mail-in ballots, deeds, powers of attorney, trusts, affidavits and depositions, but not copy certifications, vital records, remote online notarizations or Form I-9 [7].
  • The UPS Store: its Kansas City page lists notarizing [10].
  • Fee limits (statewide): Missouri law caps what a notary may charge: $5 per signature for an acknowledgment, a jurat or a signature witnessing, and $1 per page for a certified copy, with a $3 minimum. A notary may charge less or nothing, may add a travel fee only if you agree to it in advance, and may not charge to notarize an absentee ballot or absentee voter registration. A notary who charges must display or hand you a fee schedule [12][13].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Missouri DHSS (statewide)

  • Fees (statewide): the Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services (DHSS) lists $15 for a birth certificate and $14 for a death certificate, with $11 for each additional copy of the same death record ordered at the same time. Each fee covers a five-year search and one certified copy if the record is found; it isn't refunded if no record is found. A statement of marriage, divorce or single status from the state is $15 [17][18].
  • Local offices (statewide): local public health agencies charge the same birth and death fees as the state, but hold only births from 1920 on and deaths from 1980 on. Certified marriage and divorce records come from the office that issued them, which sets its own fee [18].
  • Who can order (statewide): Missouri vital records aren't open to the public; state law allows a certified copy only for a person with "a direct and tangible interest in the record" [17]. In person, bring ID; by mail, the application must be notarized and include a self-addressed stamped envelope. The state office is the Bureau of Vital Records, 930 Wildwood Dr., Jefferson City [19]. DHSS says it charges no extra administrative fee, but VitalChek, its online vendor, adds a processing fee [20].

Birth and death certificates: Kansas City Health Department

  • Where: the Office of Vital Records, 2400 Troost Ave., Suite 1000, Kansas City, MO 64108; open Monday–Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., closing at 1 p.m. every fourth Thursday. Email births@kcmo.org or call (816) 513-6309 [3].
  • Fees: a birth certificate is $15 in person or by mail. A death certificate is $14 for the first copy and $11 for each additional copy. Ordering online or by phone through VitalChek costs $26.26–$45.25 for a birth certificate and $25.25–$44.25 per death certificate. In person, pay with cash, debit or credit and bring ID [3].
  • Which records: the office has Missouri births from 1920 on and Missouri deaths from 1980 on; the state has births from 1910 [3].
  • By mail: the request must be notarized and include a self-addressed stamped envelope and a check or money order payable to "Vital Records" [3].

Marriage licenses: Jackson County Recorder of Deeds

  • Where: the Historic Truman Courthouse, 112 W. Lexington, Suite 30, Independence, MO 64050, Monday–Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.; 816-881-4483, records@jacksongov.org. You can start the application online, but both applicants must appear in person [9].
  • Fee and ID: $52, non-refundable, by cash or credit or debit card (Discover, Visa, MasterCard), with a 2.75% card fee. Both applicants must be at least 18 and bring a valid ID such as a driver's license, state ID, passport or military ID [9].
  • When it's valid: the ceremony must take place in Missouri within 30 days, and the officiant returns the license within 15 days [9].
  • Certified copies: $10, in person or by mail; usually returned the same day or the next morning [21][22].
  • Statewide rules: under RSMo 451.040, both applicants sign the application in front of the recorder of deeds or a deputy, or through an approved online process, and the license is "void after thirty days from the date of issuance." We didn't find a waiting period in the statute. Common-law marriages are void in Missouri [23]. RSMo 451.151 adds a $20 state fee to every license and $7 to every certified copy, which are included in county totals [24].

Recorded documents: Jackson County Recorder of Deeds

  • Recording: new fees apply to documents received on or after Aug. 28, 2026: deeds are $23 for the first page and $3 for each additional page. Cards carry a 2.75% fee [22].
  • Copies: $2 for the first page and $1 for each additional page, plus $1 per document to certify [22]. Recorded documents from 1962 on, and marriage licenses from 1826 on, are free to search online; older records are on microfilm at the recording offices [21]. The Kansas City courthouse is at 415 E. 12th St., 816-881-3000 [22].
  • Recording fees (statewide): RSMo 59.310 sets the recorder's own fee at $5 for the first page and $3 for each page after that, and caps copies of recorded documents at $2 for the first page and $1 for each page after that, plus $1 for a certificate and seal [25]. County fee tables are higher than that base fee, so use the county's posted total.

Court copies: 16th Judicial Circuit (Jackson County)

  • Fees: regular copies are $0.30 a page; certified copies $4.00 per document plus copies; authenticated copies $6.00 per document plus copies; microfilm or archived copies $5.00 for the first page and $0.50 for each page after that [26].
  • How: the Kansas City copy desk is at 415 E. 12th Street, Suite 305 (816-881-6465), and the Independence desk at 308 W. Kansas Ave., Suite 107. In person, pay by money order, cashier's check, card or cash; by mail, money order or cashier's check. No personal checks. Copies won't be faxed or emailed [26].
  • Statewide: we didn't find a statewide circuit court copy fee schedule; each Missouri circuit posts its own.

Clay County offices (for the Clay County part of the city)

  • Recorder of Deeds: Clay County Administration, 1 Courthouse Square, Liberty, MO 64068; mailing address PO Box 238, Liberty, MO 64069; 816-407-3550. Office hours are Monday–Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., with no recordings or marriage licenses accepted after 4 p.m. The page says there's no office location at the Annex [2].
  • Marriage license fee (sources differ): the marriage license page says "$53.00 license fee payable by cash or credit cards only," with a processor fee for cards [27], and the fee schedule also lists $53.00 [28]. The Recorder's main page says a statewide statutory fee adjustment took effect Aug. 28, 2026, "a flat $2.00 fee increase for the recording of documents, the recording of surveys and plats, and the issuance of marriage licenses" [2]. The marriage page doesn't say whether $53 includes that increase, so confirm with the office.
  • Marriage license rules: applications are taken Monday–Friday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Both applicants appear together with a government-issued photo ID; all documents must be in English; you give your Social Security number, but no card is needed. The page quotes the state rule that no license is issued to anyone under 18. The ceremony can only be performed in Missouri, and you give the officiant's name and phone number when you apply [27].
  • Marriage license copies: $10.00 certified or $2.00 non-certified, by mail (check, cash or money order) to Recorder of Deeds, P.O. Box 238, Liberty, MO 64069, ATTN: Marriage License Desk, with a self-addressed stamped envelope, or by phone with a card at 816-407-3550 [27][28].
  • Recording: standard documents are $26.00 for the first page and $3.00 for each additional page; non-standard documents add $25.00; plats are $71.00 (24" x 36") or $46.00 (18" x 24"). Cash, check or card, with a processor fee for cards [28].
  • Recorded-document copies: $2.00 for the first page, $1.00 for each additional page, $1.00 per document to certify and $5.00 per plat page [28].
  • Court copies, 7th Judicial Circuit (2021 order, may be outdated): the Circuit Clerk's fee page, under an order dated June 1, 2021, lists certified copies at $4.00 for the first page and $1.00 for each additional page; authenticated copies at $6.00 for the first page and $1.00 for each additional page; photocopies and copies printed from microfilm at $1.00 a page. Ten or more pages may be charged the actual cost of search and copying, and mailed copies add postage and packaging [29]. You can fax a copy request to 816-407-3899 with case numbers and return information [30]. Confirm current fees with the office.
  • Birth and death certificates, Clay County Public Health Center: 800 Haines Drive, Liberty, MO 64068, 816-595-4200. Walk-in Monday–Friday 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., except holidays, with same-day service; bring a photo ID. Birth certificates are $15 a copy for Missouri births after 1920; death certificates are $14 for the first copy and $11 for each additional copy, for Missouri deaths after 1980 [31].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: the Missouri Secretary of State issues certifications (for notaries), authentications (for recorders of deeds, circuit clerks, judges, county clerks and the State Registrar) and apostilles for countries in the Hague Convention. The country where you will use the document decides which certificate you get [32].
  • Which copy: a birth certificate must be a certified copy from the state Bureau of Vital Records; a marriage license, a certified copy from the recorder of deeds of the county of the marriage (or a state statement of marriage); a divorce decree, a certified copy from the circuit clerk [32].
  • Fee: $10 per certification or authentication. One document with several notarizations by the same notary is $10; several documents are $10 each; one document notarized by different notaries is $10 per notary. Checks and money orders go to the Secretary of State's Office; Visa, MasterCard, American Express and Discover payments add a convenience fee [32].
  • How: mail the original document with the cover letter to Commissions, Secretary of State's Office, 600 West Main, Room 322, Jefferson City, MO 65101. Documents come back by regular mail unless you include a prepaid envelope or air bill. Phone (573) 751-2783 or toll-free (866) 223-6535 [32][33]. We didn't find walk-in service or processing times on the page.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [34]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Is printing free at the Kansas City Public Library?

Black and white is free for the first 50 pages a day, then $0.10 a page up to 150; color is $0.50 a page [6].

Can I fax at the library?

Yes, at every location except Sugar Creek. Faxes to national landlines are currently free; international landlines are $1 a page [4].

Where can I get something notarized for free?

At the Kansas City Public Library, subject to staffing; call 816.701.3400 first [7].

Where can I apply for a passport in Kansas City?

The locator lists 8 facilities inside the city, including the library's Ruiz and Westport branches, which take applications by appointment [8][14].

Where do I get a Kansas City birth certificate?

At the Health Department's Office of Vital Records, 2400 Troost Ave., $15 in person or by mail [3].

How much is a marriage license in Jackson County?

$52, applied for in person by both applicants at the Truman Courthouse in Independence [9].

Where do I get an apostille?

By mail from the Missouri Secretary of State in Jefferson City: $10 per certification or authentication (statewide) [32].
