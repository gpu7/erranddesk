Passport photos: where to get one, what it costs, and the official rules (2026)

Every U.S. passport application needs one photo that meets the State Department's rules. Here's where to get one, what each place officially charges, and how to take it yourself. Each figure links to its source.

Not affiliated with the government. Errand Desk is an independent site. We're not part of the U.S. Department of State, USPS, or any business listed here. For official rules, always check travel.state.gov. This is general information, not legal advice. The State Department makes the final call on whether a photo is accepted.

Short version: Published passport photo prices run from $7.64 (Walmart Photo's listing, quantity not stated) to $17.99 (CVS, two prints) [6][5]. Post offices charge $15.00 for photos [7]. The UPS Store publishes no national price [3]. You may also take the photo yourself, and a friend or family member can take it [1]. The photo is separate from the $35 acceptance fee charged where you apply in person [9].

Walgreens vs CVS passport photos

From each company's own page (the same facts as the comparison table):

  • Price: Walgreens, $16.99 for two 2×2 printed photos [4]. CVS, $17.99 for two 2×2 prints [5].
  • Digital copy (for online renewal): Walgreens, free emailed digital copy with purchase [4]. CVS, $3.99 emailed digital copy (select locations), or $10.99 on a USB drive [5].
  • Appointment: neither requires one [4][5].
  • Where: Walgreens, in-store only, "at most of our Walgreens locations" [4]. CVS, "Select CVS Pharmacy locations," in store only [5].

Photo fees vs. passport fees

  • If you apply in person with Form DS-11, you pay the State Department's application fee plus a $35 facility acceptance fee to the place where you apply. This applies to adults and children [9].
  • If a form for your application has to be notarized, see where to get something notarized and what it costs.
  • The photo is a separate cost. "Acceptance facilities and photo vendors charge different fees for taking your photo" [1]. At a Post Office, for example, the $15.00 photo fee is listed separately from the $35.00 acceptance fee [7].
  • The State Department says it can't refund the application fee or the execution (acceptance) fee, even if no passport is issued [9].

Official photo requirements (State Department)

From the State Department's passport photo page [1]:

  • One color photo, taken in the last 6 months.
  • Size: 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm). Head size: 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25 to 35 mm) from the bottom of the chin to the top of the head.
  • Background: plain white or off-white, without shadows, texture, objects or lines.
  • Pose: face the camera directly with your head not tilted and your eyes open and visible. A neutral expression or a smile with your mouth closed is fine.
  • No glasses: remove eyeglasses, sunglasses and tinted glasses. If you can't remove them for medical reasons, include a signed doctor's note.
  • Hats and head coverings: remove them unless worn for religious purposes (include a signed statement that it's religious attire worn daily in public) or medical purposes (include a signed doctor's statement). Your full face must be visible, with no shadows. The covering should be one color, without patterns or small holes.
  • Clothing: no uniforms, clothing that looks like a uniform, or camouflage. No headphones or wireless devices. Jewelry and facial piercings are OK if they don't hide your face. Remove face coverings and medical masks.
  • No edits: submit the original photo, with no filters, software or app changes. The State Department says it checks photos for digital alteration.
  • Print quality: high resolution, matte or glossy photo-quality paper, with no visible printer dots. No photocopies or scans, and no damaged photos.
  • Babies: no other person in the photo, and the child must face the camera. It's OK if a baby's eyes aren't fully open.

Digital photo rules for online renewal

From the State Department's "Uploading a Digital Photo" page [10]:

  • File type JPG, JPEG, PNG, HEIC or HEIF, between 54 KB and 10 MB, in color and taken in the last 6 months.
  • Frame the photo so its bottom edge is at your shoulders, near where they meet your arms. You can crop in the application.
  • Don't photograph or scan a printed photo, and don't send the image by text message (it lowers quality).
  • The upload tool checks basic requirements. An employee reviews the photo later and may ask for a new one.

Common reasons photos get rejected

The State Department says "Unacceptable photos are the number one reason why we put passport applications on hold" [1]. Its unacceptable examples include [1]:

  • Shadows on the face or background, or a photo that's under- or overexposed
  • Wrong head size: too close to or too far from the camera, or head not centered
  • Blurry, grainy or wrong-size prints
  • Edited or filtered photos, a cropped-out background, or red eye
  • Head tilted or turned, mouth open, or eyes closed
  • Glasses (even resting on your head), sunglasses, hats, or hair or a scarf covering the face
  • A background that isn't plain white or off-white (for example blue, textured or lined)

Can I take my own passport photo? DIY rules

The State Department lists "a friend or family member" alongside acceptance facilities and photo companies as a place to get your photo [1]. If you do it yourself, it must meet every rule above. The official tips [1]:

  • Stand several feet from a white or off-white wall. Use a white blanket or sheet to cover a non-white wall.
  • Use even lighting. Avoid overhead or side lights that cast shadows.
  • Set the camera to its highest quality setting.
  • Print at exactly 2 × 2 inches on matte or glossy photo-quality paper, with no visible printer dots.
  • Don't edit or filter the photo.

Tips before you go

  1. Call ahead. Walgreens, CVS and The UPS Store all say the service is at most, select or participating locations, not every store [4][5][3].
  2. Renewing online? Ask for a digital copy. Walgreens includes one free, and CVS charges $3.99 at select locations [4][5].
  3. Wear everyday clothes (no uniform or camouflage), and take off your glasses before the photo [1].
  4. Applying in person at a Post Office? You can book photos with your application appointment [7].

FAQ

How much does a passport photo cost?

Published prices we found: $7.64 at Walmart Photo, $15.00 at Post Offices, $15.95 at FedEx Office, $16.99 at Walgreens and $17.99 at CVS [6][7][2][4][5]. The UPS Store has no national price. One example store charges $19.99 plus tax [3][8].

Can I take my own passport photo?

Yes. The State Department lists "a friend or family member" as an option. The photo must still meet all requirements, including 2 × 2 inch size and photo-quality paper [1].

Can I wear glasses in my passport photo?

No, unless you can't remove them for medical reasons. In that case, include a signed doctor's note [1].

Is the photo included in the passport fee?

No. Photo vendors and acceptance facilities charge separately for photos [1]. The $35 acceptance fee covers accepting your application, not the photo [9][7].

What photo do I need to renew online?

A digital color photo taken in the last 6 months, in JPG, JPEG, PNG, HEIC or HEIF format and between 54 KB and 10 MB [10].
