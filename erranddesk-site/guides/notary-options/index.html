Where to get something notarized, and what it costs (2026)

You have more options than you might think, and some are free. This page covers The UPS Store, banks and credit unions, public libraries, AAA, and FedEx Office's online notary. It lists only what each one officially publishes, with a source for every figure.

Not legal advice. This page is general information. A notary can't tell you what your document should say, and neither can we. For that, ask an attorney or whoever asked you for the document.

Short version: Your state sets the maximum fee a notary may charge, and it varies a lot: for example $2 in New York, $10 in Florida and $15 in California and Colorado [20][18][16][21]. Some banks, credit unions, libraries and AAA clubs notarize free for customers or members [6][10][11][15]. FedEx Office's online notary costs $25 for the first seal [13]. Call ahead: notary availability varies by location.

State maximum notary fees: they vary by state

Each state sets the most a notary may charge for a notarial act, and some count per signature while others count per act or per document. Here are some examples from official state sources:

Remote online notarization (RON): the basics

  • What it is: you and a commissioned notary meet over live audio-video instead of in the same room. Your identity is checked with ID and other verification steps [13][23].
  • Not every state authorizes it. The National Association of Secretaries of State says "47 states and the District of Columbia" have a law allowing remote e-notarization, and that requirements "vary among states" [23]. California's own handbook says California notaries can't currently perform RON. Its Online Notarization Act takes effect once the state finishes a technology project, or by January 1, 2030 at the latest [16].
  • The receiving agency decides what it accepts. For example, the U.S. State Department accepts an electronically notarized parental consent form (DS-3053) for a child's passport "if it is allowed under state law," and you must bring a printed copy [24][25].
  • Some documents don't fit. FedEx Office's online notary says certain documents "have unique restrictions." It tells you to check with the document's recipient, and it doesn't handle documents for use outside the US [13].

Want to notarize online? You can start a session directly with Notarize (Proof) (paid link), the service behind FedEx Office's online notary [13]. See its pricing page for current fees [14], and check with whoever receives your document that they accept online notarization first.

What to bring

  • A valid government-issued photo ID for every signer [1][12]. California notaries must identify the signer by "satisfactory evidence" [16].
  • Every signer in person, plus any required witnesses [6][12]. Not every location supplies witnesses [1].
  • The complete, unsigned document, all pages and not just the signature page. Bank of America and Columbus Metropolitan Library both say not to sign beforehand [6][12].
  • A membership card if you're using a members-only service such as AAA [15].

Common reasons a notary says no

  • The signer isn't physically there. Texas: "The person whose signature is notarized must personally appear before the notary" [17]. California likewise requires physical presence for in-person notarization [16].
  • No acceptable ID [7][12].
  • The document is incomplete or has blank spaces. California notaries "must refuse" a document that is clearly incomplete [16]. Bank of America lists "missing pages or blank spaces that affect the intent of the document" [7].
  • You need legal help, not a notary. A notary who isn't an attorney can't give legal advice or prepare your document. Texas requires notaries who advertise in other languages to state, "I AM NOT AN ATTORNEY LICENSED TO PRACTICE LAW IN TEXAS AND MAY NOT GIVE LEGAL ADVICE" [17], and The UPS Store's page says its notaries are not allowed to "give advice on legal matters" [1].
  • That location doesn't handle that document type. Examples: I-9s and wills at many UPS Stores, and deeds, wills and powers of attorney at Columbus libraries [1][12].
  • The signer and notary can't communicate directly, or the notary believes the signer doesn't understand what they're signing [7].

FAQ

How much does a notary cost?

It depends on your state's maximum and on where you go. State maximums in our examples range from $2 (New York) to $15 (California, Colorado) for an in-person act [20][16][21]. Some banks, credit unions, libraries and AAA clubs charge nothing [6][10][11][15].

Does The UPS Store have a set notary price?

Not nationally. Its stores are independently owned franchises, and prices may vary by location [5]. Individual store pages show prices such as $2, $6 and $10 per signature in different states [2][4][3].

Can I get something notarized at FedEx Office?

Not in person. FedEx Office offers an online notary with Notarize that you use on your own device. A store can help you scan and print before and after [13].

Is notarization free at Bank of America?

Bank of America says it "does not charge a fee for notary services." Its FAQ adds that notary service is available at some financial centers and provided to customers "at no charge for most types of transactions" [6].

Is online notarization accepted everywhere?

No. Authorization varies by state, and California notaries can't currently perform it [23][16]. Whoever receives your document decides what it accepts, so ask them first.
