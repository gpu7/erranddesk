Where to get a document notarized and copied in one trip (2026)

If you need a document notarized, then copied, scanned or mailed, the place you pick decides how many stops you make. This page compares The UPS Store, FedEx Office, banks and public libraries using only their official pages. For prices, see our notary, photocopy and shipping guides.

Short version: Of the places compared here, only The UPS Store's national pages say it will notarize, make copies and ship in the same visit, at participating locations [1][3]. FedEx Office doesn't notarize in its stores. Its notary is online only, but the store can help you before and after [6]. Bank and library notaries are often free, but you may need a second stop to copy or mail [9][10][12].

Not legal advice. This page is general information. A notary can't tell you what your document should say, and neither can we. For that, ask an attorney or whoever asked you for the document. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with any government agency or with the businesses listed here.

Can The UPS Store notarize, copy and ship in one visit?

Yes, at participating locations. The UPS Store's notary page says notary is "available at participating locations" [1]. Its copies page says "once your documents are notarized, the center will help you make any necessary copies and ship them where they need to go" [2]. Its domestic shipping page says, "After assisting you with notarizing, our in-store experts can make as many copies as you'd like and send them anywhere they need to go" [3].

One store page, as an example, says it can make copies of notarized documents, scan them to a PDF, fax them, or ship them by UPS or the U.S. Postal Service. It lists notary at "$6 for each signature" [4]. Prices are set by each store. Our notary guide and photocopy guide have more store examples.

Notary hours can be shorter than store hours. One store, as an example, has a notary weekdays from 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM (10:00 AM to 2:00 PM on Tuesday) and not on weekends. It needs no appointment, but you can book one online [5].

Does FedEx Office notarize documents?

Not in its stores. FedEx Office says "documents can only be notarized online on your own device with audio and visual capabilities; however, a FedEx Office location can help you before and after your online notary session." It suggests scanning paperwork at a store first, then printing the notarized document and shipping hard copies afterward [6].

The online notary costs $25 for a single document. Each additional notary seal is $15, and each witness is $15. You need a valid government-issued photo ID and a U.S. Social Security number. FedEx Office says it can't be used for documents meant for use outside the United States [6]. For copy prices, see the photocopy guide.

Can a bank or library notarize and copy at the same time?

Banks and libraries often notarize for free, but their notary pages don't say they'll copy or mail for you. Bank of America does not charge a fee for notary services, and you schedule a financial center appointment [9]. More banks are in our free notary guide.

Some libraries offer both a free notary and printing. Ann Arbor District Library notarizes free by appointment and charges $.10 a page for printing after the first 30 free pages [10][11]. Columbus Metropolitan Library notarizes free by appointment and charges $0.10 a page for black-and-white printing [12][13]. These pages list printing prices, not copier prices. To mail afterward, see the shipping guide.

Can I fax, print and copy in one place?

Yes, at many shipping and office stores and some libraries, but prices and services vary by location. Some examples from official pages:

  • FedEx Office: one Dallas store page, as an example, lists "self-service copying, faxing, and scanning." Pricing there starts at $2.19 to send a local fax, $2.49 to send a domestic fax and $1.59 to receive one, with a free cover sheet and a printed confirmation. Printing on its self-service copiers starts at $0.23 a page for black and white and $0.71 for color [14].
  • Staples: its in-store printing page says, "Copy, print, scan and fax all in one place. Just pay at the machine and go." You can print from email or a USB drive, and faxing comes "along with a printed confirmation" [15]. Its document printing page lists prices "from $0.34 per page in black & white" and "$0.82 per page in color" [16]. A national fax price is Not stated on these pages.
  • The UPS Store: its fax page covers "sending and receiving faxes," with staff on hand to help [17], and its copies page says the center can copy and ship documents after they're notarized [2]. A fax price is Not stated on the national page, since each store sets its own.
  • Public libraries: Denver Public Library, as an example, offers free printing and copying, limited to 100 pages on library-supplied paper, and says "our copiers now also allow you to fax for free!" You can send faxes but not receive them, and you won't get a confirmation receipt [18].

For more store-by-store prices, see the fax price guide and the printing price guide.

What to bring so you only make one trip

  1. A valid government-issued photo ID for each signer [1][12].
  2. Every signer, and any witnesses. Some documents need witnesses, and not all locations can provide them [1]. Columbus Metropolitan Library asks you to bring all signers and witnesses [12].
  3. All pages of the document, unsigned. Bank of America suggests you don't sign or date it before the visit, and to bring all pages, not just the signature page [9].
  4. The delivery address, if you'll ship it. See what to bring to ship.
  5. A file on a flash drive or in the cloud, if you'll print something there. FedEx Office mentions printing from email, a flash drive or the cloud [6].

Tips before you go

  • Call ahead about the notary. UPS Store notary is at participating locations, and many don't notarize I-9s or wills [1]. Check notary hours, which may differ from store hours [5].
  • Book library and bank notaries ahead. Ann Arbor, Columbus and Bank of America all use appointments [9][10][12].
  • A copy isn't a certified copy. If someone asks for a certified copy, ask them what they'll accept. Columbus Metropolitan Library, for example, won't certify "true copies" [12].
  • Need it for use in another country? FedEx Office's online notary isn't for that [6]. Ask whoever requested the document what they need.

FAQ

Can I get a document notarized and copied in the same place?

Yes. The UPS Store says that once your documents are notarized, the center will help you make any necessary copies and ship them, at participating locations [1][2].

Does FedEx Office have a notary in the store?

No. FedEx Office says documents can only be notarized online on your own device, starting at $25. A FedEx Office location can help you scan, print and ship before and after the session [6].

How much does The UPS Store charge to notarize?

There's no national price, because stores set their own. One store page, as an example, lists $6 for each signature [4].

Is a bank notary free?

At Bank of America, yes: it "does not charge a fee for notary services" [9]. Other banks set their own policies.

Can the library notarize and print my document?

Some can. Ann Arbor District Library and Columbus Metropolitan Library both offer free notary by appointment and charge per page for printing [10][11][12][13].
