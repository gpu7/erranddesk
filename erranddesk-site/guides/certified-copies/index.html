How to get a certified copy of a birth certificate or other record (2026)

A certified copy is a copy issued by the office that holds the original record, with that office's seal or stamp. Here is which office to ask for birth, death, marriage, property and court records, with example fees taken only from the issuing offices' own pages.

Short version: Birth and death certificates come from the state vital records office, and in some states also from a county office. Property records come from the county recorder, and court records from the clerk of the court that handled the case. Fees differ by state and county: in our examples, a certified birth certificate costs $9 to $34 before any vendor or shipping fees [4][5]. For online orders, use the government office's own site or the vendor it links to, such as VitalChek [3][4].

Not legal advice. This page is general information from official pages. Who may order a record, and what ID you need, is set by each state. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with any government agency or with VitalChek.

What is a certified copy?

It's a copy made and certified by the office that keeps the original. For a birth certificate, the U.S. Department of State says the document "must have the official seal or stamp of the issuing office," and that if you don't have the original, you should "get a replacement from the office which issued it" [10]. A photocopy you make yourself, or one a notary signs, isn't the same thing.

Some offices also issue an "informational" certified copy to people who aren't authorized to get the full version. In Los Angeles County, that copy is stamped "INFORMATIONAL, NOT A VALID DOCUMENT TO ESTABLISH IDENTITY" [5].

How do I get a certified birth or death certificate?

Order from the vital records office in the state where the birth or death happened, not the state where you live now. Many states let you order by mail, in person or online. Some examples from official pages:

  • Fees are often kept if no record is found. Texas says its fees are non-refundable "even if the record is not found" [2], and California keeps the fee as a search fee [1].

  • Need it fast? Texas charges $25 for expedited processing [2]. Cook County prints most records while you wait at its offices [8].

Can I order a certified copy online?

Often, yes, but start from the government office's own website. Some offices send online orders to one named vendor. Florida says VitalChek "is the only contracted vendor" for its Bureau of Vital Statistics and that it doesn't recommend any other online vendor [4]. Los Angeles County says VitalChek "is the only authorized third party vendor" it works with [5]. Vendor processing fees are added to the office's own fee [3][4][8].

Where do I get a certified marriage certificate?

Usually from the county clerk or recorder where the marriage license was issued. Los Angeles County charges $19 per certified copy of a public or confidential marriage record. Only the spouses, or someone with a court order, can get a confidential one [6]. Some states also issue marriage records: California's state fee is $19 [1].

How do I get a certified copy of a deed?

Ask the recorder in the county where the property is. Los Angeles County charges $6 for the first page and $3 for each additional page for a certified copy of a real estate record, plus a search fee of $0.50 per name per year ($1 minimum) [7]. Other counties set their own fees.

How do I get a certified copy of a court record?

Ask the clerk of the court that handled the case. In federal district courts, it's $12 to certify a document, $24 for an exemplification, $0.50 per page for a paper copy and $34 per name or item for a records search, effective Dec 1, 2023 [9]. State and local courts set their own fees, so check the court's website.

Using a certified copy for a passport or an apostille

  • Passport: a U.S. birth certificate used as proof of citizenship must show, among other things, the issuing office's seal or stamp and the registrar's signature, and "You cannot submit electronic copies of birth certificates" [10]. See our passport application checklist.
  • Apostille: for a birth, marriage or death certificate used abroad, the state that issued it handles the next step. See how to get an apostille.

FAQ

How much does a certified copy of a birth certificate cost?

It depends on the state or county. In our examples: Florida $9, Cook County $15, Texas $22, New York State $30 by mail, California $31 and Los Angeles County $34 [1][2][3][4][5][8]. There's no national price.

Can a notary make a certified copy of a birth certificate?

For passports, the State Department says the certificate must carry the seal or stamp of the issuing office, and to get a replacement from the office that issued it [10]. That means the vital records office, not a notary.

Is VitalChek official?

VitalChek is a private company. Some government offices name it as their online vendor: for example Florida, Los Angeles County and Cook County, and New York State's online order link goes to it [3][4][5][8]. Check your office's own page to see which vendor, if any, it uses.

Do I get my fee back if no record is found?

Often not. Texas says its fees are non-refundable even if the record isn't found, and California keeps the fee as a search fee [1][2].

Where do I get a certified copy of a court judgment?

From the clerk of the court that issued it. Federal district courts charge $12 to certify a document [9].
