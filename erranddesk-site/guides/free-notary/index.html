Where to get something notarized for free (2026)

Many banks, some credit unions, some public libraries and some AAA clubs notarize documents at no charge, usually for their own customers or members. This page goes deep on those free options, using only each organization's official pages. For paid options such as The UPS Store and online notary, and for state fee limits, see our main guide: where to get something notarized, and what it costs.

Not legal advice. This page is general information. A notary can't tell you what your document should say. For that, ask an attorney or whoever asked you for the document.
Short version: if you have a bank account, start with your own bank. Bank of America and TD Bank say notary service is free for their customers [1][8]. PNC and Regions say the service is for their customers but don't publish a fee [7][9]. If you don't bank anywhere, some public libraries notarize for free, and Ann Arbor's is open to the public [11]. Almost everyone asks you to book or call first.

Is a bank notary free if I'm not a customer?

Usually you can't count on it. Bank of America, TD Bank, PNC and Regions all describe the service in terms of their own customers [1][7][8][9]. TD Bank is the one bank in our table that says it serves non-customers, in Massachusetts, New York, North Carolina and South Carolina, "as required by law" [8]. The other banks don't say. If you don't have a bank account, a public library may be a better bet (see below).

Do credit unions notarize for free?

Many credit unions offer notary service to members, with their own rules. One example is Navy Federal Credit Union [10]:

  • Navy Federal documents: no charge.
  • Other documents: first 2 per week at no charge, then $5.00 per document, "not to exceed the local jurisdiction fee maximum."
  • Where: not offered in Louisiana. In California, Navy Federal documents only.
  • Won't notarize: wills, advance directives, I-9 forms or vital records.

This is one example. Other credit unions set their own policies, so check yours.

Can I get something notarized for free at the library?

Some public libraries offer free notary service. Policies vary widely. Two examples:

  • Ann Arbor District Library (MI): free to the public by appointment only, and no library card is needed. Appointments are at the Downtown Library, Monday through Friday, 10am to 5:30pm, with no weekend or same-day bookings [11].
  • Columbus Metropolitan Library (OH): free basic notary service by appointment at all locations [12]. It won't notarize real estate documents such as mortgage closing documents, deeds, wills, living wills, trusts, powers of attorney, "true copy" certifications or Form I-9 [12].

These are examples. Search your own library system's website for "notary," or call your branch.

Does AAA notarize for free?

Some AAA clubs offer free notary service to members. AAA is organized into regional clubs, so check yours. One example is AAA Hoosier: "Free notary service is available to members at our AAA Hoosier service center locations for most documents." Bring a valid government-issued photo ID and your AAA membership card, and "Please make an appointment" [13].

Tips before you go

  1. Call or book first. Bank of America, PNC, Regions, the two libraries and AAA Hoosier all point you to an appointment or a call [1][7][9][11][12][13].
  2. Don't sign early. Bank of America and Wells Fargo say to wait to sign until you're with the notary [1][5].
  3. Bring everyone and every page. All signers must be present with ID, and banks ask for the complete document, not just the signature page [1][5][6][7][8].
  4. Ask about your document type. Navy Federal and Columbus Metropolitan Library list documents they won't notarize, such as wills [10][12]. See also common reasons a notary says no.
  5. Need a copy or a scan afterward? See where to scan documents and how much it costs to print.

FAQ

Where can I get something notarized for free?

Start with your own bank. Bank of America and TD Bank say it's free for customers [1][8]. Some credit unions, public libraries and AAA clubs also offer it free, such as Navy Federal for its own documents, Ann Arbor District Library and AAA Hoosier [10][11][13].

Does Bank of America notarize for free?

For customers, yes. Bank of America says notary service is "at no cost to our customers" in many financial centers, and "at no charge for most types of transactions." Schedule an appointment first [1][2].

Does Chase notarize for free?

Chase lists notary services among services generally offered at its branches [4], but its official pages we checked don't state a fee or who can use it. Call your branch.

Does Wells Fargo notarize for free?

Wells Fargo's official appointment FAQ covers notary visits but doesn't state a fee or who can use it [5]. Call your branch.

Can I get something notarized at a bank if I'm not a customer?

TD Bank says it serves non-customers in Massachusetts, New York, North Carolina and South Carolina, "as required by law" [8]. PNC says everyone named must be PNC clients [7]. Other banks we checked don't say, so call first.

Can I get something notarized at the library?

At some libraries. Ann Arbor District Library notarizes free for the public by appointment, and Columbus Metropolitan Library offers free basic notary service by appointment [11][12].
