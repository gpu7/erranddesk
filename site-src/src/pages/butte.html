Butte-Silver Bow, Montana document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
One government for city and county: Butte and Silver Bow County share a single consolidated government, the City and County of Butte-Silver Bow, with its courthouse at 155 W Granite Street [1]. The Census Bureau's 2025 Gazetteer lists it as "Butte-Silver Bow (balance)," a place covering about 716 square miles, and our map dot uses the Census internal point for that whole area, which sits southwest of uptown Butte [2]. County offices such as the Clerk & Recorder and the Clerk of the District Court serve all of it [3][1].
Short version: the Clerk of the District Court, in the courthouse, accepts passport applications from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. with a $35 execution fee paid by cash or check, and the locator says no appointment is needed [1][4]. It issues marriage licenses for $53, cash or money order only [1]. Birth and death certificates come from the Clerk & Recorder [3]; state law sets the county fee at $8 for a birth certificate and $7 for a death certificate [5], versus $16 from the state office [6]. Montana caps notary fees at $10 per notarial act (statewide) [7]. We didn't find library print, fax or notary prices.

Notary in Butte

  • Local options: we didn't find a free or low-cost public notary service on the Butte-Silver Bow pages we checked. See notary options.
  • Fees (statewide): Montana law lets a notary "charge a fee not to exceed $10 for each notarial act," such as an acknowledgment, a signature witnessing or a jurat. A notary may add a travel fee (no more than the IRS standard mileage rate) or a fee for an electronic or remote notarization only if the notary explains it's extra and not set by law and you agree to the amount in advance. A notary who charges fees must display a list of them in English [7]. That's the same $10 cap as Wyoming and Arizona and double North Dakota's $5.

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Butte-Silver Bow Clerk & Recorder

  • Birth records: certified copies for births in 1920 and later can be obtained at any Montana county Clerk & Recorder by mail or in person, for anyone born in Montana; records before 1920 come from the county of birth. Proof of identity is required, and state law limits who can get one [3].
  • Death records: any Montana county can print a certified death record from 2003 on. For Butte-Silver Bow deaths before 2003, the office works with the Butte Archives; call for instructions [3].
  • Fees: the county page says a certified death certificate has cost $7 since July 1, 2021 [3]. We didn't find the birth price on the page; state law sets it at $8 [5].
  • Phone: (406) 497-6335 [3].

Birth and death certificates (statewide)

  • Any county office (statewide): you can buy a Montana birth or death certificate in person at any county Clerk & Recorder's office in Montana, no matter which county the event happened in. Bring a current photo ID; you fill out the application there. A long-form birth certificate (often needed for dual citizenship or tribal enrollment) comes only from the state office in Helena or the Clerk & Recorder in the county of birth [6].
  • County price (statewide law): state law sets the county clerk's fee at $8 for each certified copy of a birth certificate and $7 for each certified copy of a death certificate [5].
  • State price: the Office of Vital Records charges $16 for every certificate, birth or death, certified or informational, abstract or long form [6][10]. Its fee schedule took effect Sept. 21, 2024 [10].
  • State office (Helena): order by mail to Montana Vital Records, PO Box 4210, Helena, MT 59604, with a check or money order and a copy of your ID or a notarized application; online or by phone through VitalChek (1-888-877-1946) or VRO, which add card service fees. The office is closed to walk-ins. Questions: (406) 444-2685, option 2, or HHSVitalRecords@mt.gov [6].
  • Late-October outage: the state notice says no Montana office issued birth certificates Oct. 21–25, 2026, during a move to a new records platform, and statewide issuance resumed Monday, Oct. 26 [10].

Marriage licenses: Clerk of the District Court

  • Where and when: Courthouse, Room 313, 155 W. Granite St., (406) 497-6350. Licenses are issued 8 a.m.–4 p.m. [1].
  • Fee: $53.00 by cash or money order. No personal checks and no credit or debit cards [1].
  • What to bring: proof of age for each applicant; a driver's license or birth certificate is enough. If either of you was married before, know the county, state and date of the divorce or death [1].
  • Validity: valid immediately and for 180 days [1].
  • License fee (statewide law): $53 when one or both of you are present at the ceremony, or $83 when neither is (a double proxy) [11]. Marriage licenses come from the Clerk of District Court in Montana, not the Clerk & Recorder.
  • Proof of age (statewide law): each applicant must provide a birth certificate or other satisfactory evidence of age [12].

Recorded documents: Butte-Silver Bow Clerk & Recorder

  • Copies (statewide law): county clerk copy fees are 50 cents for the first page of a document and 25 cents for each later page, plus $2 for each certification with seal [5].
  • Recording fees: we didn't find Butte-Silver Bow's recording fee schedule in our checks.

Court records: Clerk of the District Court

  • Fees (statewide law): the clerk of district court charges $1 a page for the first 10 pages of copies from a file, for each request, and 50 cents for each additional page; 25 cents a page for copies sent by fax, email or other electronic means; $2 for each certificate with seal; $5 for a copy of a marriage license and $10 for a copy of a dissolution decree. A records search is $2 per name for each year, for up to 7 years, and $1 per name for each further year [11].

Apostilles (statewide)

Montana apostilles come from the Secretary of State in Helena, not the county. For a Montana birth or death certificate, note on your Vital Records application that it's for an apostille (you'll get a long form), and contact the Secretary of State's office to tell it the document is coming. Pay the Secretary of State $10.00 per apostille, separately from the Vital Records fee [13]. Vital Records will mail the certificate to the Secretary of State once it's issued; the Secretary of State's office is at (406) 444-1877 [6]. We couldn't open the Secretary of State's own apostille page from our checker (its firewall blocked us), so we haven't confirmed its current instructions there: sosmt.gov/apostilles-authentications/apostilles. See the apostille guide.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [14]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Do I need an appointment for a passport in Butte?

Not at the Clerk of Court: the locator lists "WALK-INS - NO APPOINTMENT NECESSARY," Mon–Fri 9 a.m.–4 p.m. [4]. Bring your own photo; the office doesn't take them [1].

Where can I apply for a passport in Butte?

The Clerk of Court in the courthouse at 155 West Granite St. and the post office at 701 Dewey Blvd. [4].

What does a notary cost in Montana?

No more than $10 per notarial act, plus travel or remote-notarization fees only if you agree to them in advance (statewide) [7].

How do I pay for a marriage license in Butte?

$53.00 by cash or money order; personal checks and cards aren't accepted [1].

Is Butte a city or a county?

Both: Butte and Silver Bow County have one consolidated government, the City and County of Butte-Silver Bow [1].
