Austin, Texas document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Austin, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
Which offices serve Austin: the city's public library is the Austin Public Library, which also runs passport and notary offices [1]. Austin Public Health's Office of Vital Records registers births and deaths inside Austin's full purpose city limits [2]. The Travis County Clerk issues marriage licenses, records deeds and has older (1903–1956) county birth and death records [3][4][5]. The Travis County District Clerk sells copies of court records and runs three passport offices [6][7]. Apostilles come from the Texas Secretary of State in Austin [8]. The City of Austin extends into Travis, Hays and Williamson counties [9]. This guide covers Travis County offices; if you live in another county's part of the city, check that county's clerk for marriage licenses and recording.
Short version: Austin Public Library prints and copies are $0.20 a page, black and white only, and scanning to a USB drive is free; the library has no fax machines [10]. The library notarizes by appointment for $10 for one or two signatures per document [1]. The passport locator lists 16 Austin facilities [11]. A Travis County marriage license is $80 [3], and a City of Austin birth certificate is $23 [12]. Texas notary fees are capped per signature (statewide) [13], and apostilles are $15 at the Secretary of State [14].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Austin Public Library

  • Printing and copying: every location except the Austin History Center has black-and-white print/copy machines; prints and copies are $0.20 a page, and there's no color. The machines take bills and coins and give change. You can pay for printing by credit card, but copying needs cash unless you go to the Check Out desk first [10].
  • From library computers: print jobs are $0.20 a page, black and white only, released from any Self Service Station and saved for 48 hours. Cash over $5 is paid at the Circulation Desk [15].
  • Scanning: free, in color or black and white, to a USB drive at all library locations; the scanner bed takes up to tabloid (11" x 17") or A3 [10].
  • Fax: the library doesn't have fax machines [10].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 31 locations in Austin; see its city page for addresses and phone numbers. That page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [16]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [17].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Austin

  • Austin Public Library: $10 for one or two signatures per document, by appointment only. It does acknowledgments and oaths or affirmations for papers up to 15 pages. It won't notarize wills or other legal papers, money or financial papers, copies of IDs or vital records, passport forms, real estate papers or papers in another language, and it accepts only U.S. IDs [18][1]. Book through the library's Qless appointment link on the notary page, the same system used for passports; the passports page says appointments show for the next three days only [18][1]. The notary page doesn't list locations [18]. The passport locator notes notary service on site at the Yarborough and Spicewood Springs branches [11].
  • The UPS Store: its Austin page lists notarizing [16]. We didn't find store notary prices; the Texas maximums are below.
  • Fee limits (statewide): Government Code § 406.024 lets a Texas notary charge up to $10 for taking an acknowledgment or proof of a deed or other instrument for the first signature and $1 for each additional signature, $10 for administering an oath with a certificate and seal, $10 for a certificate under seal not otherwise provided for, $1 a page for a copy of a record, and $10 for a notarial act not otherwise provided for. The section says the Secretary of State adjusts these amounts for inflation every five years; we didn't find published adjusted amounts, and the Secretary of State's notary pages returned "not found" when we checked on Oct 30, 2026 [13]. The Secretary of State says a notary may charge less or nothing, and an employer may decide what an employee notary charges [19].
  • Online notarization (statewide): under § 406.111, an online notary may charge up to $25 for an online notarization on top of any other fee allowed [13].
  • What notaries can't do (statewide): a Texas notary can't certify a copy of a birth certificate or a marriage license, and a notary who isn't a lawyer can't advertise as a "notario publico" [19][13].

A $35 acceptance fee is paid separately to the acceptance facility, and expedited service is $60 more [23].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Texas Vital Statistics (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): Texas Vital Statistics (DSHS) charges $22 for a birth certificate (long or short form), $60 for an heirloom birth certificate, $20 for the first death certificate and $3 for each additional copy, and $20 to verify a marriage or divorce. Expedited processing is $25 and is shipped overnight. Paying by card in person in Austin adds $2.25 [24].
  • By mail (statewide): send a notarized application, a copy of your ID and a check or money order [25] to Texas Vital Statistics, Department of State Health Services, P.O. Box 12040, Austin, TX 78711-2040. Its phone number is 888-963-7111, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m. [26].
  • Local offices (statewide): local registrars and county clerks also issue certificates and set their own fees and hours [27]. The local offices for this city are below.

Birth and death certificates: City of Austin Office of Vital Records

  • Where: 7201 Levander Loop, Building C, on the Betty Dunkerley Campus next to the Animal Center. Open Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m.; walk-in applications are taken until 4:15 p.m., and most certificates are ready within 30 minutes [12].
  • Which records: long form certificates for births and deaths inside Austin's full purpose city limits, and abstract certificates for births anywhere in Texas [12].
  • Fees: birth certificates are $23 each; death certificates are $21 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy ordered at the same time. Cash, exact money order, personal check or card in your name. VitalChek online and phone orders add processing and shipping fees [12].

Older records: Travis County Clerk

  • The County Clerk isn't the local registrar for all birth and death certificates. It has Travis County births and deaths from 1903–1956, by mail: $22 for a certified birth certificate and $20 for a death certificate. For births and deaths outside Austin but in Travis County, it points you to the Justice of the Peace court for the area [5].

Marriage licenses: Travis County Clerk

  • Fee: $80, or $20 with a completed premarital education course (Texas Twogether) certificate shown when the license is issued. The informal (common-law) declaration is $45 [3]. Since April 1, 2026, a $100 fee is added if neither applicant can prove Texas residency [28].
  • How: both applicants appear together at the Civil Family Courthouse, 1700 Guadalupe St., 4th Floor, Suite 4.300. There's a 3-day waiting period, waived with a premarital course certificate, among other exceptions, and the license expires if it isn't used before the 90th day. Phone 512-854-9188 [3].
  • Copies: a certified copy of a marriage license is $6, and a plain copy is $1 [4].
  • Waiting period (statewide): Texas has a 72-hour wait between issuing a license and the ceremony. It doesn't apply to active-duty military or Department of Defense employees, if a judge waives it, or if you completed a premarital education course within the past year. A license expires if the ceremony isn't held within 90 days [29].
  • Twogether in Texas (statewide): the state's official Twogether in Texas site, run by Texas Health and Human Services, says completing its 8-hour premarital course waives the $60 state portion of the marriage license fee and the 72-hour waiting period when you show the original certificate of completion to the county clerk. Counties may add up to $22 in local fees, which aren't waived. The certificate is good for one year [30][31]. What the course costs depends on the provider: some are free and others charge for materials. Find a registered provider, including online and no-cost options, on the site's Search Providers page [31][32].

Recorded documents: Travis County Clerk

  • Recording fees: $25 for the first page and $4 for each additional page [4].
  • Copies: plain copies are $1 a page, and certified copies are $5 plus the cost of the copies. Self-serve photocopies are $0.20 a page [4].

Court records: Travis County District Clerk

  • Online: public court records can be viewed online at no cost; official, certified physical copies are requested from the office [6].
  • Fees: paper copies are $1 a page, certified or not; the certificate and seal are $5; electronic copies of electronic documents are $1 for up to 10 pages and $0.10 a page after that; a records search is $5 [33].

Clerk copy fees (statewide)

  • District clerks (statewide): Government Code § 51.318 sets district clerk fees. A certified copy is $5 for the certificate and seal plus, for each page, $1 printed on paper, $1 for a paper document converted to electronic format, or, for an electronic copy of an electronic document, $1 for up to 10 pages and $0.10 for each page over 10. A noncertified copy is $1 a page on paper or converted to electronic format, or $1 for an electronic document up to 10 pages and $0.10 for each page over 10. A records search is $5 [34]. Section 51.606 says no fee may be charged just to look at records [34].
  • County clerks (statewide): county clerk copy fees are set in Local Government Code Chapter 118, not Chapter 51. We didn't read Chapter 118, so this guide uses each county clerk's own fee pages, above.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: the Texas Secretary of State is the only office that issues apostilles for Texas public documents. It doesn't apostille federal documents, and it doesn't apostille documents meant for use inside the United States [35][8].
  • Fee: $15 per apostille, or $10 per document for adoption-related documents, with a $100 maximum [14].
  • In person in Austin: Authentications Unit, 400 West 15th Street, Austin, (512) 463-5705, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Same-day service is limited to 10 documents. Tuesday–Thursday are by appointment; Monday and Friday are walk-in, with waits that can be over an hour. More than 10 documents go in the drop box, with about a 72-hour turnaround. Pay by check, money order, card or exact cash [8].
  • By mail: about 2 weeks. Send Form 2102 naming the country, with a check or money order payable to the Office of the Texas Secretary of State. Certified vital records must have been issued within the past 5 years, and photocopies aren't apostilled [8]. See the apostille guide.

More on certified copies: how to get a certified copy.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [36]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • City list: Austin Resource Recovery lists local places that shred documents for a fee or take already-shredded paper, and says to call the business to confirm. Its Recycle and Reuse Center, 2514 Business Center Drive (512-974-4343, appointments required), takes shredded paper for recycling in paper bags but doesn't shred on site [37].
  • Events: Travis County describes an Annual Free Shred Day at the Travis County Central Booking parking lot, free for residents, with up to five bankers boxes of paper [38]. We didn't find a 2026 date, and we didn't find a City of Austin shred event.
  • The UPS Store: its Austin page lists shredding [16]. We didn't find prices.

For paid per-pound shredding, see where to shred documents.

Questions

How much does printing cost at the Austin Public Library?

$0.20 a page for black-and-white prints and copies; there's no color, and scanning to a USB drive is free [10].

Can I fax at the Austin Public Library?

No. The library doesn't have fax machines [10]. The UPS Store's Austin page lists faxing [16].

Does the Austin Public Library notarize?

Yes, by appointment, for $10 for one or two signatures per document, with limits on what it will notarize [18].

Where can I apply for a passport in Austin?

The locator lists 16 Austin facilities, including post offices, library branches and Travis County District Clerk offices [11]. Library offices are appointment-only [1]; the District Clerk takes walk-ins at 5325 Airport Blvd. [7].

How much is a marriage license in Travis County?

$80, or $20 with a Twogether in Texas premarital course certificate [3].

Where do I get an Austin birth certificate?

From Austin Public Health's Office of Vital Records at 7201 Levander Loop, Building C; it's $23 [12].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Texas Secretary of State in Austin, in person or by mail, for $15 per apostille (statewide) [8][14].
