What to expect at a passport acceptance visit (2026)

This page is the in-person acceptance appointment: the unsigned application, the two payments, and how a post office or a library acceptance facility handles booking and photos. Which form you need, the document list, State Department fees and processing times are in the passport checklist. Photo rules and store prices are in the passport photo guide.

Short version: At a post office, USPS says to print Form DS-11 and not sign it. A postal employee witnesses the signature. You pay the post office its acceptance fee, and a separate payment to the U.S. Department of State goes with the application [1]. Book the visit. Some post offices also have limited walk-in hours [1]. A library acceptance facility can use different booking rules and a different photo price. Houston and Austin publish their own [2][3].

Not legal advice, and not a government site. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the U.S. Department of State, USPS, or the libraries below. Facility rules differ. Confirm the location before you go.

Is the acceptance visit the right trip?

USPS says thousands of post offices accept first-time passport applications for the State Department. Most of those locations can also take a passport photo [1]. USPS also says people who are eligible to renew must renew by mail or online, and cannot renew in person. It points eligibility questions to the State Department [1]. Use the checklist for that eligibility list. This page does not restate it.

That "cannot renew in person" line is USPS's. It is not every acceptance facility. Austin Public Library says it can accept a renewal as a new DS-11 application and that it cannot take the mail-in Form DS-82 [3]. Houston Public Library publishes its own list of who may book there (a first passport, an applicant under 16, or a previous passport that was issued under 16, lost, stolen, damaged, or issued more than 15 years ago) and points everyone else toward renewing by mail with DS-82 [2]. Read the facility's page, not a single national sentence.

What should be unsigned when I arrive?

For a post office visit, USPS says to complete Form DS-11 on the State Department website, print it, and not sign it. "A Postal employee must witness your signature." USPS also says to photocopy your proof of identity and citizenship documents [1]. Per-page copy prices are in the photocopy guide.

Austin Public Library's acceptance offices say the same about the signature, in their own words: bring Form DS-11, "Do not sign it yet," fill it out completely in black ink or type it, and print it on one side only [3]. Houston Public Library says to complete the DS-11 in black ink before you arrive. Its passport page does not say when you sign [2]. Follow that facility's instruction rather than assuming the post office rule applies there.

A mail renewal is a different form. USPS says an eligible renewal uses Form DS-82, which you print and sign, and mail to the State Department instead of taking it to the acceptance counter [1]. Addresses and the rest of the mail-renewal rules are on the checklist.

Why are there two payments?

USPS splits a first-time application into an acceptance fee paid at the post office and a State Department processing fee sent with the application. The two are paid separately [1].

  • Post office: $35.00 acceptance fee, plus $15.00 for a photo if you need one. Pay by check or money order payable to "Postmaster," or by debit or credit card [1].
  • State Department, at a post office: USPS says these fees vary and are not paid by credit or debit card. You may use a personal, certified, cashier's, or traveler's check, or a money order, payable to "U.S. Department of State." The payment is sent with the application [1]. Current State amounts are on the checklist. For where to buy the money order, see the money order guide.

Other acceptance facilities set their own acceptance-fee instructions. They are not the post office list above.

  • Houston Public Library: a non-refundable $35 acceptance fee plus $10 for a photo if needed, by check, money order, or credit card, payable to "City of Houston." State Department fees are a separate check or money order payable to "U.S. Department of State." The library tells you to use the State Department fee calculator for those amounts [2].
  • Austin Public Library: $35 acceptance fee and an $18 photo fee to the City of Austin, by credit or debit card, check, or money order. No cash. The State Department payment is check or money order only. The library says it does not take electronic payments for that part [3].

Do I need an appointment, and can they take the photo?

USPS says to make a first-time passport or photo appointment with its online scheduler, a lobby self-service kiosk, or a retail counter. It also says some locations have limited passport walk-in hours [1]. Many post offices can take a hard-copy photo, and many can take a digital photo. The $15 photo fee applies "if needed" [1]. You can also bring a photo that already meets the rules. Those rules, and prices at other stores, are in the photo guide.

Library acceptance facilities publish their own booking and photo rules:

  • Houston Public Library lists six acceptance facilities: Alief-David M. Henington, Central, Collier, McGovern-Stella Link, Park Place, and Walker. Schedule up to 2 weeks ahead and arrive at least 15 minutes early. The library says new appointments open each day, and to try again if no date is open. Questions: 832-393-7277. It takes photos at those six facilities [2]. More Houston passport sites, including the county, are on the Houston page.
  • Austin Public Library takes appointments only, for the next three days, and not by phone or email. No walk-ins. If the page says "Currently unavailable," the open slots are taken. Late arrivals are rescheduled. Open offices are the Central Library (710 W. César Chávez St., 2nd floor, room 207), Spicewood Springs, and Yarborough. The Ruiz Branch passport office is listed as temporarily closed. Photos are on site for $18 [3]. Other Austin sites are on the Austin page.
  • Denver: the facilities on our Denver page are post offices. Use the USPS scheduler described above, and use that city page for which Denver offices it lists. This guide does not repeat their hours.

What if the trip is soon?

USPS tells people traveling in less than 10 weeks to call the State Department passport agency at 1-877-487-2778 (1-888-874-7793 TDD/TTY). It says the appointment date must be within 72 hours (3 business days) of travel, and that you must provide proof of travel. It also says appointments for travel in the next 72 hours are extremely limited and that other restrictions apply [1]. Those sentences are USPS's instructions. They are not a substitute for the State Department's own eligibility page. Processing times stay on the checklist.

FAQ

Do I sign the DS-11 before the acceptance visit?

Not at a post office. USPS says to print it and not sign it, because a postal employee must witness the signature [1]. Austin Public Library also says "Do not sign it yet" [3]. Houston's page says to complete the form in black ink before you arrive and does not say when to sign [2].

Can I renew a passport at the acceptance counter?

USPS says no if you are eligible to renew: mail or online only [1]. Austin Public Library says it can take a renewal as a new DS-11 and cannot take Form DS-82 [3]. Check the facility.

Why do I pay twice?

The acceptance facility's fee and the State Department's fee are separate. At a post office the acceptance fee is $35, paid to the post office, and the State fee is a separate check or money order sent with the application [1]. Houston and Austin publish their own city fees and payment methods [2][3].

Does every acceptance facility take walk-ins and photos?

No. USPS says to make an appointment and that some post offices have limited walk-in hours. Photo service is offered at many post offices, not described as all of them [1]. Houston books up to two weeks out and takes photos at its six library facilities [2]. Austin is appointment-only, shows only the next three days, and charges $18 for a photo [3].
