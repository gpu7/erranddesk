Library printing: what to bring (2026)

This page is how to prepare for a library print visit: whether you need a card, how the file gets to the printer, and how you pay and pick the pages up. Per-page prices at office stores, pharmacies, and more libraries are in printing prices. Scanning is in the scanning guide, and copying a paper original is in the photocopy guide.

Short version: There is no national library printing policy. Denver describes free printing from library computers or mobile print, on library paper, with a 100-page limit [1]. Houston uses a mobile-print upload and your email at a release terminal, and it charges per print [2]. Duluth and Cheyenne publish their own prices and their own way to send a job [3][4][5]. A USB instruction on one of these pages is about saving a scan, not about printing from a USB drive.

Prices and steps below are what each library publishes for its own system. They are not a national rate, a guest policy, or a promise that every branch works the same way.

What should I check before I go?

  1. Card or guest rules for the computer you will use. Ask the library. The four systems below do not share one rule, and a rule for borrowing a laptop is not a rule for the printer.
  2. How the file gets there. It might be a library computer, a mobile or wireless upload, or an email address you type at a release terminal. One library's email step is not every library's email step.
  3. File type, paper size, color, and how many sides count. "Per page" and "per side" are different. Letter size and legal size can have different prices.
  4. How you pay and how you release the job. Some systems hold a card authorization. Some keep a wireless job for a stated number of hours. Some do not say.

Denver Public Library

Denver says "Free printing from library computers or mobile print from your own device." It offers letter (8.5 by 11 inches) or legal (8.5 by 14 inches). "Printing and copying is limited to 100 pages." The page does not say whether those 100 pages are per day, per visit, or another period. "Only paper supplied by the library can be used," because of the printer vendor's contract. You can also scan a document and send it to your email [1].

A regular public computer requires a free Denver Public Library card. Express computers are 15-minute sessions, no library card required, limited to one session a day. The printing paragraph does not add a separate card rule for mobile print, so this page does not invent one [1]. Library computers have USB ports. The page does not say those ports are how you print a file [1]. More Denver errands are on the Denver page.

Houston Public Library

Houston's mobile-print page is a branch pickup workflow. You choose the library, upload a file (it lists PDF, DOC, DOCX, JPG, PNG, PPT, XLS, TXT, and similar formats), adjust settings, and enter your email address. You then use that email at the print release terminal at the branch you selected. The same steps are described for the Princh mobile web app, including scanning a QR code on a poster near the printer [2]. That email-at-the-terminal step is Houston's. It is not a reason to assume another library accepts email.

The page lists 15¢ for black and white and 50¢ for color, "per print," plus an automatic $5 credit card hold per transaction and a minimum charge of $1 no matter how few pages you print [2]. Bring a card that can cover the hold. The same retail comparison, with these Houston amounts, is in printing prices.

USB on this page is for scanning, not for printing a file you brought. "All public printers have the ability to scan for free to a USB (Flash) drive only. Customers should bring their own USB devices." Some branches also have a SimpleScan device that can scan to a USB, scan to print, or scan to email. "Scan to print" there means scanning a paper original, not opening a file from a USB stick [2]. The Houston page has the rest of the city's print and passport notes.

Duluth Public Library

Full color printing is available at all Duluth library locations. The published prices are $0.15 per black-and-white page for letter size and $0.30 for legal and ledger, and $0.40 per color page for letter size and $0.60 per color page for legal and ledger [3]. Those are per page, for this system.

A job can be sent wirelessly from a phone, laptop, or a computer at home: upload the document, accept the agreement, wait until it says "ready," then release and pick it up at the library. Wireless jobs are stored for 72 hours. "Only letter size documents (8.5"x11") can be printed through this wireless method" [3]. The printing section does not mention a USB drive, and it does not say a library card is required to release a print. This page does not add either rule. The Duluth page covers the library's other services. It does not call printing free, and neither does this guide.

Laramie County Library, Cheyenne

The library's fees page says black-and-white printing is up to $1.00 free per day, then $0.10 per side, and color printing is up to $1.00 free per day, then $0.50 per side [4]. "Per side" means a double-sided sheet can count as two. The computer-center page lists the same $0.10 and $0.50 per-side rates and does not repeat the daily free dollar on that screen [5]. Use the fees page for the allowance.

Wireless printing from a personal device uses the SmartAlec Print Portal [5]. The same page says "Bring a USB drive to save documents." That sentence is about saving documents. It does not say you print by plugging in a USB drive, so this guide does not say that. Borrowing an in-house laptop requires a full-privilege adult library card. The printing lines do not repeat that requirement [5]. See the Cheyenne page for the library's hours and its other document services.

FAQ

Do I need a library card to print?

The four systems on this page do not share one answer. In Denver, a regular public computer needs a free library card, and an express computer does not. Denver's printing paragraph does not add a separate card rule for mobile print [1]. Houston's mobile-print steps use your email at the release terminal and do not say to bring a card [2]. Duluth's printing section and Cheyenne's printing lines do not state a card requirement for the print job [3][5]. Ask the branch before you rely on a guest session.

Can I email a file to any library printer?

No. Houston's mobile print uses the email address you enter, and you pick the job up at that library's release terminal [2]. Duluth's wireless steps are upload, accept, wait until ready, then release, and the job is held 72 hours [3]. Cheyenne points wireless printing to SmartAlec [5]. Denver says mobile print from your own device and does not describe an email address [1].

Can I print from a USB drive?

Not from the wording on these four pages. Cheyenne says to bring a USB drive to save documents [5]. Houston says public printers scan free to a USB drive you bring, and that a SimpleScan station at some branches can scan a paper original to print [2]. Neither sentence says to open a document file from a USB stick and print it.

Is library printing free?

Sometimes, with a limit the library states. Denver says free printing, limited to 100 pages, on library paper only [1]. Cheyenne's fees page gives up to $1.00 of free printing per day, then a per-side charge [4]. Houston and Duluth publish per-print or per-page prices from the first page [2][3]. There is no national free-printing rule.
