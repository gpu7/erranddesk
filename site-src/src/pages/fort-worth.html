Fort Worth, Texas document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Fort Worth, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
Which offices serve Fort Worth: the city's public library is the Fort Worth Public Library [1]. The Tarrant County Clerk issues birth and death certificates, issues marriage licenses, records deeds and accepts passport applications [2][3][4][5]; we didn't find a separate City of Fort Worth vital records office. The Tarrant County District Clerk sells copies of court records [6]. Apostilles come from the Texas Secretary of State in Austin [7]. Fort Worth's city limits include parts of five counties: Tarrant, Denton, Parker, Johnson and Wise [8]. This guide covers Tarrant County offices; if you live in another county's part of the city, check that county's clerk for marriage licenses and recording.
Short version: Fort Worth Public Library mobile printing is free for the first 30 pages with a library card and $0.25 a page after that [1]; printing from library computers is $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color [9]. The passport locator lists 4 Fort Worth facilities, including two Tarrant County Clerk offices [10]. A Tarrant County marriage license is $76 [3], and a birth certificate is $23 [2]. Texas notary fees are capped per signature (statewide) [11], and apostilles are $15 at the Secretary of State [12].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Fort Worth Public Library

  • Mobile printing: send a document from a computer or phone (or the Princh app) and pick it up within 24 hours at the library you chose. With an active library card the first 30 pages are free and pages after 30 are $0.25 each; guests without a card pay $0.25 a page. Pay at the service desk [1].
  • Library computers: the library's public PC policy says printing is $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color, and that printing isn't available on the wireless network. The policy PDF has no date, so it may be older than the mobile printing page [9]. The mobile printing service above is a separate way to print from your own device [1].
  • Copies and scanning: the Open Access page lists document scanning, and says copier services aren't available during Open Access hours (currently at the Northwest Library) [13]. We didn't find copy or scanning prices.
  • Fax: we didn't find a fax service or price on the library pages we checked.
  • The UPS Store: it lists 12 locations in Fort Worth; see its city page for addresses and phone numbers. That page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [14]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [15].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Fort Worth

  • Library: we didn't find a notary service or price on the Fort Worth Public Library pages we checked.
  • The UPS Store: its Fort Worth page lists notarizing [14]. We didn't find store notary prices; the Texas maximums are below.
  • Fee limits (statewide): Government Code § 406.024 lets a Texas notary charge up to $10 for taking an acknowledgment or proof of a deed or other instrument for the first signature and $1 for each additional signature, $10 for administering an oath with a certificate and seal, $10 for a certificate under seal not otherwise provided for, $1 a page for a copy of a record, and $10 for a notarial act not otherwise provided for. The section says the Secretary of State adjusts these amounts for inflation every five years; we didn't find published adjusted amounts, and the Secretary of State's notary pages returned "not found" when we checked on Oct 30, 2026 [11]. The Secretary of State says a notary may charge less or nothing, and an employer may decide what an employee notary charges [16].
  • Online notarization (statewide): under § 406.111, an online notary may charge up to $25 for an online notarization on top of any other fee allowed [11].
  • What notaries can't do (statewide): a Texas notary can't certify a copy of a birth certificate or a marriage license, and a notary who isn't a lawyer can't advertise as a "notario publico" [16][11].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Texas Vital Statistics (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): Texas Vital Statistics (DSHS) charges $22 for a birth certificate (long or short form), $60 for an heirloom birth certificate, $20 for the first death certificate and $3 for each additional copy, and $20 to verify a marriage or divorce. Expedited processing is $25 and is shipped overnight. Paying by card in person in Austin adds $2.25 [20].
  • By mail (statewide): send a notarized application, a copy of your ID and a check or money order [21] to Texas Vital Statistics, Department of State Health Services, P.O. Box 12040, Austin, TX 78711-2040. Its phone number is 888-963-7111, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m. [22].
  • Local offices (statewide): local registrars and county clerks also issue certificates and set their own fees and hours [23]. The local offices for this city are below.

Birth and death certificates: Tarrant County Clerk

  • Fees: birth certificates are $23 each; death certificates are $21 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy bought at the same time. All copies are certified. Bring a current government-issued photo ID. Hours are Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m.; phone 817-884-1550 [2]. Out-of-county births are issued in abstract form only, which may not work for a passport [2].
  • Where: the Tarrant County Plaza Building, 200 Taylor Street, 3rd Floor (entrance on the west side, off Burnett Street), plus subcourthouses including the Charles F. Griffin Building, 3212 Miller Avenue, and the Dionne Phillips Bagsby Southwest Subcourthouse, 6551 Granbury Road, Suite 110 [24].
  • Payment: credit cards, cash, checks or money orders payable to the Tarrant County Clerk; cards add a processing fee [25].

Marriage licenses: Tarrant County Clerk

  • Fee: $76, or $16 for couples who present a printed, valid Twogether in Texas certificate or a similar premarital course certificate. A $100 out-of-state fee applies if neither applicant can prove Texas residency. The informal (common-law) license is $41. Cards add 1.73% [3].
  • How: both applicants appear in person or together by video conference (formal licenses only), at least 18, with valid photo ID and Social Security numbers. There's a 72-hour waiting period, waived with a Twogether or premarital counseling certificate, and the marriage must take place within 90 days [3].
  • Copies: a certified copy is $21, and each additional copy bought at the same time is $11 [26].
  • Waiting period (statewide): Texas has a 72-hour wait between issuing a license and the ceremony. It doesn't apply to active-duty military or Department of Defense employees, if a judge waives it, or if you completed a premarital education course within the past year. A license expires if the ceremony isn't held within 90 days [27].
  • Twogether in Texas (statewide): the state's official Twogether in Texas site, run by Texas Health and Human Services, says completing its 8-hour premarital course waives the $60 state portion of the marriage license fee and the 72-hour waiting period when you show the original certificate of completion to the county clerk. Counties may add up to $22 in local fees, which aren't waived. The certificate is good for one year [28][29]. What the course costs depends on the provider: some are free and others charge for materials. Find a registered provider, including online and no-cost options, on the site's Search Providers page [29][30].

Recorded documents: Tarrant County Clerk

  • Recording fees: most documents are $20 for the first page and $4 for each additional page [4].
  • Copies: in person at the Tarrant County Courthouse, 100 W. Weatherford Street, Room B30, 8 a.m.–5 p.m., copies are $1 a page plus $5 for certification. Unofficial watermarked copies of most records are free online [31].

Court records: Tarrant County District Clerk

  • Fees: paper copies are $1 a page; electronic copies are $1 for 1–10 pages plus $0.10 a page from page 11; certification adds $5. Without a case number there's a $5 research fee. No personal checks [6].

Clerk copy fees (statewide)

  • District clerks (statewide): Government Code § 51.318 sets district clerk fees. A certified copy is $5 for the certificate and seal plus, for each page, $1 printed on paper, $1 for a paper document converted to electronic format, or, for an electronic copy of an electronic document, $1 for up to 10 pages and $0.10 for each page over 10. A noncertified copy is $1 a page on paper or converted to electronic format, or $1 for an electronic document up to 10 pages and $0.10 for each page over 10. A records search is $5 [32]. Section 51.606 says no fee may be charged just to look at records [32].
  • County clerks (statewide): county clerk copy fees are set in Local Government Code Chapter 118, not Chapter 51. We didn't read Chapter 118, so this guide uses each county clerk's own fee pages, above.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: the Texas Secretary of State is the only office that issues apostilles for Texas public documents. It doesn't apostille federal documents, and it doesn't apostille documents meant for use inside the United States [33][7].
  • Fee: $15 per apostille, or $10 per document for adoption-related documents, with a $100 maximum [12].
  • In person in Austin: Authentications Unit, 400 West 15th Street, Austin, (512) 463-5705, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Same-day service is limited to 10 documents. Tuesday–Thursday are by appointment; Monday and Friday are walk-in, with waits that can be over an hour. More than 10 documents go in the drop box, with about a 72-hour turnaround. Pay by check, money order, card or exact cash [7].
  • By mail: about 2 weeks. Send Form 2102 naming the country, with a check or money order payable to the Office of the Texas Secretary of State. Certified vital records must have been issued within the past 5 years, and photocopies aren't apostilled [7]. See the apostille guide.

More on certified copies: how to get a certified copy.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [34]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • City shred carts: the City of Fort Worth says "Shred day is every day": Fort Worth residents can bring paper to secure shred carts at four drop-off stations: Brennan (2400 Brennan Ave.), Southeast (5150 Martin Luther King Freeway), Old Hemphill Road (6260 Old Hemphill Road) and Hillshire (301 Hillshire Drive). The limit is three boxes per visit and six boxes every 30 days, at no more than 40 pounds a box [35]. We didn't confirm the stations' hours.
  • Past event: Tarrant County held a Free Community Shred Day on Saturday, April 18, 2026, 9 a.m.–noon, in Mansfield [36]. That event is past, and we didn't find an upcoming Tarrant County shred event.
  • The UPS Store: its Fort Worth page lists shredding [14]. We didn't find prices.

For paid per-pound shredding, see where to shred documents.

Questions

How much does printing cost at the Fort Worth library?

Mobile printing is free for the first 30 pages with a library card, then $0.25 a page [1]. Printing from library computers is $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color [9].

Can I fax or get something notarized at the Fort Worth library?

We didn't find fax or notary services on the library pages we checked. The UPS Store's Fort Worth page lists both [14].

Where can I apply for a passport in Fort Worth?

The locator lists 4 Fort Worth facilities: two post offices and two Tarrant County Clerk offices [10]. The downtown County Clerk office is open only on Thursdays, by appointment [5].

How much is a marriage license in Tarrant County?

$76, or $16 with a Twogether in Texas or similar premarital course certificate [3].

Where do I get a Fort Worth birth certificate?

From the Tarrant County Clerk, at 200 Taylor Street or a subcourthouse; it's $23 [2][24].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Texas Secretary of State in Austin, in person or by mail, for $15 per apostille (statewide) [7][12].
