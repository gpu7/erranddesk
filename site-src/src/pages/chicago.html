Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Chicago Public Library (CPL)

  • Where: you can print at any CPL location, from a library computer or your own device. Without a library card, you can ask for a daily guest pass for computer access [1]. Most CPL locations have scanners [3].
  • Printing: $0.15 a sheet one-sided and $0.25 a sheet two-sided. The first $1.50 of printing each day is free. Color printing isn't available, and CPL takes cash only for printing [1].
  • Print from your phone or laptop: upload files on the ePrintIT website (choose this for double-sided printing) or email them to mobileprinting@chipublib.org (single-sided only), then release the job at a Print Release Kiosk at any CPL location with your library card number or a release code. Jobs are never kept more than 24 hours [1].
  • When printing stops (CPL pages differ): CPL's printing page and one FAQ say printing ends 10 minutes before closing [1][2], but other FAQ answers say 15 minutes [2][3]. Arrive early or ask the branch.
  • Scanning: free to save to a USB drive or email, as PDF, Word, TIFF or JPEG files. Printing a scan is $0.15 a single-sided black-and-white page; the scanners don't print double-sided or color [3].
  • Fax: CPL says it has no fax machines at any location [2].
  • Copy Center (closed): the Copy Center at Harold Washington Library Center has closed [2].
  • The UPS Store: its Chicago page lists 43 open locations with Chicago addresses (plus one marked permanently closed). It says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [9]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [10].

Notary in Chicago

  • Library: we didn't find notary service on CPL's printing, printer or scanner pages [1][2].
  • The UPS Store: its Chicago page lists notarizing [9].
  • Fees (statewide law): the Illinois Notary Public Act caps the fee for a non-electronic notarial act at $5, and allows up to $25 for an electronic notarial act. The current text took effect Jan. 1, 2026. For immigration forms, the cap is $5 a notarization and $75 for a complete application [11]. Ask for the price before the notary starts.
  • Secretary of State: its Notary Services page doesn't list a fee for getting a document notarized. It says Illinois notaries are commissioned for 4 years (non-residents for 1 year). Notary Division: 217-782-7017 [12].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Which county: the Census lists parts of Chicago city in Cook and DuPage counties [8]. IDPH says you may contact the county clerk where the event occurred [16]. The Cook County Clerk keeps records of births in Chicago and suburban Cook County [17].

Birth and death records (statewide rules)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) Division of Vital Records in Springfield issues birth and death certificates, and it says you may also contact the county clerk where the event occurred [16][18].
  • IDPH birth certificate fees (statewide): $15 or $10 for the first certified copy, depending on the version, and $2 for each additional copy of the same certificate ordered at the same time. A genealogical copy is $10 [16][19], and a commemorative birth certificate is $40 [19].
  • Which version costs what (IDPH pages differ): IDPH's birth certificate page says $15 gets the information collected at the time of birth, and $10 gets a set list of items (name, date and place of birth, parents' details, file date and state file number) [16]. IDPH's fee schedule calls the $15 version the long form, a full copy of the original record, and the $10 version the short form, a computer-generated abstract, but it places the item list beside the $15 version [19]. Ask IDPH or the county clerk which version you need before you order.
  • IDPH death certificate fees (statewide): $19 for a full certified copy and $4 for each additional copy [18][19].
  • IDPH by mail: Illinois Department of Public Health, Division of Vital Records, 110 W Lawrence Ave., Springfield, IL 62704-2610; pay by check or money order payable to IDPH [16]. IDPH says mail requests take about 12 weeks from when it gets your paperwork. For urgent needs, it says to send the request by overnight delivery with proof of immediate need and a prepaid overnight return envelope; certified copies are then processed in 5 to 7 business days [18].
  • IDPH online: through VitalChek, with a major credit card. On the death certificate page IDPH lists a $15.00 handling fee, plus $22.00 if you choose UPS delivery [18].
  • Office move (past): IDPH's Springfield office at 925 East Ridgley Avenue closed, and the new office at 110 W Lawrence Avenue opened on September 14, 2026 [18].

Cook County Clerk (vital records)

  • Fees: birth certificates are $15 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy of the same record [6]. Death certificates are $17, and $6 for each additional copy [17][20]. Certified marriage records are $15, and $4 for each additional copy [20].
  • In person: most records are printed while you wait at the downtown office or three suburban courthouses. Cash, debit and credit cards are accepted (a $1.25 debit or $1.75 credit card processing fee); checks aren't [6]. Downtown Chicago Vital Records is at 118 N. Clark St., open Monday–Friday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., (312) 603-7790 [6], and the Census Geocoder places that address inside Chicago city [5].
  • By mail: Cook County Clerk's Office, Bureau of Vital Records, P.O. Box A3390, Chicago, IL 60690, with the request form, a photocopy of your photo ID, a check or money order payable to “Cook County Clerk” and a self-addressed stamped envelope [6]. Mail orders take about 20 business days on average [20].
  • Online and currency exchanges: VitalChek adds $13.45 for processing and standard postage. Participating Chicago and Cook County currency exchanges add a $5 surcharge, and you pick the record up there later [6].
  • Free birth certificates: available if you are homeless, living in a domestic violence shelter, or formerly incarcerated and released in the last 90 days, with verification from a parole release form or a status certification from an approved human services provider [17].

Marriage licenses

  • Marriage records (statewide): IDPH doesn't issue certified copies of marriage records; they come from the county clerk in the county where the marriage happened. IDPH can verify the facts of a marriage from 1962 on for $5 [21].
  • When a license is valid (statewide law): Illinois law says a marriage license becomes effective in the county where it was issued one day after it is issued, unless a court orders otherwise, and expires 60 days after it becomes effective [22].
  • Cook County Clerk, fee and timing: the license is $60. Licenses are issued while you wait, are valid only for ceremonies in Chicago and suburban Cook County, take effect 1 day after issuance and are valid for 60 days from the date of issuance. The license must be returned to the Clerk within 10 days after the ceremony [7].
  • Validity dates differ: the Clerk says 60 days from the date of issuance [7], while state law says 60 days after the license becomes effective, which is one day after issuance [22]. Confirm the expiration date with the Clerk.
  • Cook County Clerk, how to apply: both of you appear together at one of the Clerk's four Vital Records locations with valid ID showing proof of age. You can fill out the application online first, then sign it in person. If you divorced within the last six months, bring a certified copy of the divorce decree [7].

Recorded documents and court copies

  • Cook County Clerk, recording: deeds, leases, mortgages and releases, easements and miscellaneous documents are $107 each under the schedule in effect since April 1, 2024 (past). That includes a $55 county recording fee, $23 GIS fee, $10 document storage fee, $18 State Rental Housing Support fee and $1 non-government filer fee; documents or filers not subject to the state fee are $19 lower. Recording by mail with return is $5 per document, and recording a DD-214 military discharge is free. The office doesn't quote recording fees by phone [23].
  • Cook County Clerk, copies of recorded documents: a certified paper copy is $55 and a certified digital PDF is $65. A non-certified paper copy is $27.50, and a non-certified electronic download is $5. Copies paid for online carry a 2.10% card fee [23].
  • Clerk of the Circuit Court of Cook County: copies of documents in the Clerk's files are $2.00 for the first page, $0.50 a page for pages 2–20 and $0.25 a page over 20. Certification with the seal is $6.00 for civil cases (not probate), a certified copy of a judgment after the first copy is $10.00, and a record search is $6.00 per year searched [13].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Fee (statewide law): Illinois law sets the Secretary of State's fee for a certificate or apostille, with seal, at $2 [24].
  • Rules (statewide law): requests go on the Secretary of State's form with the fee. The office may refuse a document that isn't an original, isn't properly certified or notarized, or is meant for use in the U.S. or in a country that isn't part of the Hague Convention [25].
  • Where (Secretary of State page): in person at the Index Department, 160 N. LaSalle St., Suite 100, Chicago (312-814-8218) or 2701 S. Dirksen Pkwy., Springfield (217-782-7017), or at select DMV+ locations: the Chicago Flagship (125 W. Monroe), Aurora (970 N. Lake St., Suite B, 630-653-3824), Champaign, Lake Zurich and Melrose Park. The page says the Chicago-area locations authenticate documents for foreign use only [26].
  • How long (Secretary of State page): "In most cases, documents are processed while you wait on a first-come, first-served basis." By mail, send documents to the LaSalle St. address in Chicago with a check or money order and a self-addressed stamped envelope; the page says to allow 7–14 business days. Each document needs its own $2 apostille or certificate [26]. We didn't find office hours on that page, so confirm them before you go.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [27]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

How much is printing at the Chicago Public Library?

$0.15 a sheet one-sided and $0.25 two-sided, cash only, and the first $1.50 each day is free [1].

Can I fax at a Chicago library?

No. CPL says it has no fax machines at any location [2].

How much is a birth certificate in Chicago?

$15 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy at the Cook County Clerk [6]. IDPH in Springfield charges $15 or $10, depending on the version [16].

How much can an Illinois notary charge?

Up to $5 for a non-electronic notarial act and up to $25 for an electronic one (statewide law) [11].

How much is an Illinois apostille?

State law sets the Secretary of State's fee at $2 for a certificate or apostille [24].
