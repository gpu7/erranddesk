Orlando, Florida document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Orlando, Florida, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide, countywide or national rather than local. We label those.

Short version: Orange County Library System prints and copies are $.15 a page, color included, and outgoing faxes are $.50 a page [1][2]. The Florida Department of Health in Orange County sells birth certificates for $15, $8 for each additional copy [3]. An Orange County marriage license is $86.00, or $61.00 with a premarital course [4]. The Clerk takes passport applications at the downtown courthouse for a $35.00 fee [5].
One county, many cities: the Census places all of Orlando city in Orange County [6], so the county offices on this page are Orange County offices. In Orange County, the Clerk of Courts handles passports, marriage licenses and court records, and the separate Orange County Comptroller records deeds and other official records [7][8]. Many places with an "Orlando" mailing address are outside Orlando city, in unincorporated Orange County or in another city. We note where the Census Geocoder places each address [9].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Orange County Library System (OCLS, countywide)

  • Prices: photocopies, computer printouts and color computer printouts are each $.15 a page in person [1]. We didn't find a free daily print allowance on that page.
  • Staff copies by mail or fax: if you ask library staff to make copies to mail or fax, it's a $5.00 handling fee plus $.25 a page for 8.5 x 11 print copies or $.50 a page for microform, up to 10 pages [1].
  • Fax (outgoing only): every library location has a fax machine. The machines send only; they don't receive. Outgoing faxes are $.50 a page, including tax, for all types of faxes [2].
  • Where: the Orlando Public Library is at 101 E. Central Blvd., (407) 835-7323. Its hours are Sunday 1 p.m.–6 p.m., Monday–Thursday 10 a.m.–7 p.m., and Friday–Saturday 10 a.m.–6 p.m. Its listed amenities include a book scanning station, black-and-white copiers, a color printer, fax and scanners [10]. The Census Geocoder places it in Orlando city [9].
  • Orange County Comptroller (recorded documents): copies of official records are $1 a page, and certified copies are $1 a page plus $2 to certify [11]. You can print any document in the Official Records for free from its website [12].
  • Orange County Clerk of Courts (court records): copies are $1.00 a page and certification is $2.00 per document [7]. Passport applicants can get photocopies of citizenship and ID documents for $1.00 a page [13].
  • The UPS Store: its Orlando page says it has 30 locations in Orlando; 2 of them are marked coming soon, which leaves 28 open stores with Orlando addresses. The page says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [14]. The Census Geocoder places these 10 in Orlando city: 424 E Central Blvd; 3208 East Colonial Dr, Ste C; 2014 Edgewater Dr; 2423 S Orange Ave; 4630 S Kirkman Rd; 3936 S Semoran Blvd; 7151 Earhart Dr; 6441 S Chickasaw Trail; 11954 Narcoosee Road; and 5143 S John Young Pkwy [14][9]. It places 16 others in unincorporated Orange County and one (4409 Hoffner Ave) in Belle Isle, and it didn't match 7512 Doctor Phillips Blvd, Ste 50 [14][9]. Prices aren't listed online: its 424 E Central Blvd store page shows no prices and says products, services, pricing and hours of operation may vary by location [15], so call the store for prices (last checked Nov 3, 2026).

Notary in Orlando

  • Orange County Clerk of Courts: the Clerk's fee list shows a notary fee of $3.50 per signature, for court documents only [7]. The State Department's locator data notes "photocopy, passport photo, and notary services" at the Clerk's office, 425 N. Orange Ave., Room 150 [16]. We didn't find whether the Clerk notarizes non-court documents.
  • Orange County Comptroller: if you fill out a Declaration of Domicile at Official Records and take the oath there, it's the $10 recording fee plus $3.50 for the oath, $13.50 in all [12].
  • Library: we didn't find notary service on the library's fee, fax or Orlando Public Library pages [1][2][10].
  • The UPS Store: its Orlando page lists notarizing [14]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store [15]. Notary rules vary by state [17].
  • How notaries are appointed (statewide law): Florida notaries are appointed by the Governor for 4 years. They must be at least 18 and legal residents of Florida, and can act only while they are within the state [18].
  • Fees (statewide law): a Florida notary may charge no more than $10 per notarial act [19]. For an online notarization, the limit is $25 per act; fees for other services, including a remote online notarization (RON) service provider's fees, aren't covered by that limit [20]. Witnessing a vote-by-mail ballot is free [19].
  • ID (statewide law): a notary may rely on an ID that is current or issued within the past 5 years and has a serial or other ID number, such as a Florida ID card or driver's license or a U.S. passport. The law lists other accepted IDs too [19].
  • Check a notary (statewide): the Florida Department of State has an online Notary Search of commissioned notaries [21]. Its Notary Section is at P.O. Box 6327, Tallahassee, FL 32314-6327 [21]; phone 850.245.6975 [22].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates (Florida Department of Health in Orange County)

  • Birth certificate fee: $15 for a birth certificate and $8 for each additional certificate [3].
  • Death certificate fee: the death certificate page lists $10 for a death certificate [27]. We didn't find a price for additional copies on that page. This office has Florida deaths from 2009 to the present. For earlier deaths, use the state Bureau of Vital Statistics in Jacksonville or VitalChek. Only a family member or legal representative can request the cause of death. Questions: 407-858-1460 [27].
  • In person: Vital Statistics, Central Health Center, Building 2, 807 West Church St., 407-858-1460, offers birth and death certificates Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. [28]. The Census Geocoder places it in Orlando city [9].
  • Address (sources differ): the Orange County Comptroller's FAQ gives the health department's vital statistics office as 832 West Central Boulevard, phone 407-836-7128 [12]. The health department's own page lists 807 West Church St. and 407-858-1460 [28]. Go by the health department's page, or call first.
  • Closure (upcoming): all Department of Health offices in Orange County will close on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, for Veterans Day [3].
  • Tax Collector: we didn't find a current official Orange County Tax Collector page with birth certificate prices.
  • Older records: Florida births before 1930 must be requested from the state Bureau of Vital Statistics in Jacksonville [3]. Records of Florida marriages before June 6, 1927 come from the Clerk of Court in the county that issued the license [29].

Birth and death certificates from the state (statewide)

  • State birth certificate fees (statewide): the Florida Bureau of Vital Statistics charges $9 for the first computer-generated birth certification, which includes a non-refundable $9 search fee, and $4 for each additional copy. A photocopy certificate is $14 for the first copy and $4 for each additional copy. Rush service is $10 more, and shipping is $1 [30].
  • Who can get a birth certificate (statewide): births less than 125 years old are confidential. Only the person named (if 18 or older), a parent on the record, a legal guardian, a legal representative or someone with a court order can get one, with a valid photo ID [30].
  • State death certificate fees (statewide): $5 for a one-year search and one certified copy (the $5 search fee isn't refundable), $4 for each additional copy, $2 for each extra year searched (up to $50), $10 for rush service and $1 for shipping. Records before 2009 need the $10 rush fee for same-day walk-in service, when available [31].
  • Who can get a death certificate (statewide): anyone 18 or older can get a certified copy without the cause of death. Records less than 50 years old that show the cause of death are confidential and limited to family and others the department lists [31].
  • State office walk-in (statewide): 1217 N Pearl St., Jacksonville, FL 32202; lobby hours Monday through Friday, 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Pay by cash, check, money order, Visa or Mastercard; contactless and tap-to-pay aren't accepted [30][31].
  • By mail (statewide): Florida Department of Health, Vital Statistics, P.O. Box 210, Jacksonville, FL 32231-0042. Computer-generated birth certificates normally take 3–5 business days plus shipping. Mark the envelope RUSH and add $10 for priority processing [30].
  • Online (statewide): VitalChek is the only vendor the state contracts with. A birth certificate through VitalChek is $19 for the first copy (including the $9 search fee and $10 rush fee), plus a $7 VitalChek processing fee [30].
  • County health departments (statewide): they issue Florida birth records from 1917 to the present, and the state says fees vary by county [30]. Compare the county fee above.

Marriage licenses (Orange County Clerk of Courts)

  • Who issues licenses (statewide law): a county court judge or the clerk of the circuit court [32].
  • Waiting period (statewide law): if you don't give the clerk certificates showing you finished a premarital preparation course, the license takes effect 3 days after you apply. The clerk must make exceptions for people who don't live in Florida and for couples who claim hardship, and a county judge may waive the wait for Florida residents with good cause [33].
  • Course discount (state law has two figures): the course must be at least 4 hours, and section 741.0305 says it cuts your license fee by $32.50 [34]. Section 741.01 says the fee is reduced by $25 for couples with course certificates [32]. Check the county fee below for what you'd actually pay.
  • Validity (statewide law): a Florida marriage license is valid for 60 days after it's issued [35].
  • Orange County fee: $86.00, or $61.00 with proof of a premarital preparation course [4].
  • Payment: cash, money order, cashier's check (payable to the Orange County Clerk of Courts), MasterCard, Visa, Discover or American Express. Card payments carry a $3.50 surcharge [4].
  • Where: the Orange County Courthouse, 425 North Orange Ave., Room 350, or the Apopka, Ocoee or Winter Park branches [4].
  • Waiting period: Florida residents without a premarital course certificate wait 3 days, starting after you both come to the Clerk's office together and buy the license in person. There's no waiting period for people who don't live in Florida. Couples claiming hardship can petition a judge to waive the wait, at the downtown courthouse only [4]. A license is valid for 60 days and can be used in any Florida county [4].
  • Ceremonies: the Clerk performs ceremonies Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m., at the same four locations, for $30.00 [36].
  • Certified copies (who has them; sources differ): the Clerk's marriage and court records pages say the Clerk sells copies of Orange County licenses filed before March 1, 1998 or after June 30, 2017, and the Comptroller has licenses filed from March 1, 1998 through June 30, 2017 [37][7]. The Comptroller's FAQ says the same [12]. The Clerk's mail request form says the Clerk has licenses filed before March 1, 1998 and the Comptroller has those filed on or after that date [38].
  • Copy prices: at the Clerk, a certified copy of a marriage license is $3.00, and each additional copy is $3.00. A decorative marriage license is $10.00 [7]. At the Comptroller (1998–2017 licenses), you can print a plain copy for free from its website, and plain or certified copies are available for a fee in person, by mail or by phone (407-836-5115, option 2) [12]. Its general copy prices are $1 a page, plus $2 to certify [11].

Recorded documents (Orange County Comptroller)

  • State base fees (statewide law): state law lists recording fees of $5.00 for the first page and $4.00 for each additional page, and requires additional service charges on top [39]. The county total below is what you pay.
  • Orange County recording fees: $10 for the first page and $8.50 for each additional page. The first four names are indexed free, then it's $1 per additional name [11].
  • Taxes on deeds: in most cases, documentary stamp taxes must also be paid when you record [12]. The Comptroller's full service fee list is a PDF we couldn't open, so we didn't find its rate table.
  • Copies: $1 a page; certified copies are $1 a page plus $2 to certify, so a 3-page certified copy is $5. Certified copies can be bought online by credit card from the search results page [11][40].
  • Payment: MasterCard and Visa are accepted in person, up to $500 per visit, and by phone for copy orders. Checks and money orders are accepted for everything except tax deeds [11].
  • Timing: recording a deed sent by mail normally takes 7 to 10 business days. The fastest way is in person at 109 East Church Street [12].
  • Where: Official Records, 109 East Church Street, Suite 300, Monday–Friday 7:30 am–4 pm, 407-836-5115. Mail goes to PO Box 38, Orlando, FL 32802 [8]. The Census Geocoder places the office in Orlando city [9].

Court records (Orange County Clerk of Courts)

  • State fee limits (statewide law): Florida law sets clerk charges of $2.00 for certifying a copy of a court record and $1.00 a page for copies up to 14 by 8½ inches [39].
  • Orange County: copies are $1.00 a page, certification is $2.00 per document, and a research fee of $2.00 or more may apply. A diligent search letter is $7.00 [7].
  • Online: non-certified copies of most case types from 2009 on are on the Clerk's MyeClerk website [7].
  • Requests: request other records online, by mail or in person at 425 N. Orange Ave., Suite 150. For copies, the Clerk contacts you with the cost and a delivery estimate, in about 10 business days for mail requests [41].
  • Payment: online by card, with a $1.50 processing fee; by mail with a personal check, money order or cashier's check; in person in Room 150 with cash, card ($3.50 surcharge), check or money order [7].

Apostilles and notarial certifications (statewide)

  • What it is (statewide): the Florida Secretary of State is the only authority in Florida that issues apostilles and notarial certifications [42]. Countries that aren't in the 1961 Hague Convention get a notarial certification instead of an apostille [43].
  • Fee (statewide): $10.00 per document, or $20.00 per document certified by any Florida county Clerk of the Court ($10 for the apostille and $10 for a certificate of incumbency) [42]. State law caps the apostille fee at $10 per apostille [44].
  • Payment (statewide): a check or money order payable to the Florida Department of State, in U.S. currency from a U.S. bank. Cash and credit cards aren't accepted [42].
  • By mail (statewide): send the request form, the document (an original certified copy, not a photocopy) and a self-addressed stamped envelope or prepaid airbill [42] to Department of State, Division of Corporations, Apostilles Section, P.O. Box 6800, Tallahassee, FL 32314; phone 850.245.6053 [22]. Requests are processed in the order received, and there's no expedited service [43].
  • Walk-in (statewide): 2415 N. Monroe Street, Suite 810, Tallahassee, Monday through Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m., except state holidays. Most requests can be completed while you wait [43].
  • Birth, death and marriage records (statewide): birth and death certificates must be signed by the State Registrar, whether issued by the Bureau of Vital Statistics or a county health department. Marriage and divorce certificates must be signed by the State Registrar or the Clerk of the County Courts [45].
  • Elsewhere (statewide): federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State, and documents from another state go to that state [42]. The office doesn't translate, and the translator can't be the notary [43].

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [46]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Where do I get a birth certificate in Orlando?

At the Florida Department of Health in Orange County, 807 West Church St., for $15, or $8 for each additional copy [3][28]. The state Bureau of Vital Statistics in Jacksonville charges $9 for a computer-generated birth certification [30].

How much is a marriage license in Orlando?

$86.00 at the Orange County Clerk of Courts, or $61.00 with a premarital course [4].

Can I fax at an Orlando library?

Yes, outgoing only, for $.50 a page at any OCLS location [2].

Can I renew my passport at the Orange County Clerk?

No. The Clerk doesn't handle passport renewals [23], and the library doesn't accept them either [24].

How much does a Florida notary charge?

No more than $10 per notarial act under state law, or $25 for an online notarization [19][20].
