Which offices serve Missoula: the Missoula County Clerk & Recorder sells birth and death certificates [1]. Marriage licenses come from a different office, the Clerk of District Court, on the 2nd floor of the Missoula County Courthouse [2]. Montana apostilles come from the Secretary of State in Helena [3].
Short version: the Missoula Public Library offers free notarization [4] and passport appointments, with passport photos for $15 [5]. Montana caps notary fees at $10 per notarial act (statewide) [6]. A county Clerk & Recorder charges $8 for a certified birth certificate under state law, versus $16 from the state office [7][8]. A marriage license is $53 [2]. Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Missoula Public Library

  • Printing: the library offers web and email printing on Levels One and Three [9]. We didn't find a print price on that page.
  • Copies: the passport locator lists the library with the note "Photocopy services available" [10]. We didn't find a copy price.
  • Fax: we didn't find a fax service or price on the library pages we checked.
  • Location: 455 E. Main St., Missoula, MT 59802, (406) 721-2665 [4].

Notary in Missoula

  • Library: "MPL offers free notarization services to the public." Call ahead at 406-721-2665 or visit Level Three. The library can't provide witnesses or notarize I-9 forms [4].
  • Vehicle titles (statewide): the library notes that, from Oct. 1, 2025, Montana motor vehicle title transfers no longer require notarization [4].
  • The UPS Store: the 400 West Broadway store lists notary hours "Available 7 Days" [13]. We didn't find notary prices.
  • Fees (statewide): Montana law lets a notary "charge a fee not to exceed $10 for each notarial act," such as an acknowledgment, a signature witnessing or a jurat. A notary may add a travel fee (no more than the IRS standard mileage rate) or a fee for an electronic or remote notarization only if the notary explains it's extra and not set by law and you agree to the amount in advance. A notary who charges fees must display a list of them in English [6]. That's the same $10 cap as Wyoming and Arizona and double North Dakota's $5.

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Missoula County Clerk & Recorder

  • How: request online, by mail with the county's printable form, or in person [1].
  • Price: we didn't find a price on the county page. State law sets the county fee at $8 for a certified birth certificate and $7 for a certified death certificate [7].
  • Outage dates differ: on Oct. 29, 2026, the county page still said it could issue birth certificates only for people born in Missoula County during state maintenance, and that statewide issuance would resume Oct. 28 [1]. The state notice gives Oct. 26 [16]. Call before you go.

Birth and death certificates (statewide)

  • Any county office (statewide): you can buy a Montana birth or death certificate in person at any county Clerk & Recorder's office in Montana, no matter which county the event happened in. Bring a current photo ID; you fill out the application there. A long-form birth certificate (often needed for dual citizenship or tribal enrollment) comes only from the state office in Helena or the Clerk & Recorder in the county of birth [8].
  • County price (statewide law): state law sets the county clerk's fee at $8 for each certified copy of a birth certificate and $7 for each certified copy of a death certificate [7].
  • State price: the Office of Vital Records charges $16 for every certificate, birth or death, certified or informational, abstract or long form [8][16]. Its fee schedule took effect Sept. 21, 2024 [16].
  • State office (Helena): order by mail to Montana Vital Records, PO Box 4210, Helena, MT 59604, with a check or money order and a copy of your ID or a notarized application; online or by phone through VitalChek (1-888-877-1946) or VRO, which add card service fees. The office is closed to walk-ins. Questions: (406) 444-2685, option 2, or HHSVitalRecords@mt.gov [8].
  • Late-October outage: the state notice says no Montana office issued birth certificates Oct. 21–25, 2026, during a move to a new records platform, and statewide issuance resumed Monday, Oct. 26 [16].

Marriage licenses: Missoula County Clerk of District Court

  • How: apply online first, then both of you appear in person at the Clerk of District Court, 2nd floor of the Missoula County Courthouse, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Monday through Friday [2].
  • Fee and payment: $53, by cash, money order or card (a card fee of 1.995% plus $1.25 applies). Personal checks aren't accepted [2].
  • Validity: 180 days, in Montana only [2].
  • License fee (statewide law): $53 when one or both of you are present at the ceremony, or $83 when neither is (a double proxy) [17]. Marriage licenses come from the Clerk of District Court in Montana, not the Clerk & Recorder.
  • Proof of age (statewide law): each applicant must provide a birth certificate or other satisfactory evidence of age [18].

Recorded documents: Missoula County Clerk & Recorder

  • Copies (statewide law): county clerk copy fees are 50 cents for the first page of a document and 25 cents for each later page, plus $2 for each certification with seal [7].
  • Recording fees: we didn't find a Missoula County recording fee page in our checks.

Court records: Clerk of District Court

  • Fees (statewide law): the clerk of district court charges $1 a page for the first 10 pages of copies from a file, for each request, and 50 cents for each additional page; 25 cents a page for copies sent by fax, email or other electronic means; $2 for each certificate with seal; $5 for a copy of a marriage license and $10 for a copy of a dissolution decree. A records search is $2 per name for each year, for up to 7 years, and $1 per name for each further year [17].

Apostilles (statewide)

Montana apostilles come from the Secretary of State in Helena, not the county. For a Montana birth or death certificate, note on your Vital Records application that it's for an apostille (you'll get a long form), and contact the Secretary of State's office to tell it the document is coming. Pay the Secretary of State $10.00 per apostille, separately from the Vital Records fee [3]. Vital Records will mail the certificate to the Secretary of State once it's issued; the Secretary of State's office is at (406) 444-1877 [8]. We couldn't open the Secretary of State's own apostille page from our checker (its firewall blocked us), so we haven't confirmed its current instructions there: sosmt.gov/apostilles-authentications/apostilles. See the apostille guide.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [19]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Is there a free notary in Missoula?

Yes. The Missoula Public Library offers free notarization; call ahead at 406-721-2665 or visit Level Three [4].

What does a notary cost in Montana?

No more than $10 per notarial act, plus travel or remote-notarization fees only if you agree to them in advance (statewide) [6].

Where can I apply for a passport in Missoula?

The Missoula Public Library, by appointment, and the post office at 1100 West Kent Ave. [10][5].

How much are passport photos at the Missoula library?

$15. The library doesn't photograph infants or toddlers [5].

Where do I get a marriage license in Missoula?

At the Clerk of District Court, 2nd floor of the Missoula County Courthouse, for $53. Apply online first [2].
