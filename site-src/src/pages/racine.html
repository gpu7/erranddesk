Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Racine Public Library

  • Where: Main Library, 75 7th Street, Racine, (262) 636-9217. The page footer lists hours of Monday–Thursday 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. and Friday–Saturday 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. [1]. The Census Geocoder places the Main Library inside Racine city [4].
  • Printing: 10 cents a page for black and white and 50 cents for color. The printing station is on the second floor, across from the staircase, and a copier is on the first floor [1]. We didn't find a copier price.
  • Print from anywhere: since November 2024 the library uses Princh. Go to print.princh.com, enter printer code 110995, upload your file and pay online; you don't have to be in the library for that part. Pick up your pages at the library printers within three hours. Printing by app or email forward is no longer available, and you can't print a webpage by entering its URL [1].
  • Fax and scanning: we didn't find fax or scanning prices on the library's printing page.
  • The UPS Store: it lists 1 location with a Racine address: 2310 S Green Bay Rd, Ste C, (262) 554-7337. Its city page says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [6]. The Census Geocoder places it inside Racine city [4]. We didn't find prices on the store page; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [7].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Racine

  • Racine Public Library, free: notary service is free while the library is open, for anyone, with or without a library card. Call (262) 636-9170 to book a time during library hours; same-day appointments aren't always available. All signers must be there in person [2].
  • Individuals only: the library notarizes only for individuals, not for businesses or organizations [2].
  • What to bring: your documents, unsigned (you sign in front of the notary); a current, valid ID with your photo and signature, such as a driver's license, state ID card, military ID or U.S. passport; and your own witnesses if the document needs them [2].
  • Limits: library notaries can't certify copies of birth, death or marriage certificates, verify employment eligibility for employers, or choose, complete or give advice on documents [2].
  • The UPS Store: its Racine page lists notarizing [6].
  • Fees (statewide): Wisconsin's Department of Financial Institutions (DFI) says a notary "may charge no more than $5 for most in-person notarial acts." Ask for the price before the notary starts [8].
  • Statewide commission: DFI says a Wisconsin commission lets a notary act in all counties in the state [8].
  • No copies of vital records (statewide): DFI tells notaries they are "strictly prohibited" from making copies, certified or uncertified, of birth, death, divorce, marriage and other vital records. Get certified copies from a state or county vital records office instead [8].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death, marriage and divorce records (statewide rules)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS) says Wisconsin birth, death, marriage and divorce records from October 1907 to now are available from the Wisconsin Vital Records Office, and also through any Wisconsin county Register of Deeds office. Availability depends on the event type and date [11].
  • Fees (statewide): $20 for the first copy of each record and $3 for each additional copy of the same record [11]. State law sets the same $20 and $3 fees [12]. DHS says the long form birth certificate doesn't cost extra; write "Long Form" on the application [11].
  • Who can get a certified copy: state law limits who can get one; an uncertified copy is printed on plain paper and stamped as not usable for legal or identification purposes [11].
  • State office by mail: Wisconsin Vital Records Office, PO Box 309, Madison, WI 53701. It takes about 10 business days after the office gets your order, plus mail time. Pay by cash, check or money order in U.S. funds [11].
  • Online and phone: through VitalChek, online or at 877-885-2981; there is a fee for using VitalChek, and requests take about five business days [11].

Marriage licenses

  • Marriage licenses (statewide law): in Wisconsin the county clerk issues marriage licenses. A license can't be issued within 3 days of the application unless the clerk, at its discretion, issues it sooner for an added fee of up to $25. Each applicant must show a certified copy of a birth record, or one of the other documents the law allows, and swear to the application before the clerk. State law sets the county clerk's fee for each license at $49.50; that is the statutory clerk fee, not necessarily the full price a county charges [13].
  • Racine County Clerk: Racine County's marriage license page returned "Access Denied" to our checks, so we didn't find the county's fee, hours or appointment rules. As noted above, the $49.50 statewide figure is the clerk fee set by state law, not necessarily the full price a county charges. Confirm with the office.

Recorded documents and court copies

  • Recording fees (statewide law): Wisconsin law sets the register of deeds fee for recording a document at $30. Copies of recorded documents are $2 for the first page and $1 for each additional page, plus $1 to certify a copy [14].
  • Court copies (statewide fee table): the Wisconsin court system lists Clerk of Circuit Court copies at $1.25 a page, and certified copies at $5 to certify the document plus $1.25 a page [15].
  • Racine County: we didn't check the Register of Deeds' real estate fee page or the Clerk of Circuit Court's copy fees.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Two state offices: both the Wisconsin Secretary of State (SOS) and DFI can issue apostilles for Hague Convention countries. Authentications for countries outside the Hague Convention come only from the SOS [16].
  • DFI: standard service is $10 per document (3–7 business days) and expedited is $35 per document (1–2 business days), not counting mail time. DFI, 4822 Madison Yards Way, North Tower, Madison, WI 53705; (608) 266-8915 [17].
  • Secretary of State: standard service is $10 per document (an estimated 7–20 days from when the office gets the documents) and expedited is $35 per document (2–4 business days). Walk-in expedited service is generally available the same day at the office on the basement level of the State Capitol in Madison. It accepts card, cash, check or money order [16].
  • Notarized documents: the SOS accepts only wet-ink stamped or embossed notarizations by a Wisconsin notary, not electronic ones. It can't process federal documents or vital records from another state or country [16].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [18]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

How much is printing at Racine Public Library?

10 cents a page for black and white and 50 cents for color [1].

Is there a free notary in Racine?

Racine Public Library offers free notary service while it's open; call (262) 636-9170 to book [2].

Can I print at the Racine library from home?

Yes. Upload at print.princh.com with printer code 110995, pay online, and pick up within three hours [1].

How much can a Wisconsin notary charge?

No more than $5 for most in-person notarial acts (statewide) [8].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions or the Wisconsin Secretary of State, both in Madison: $10 per document standard or $35 expedited (statewide) [17][16].
