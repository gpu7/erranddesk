Fresno, California document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Fresno, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Not affiliated, not legal advice. Errand Desk isn't affiliated with the Fresno County Public Library, the County of Fresno, the Superior Court of California, the California Department of Public Health, the California Secretary of State, the State of California, California State University, Fresno, USPS, The UPS Store, the U.S. Department of State or any business named here. This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
Which offices serve Fresno: Fresno's library is the Fresno County Public Library; its downtown Central Library closed for renovation after Feb. 15, 2026, and the closure is expected to last until 2028 [1]. The Fresno County Clerk issues marriage licenses, accepts passport applications and notarizes signatures, all by online appointment [2][3][4]. The Fresno County Recorder sells birth, death and public marriage certificates and recorded documents [5][6]; the Department of Public Health also issues birth and death certificates for the last 2 years [7]. Court records come from the Superior Court of California, County of Fresno [8], and apostilles from the California Secretary of State [9].
Short version: Fresno County Public Library prints are $0.10 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color, self-service scanning is free, and faxing is $1 for the first page plus $0.30 for each additional page at branches that offer it [10]. The County Clerk notarizes for $15 a signature by appointment [4]. The passport locator lists 3 Fresno facilities: a post office, the County Clerk and The Passport Place at Fresno State [11]. Marriage licenses are $58 public or $69 confidential [2]. California notaries may charge at most $15 per signature (statewide), and a 2026 law raises that to $20 [12][13].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Fresno County Public Library

  • Prints and copies: self-made computer printouts are $0.10 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color; self-serve photocopies are $0.10 a page, and staff-made copies from microform are $1.00 a page. The same fees apply if you bring your own paper [10].
  • Mobile printing: branch pages link to the library's mobile printing service, which lets you send a print job from your phone or computer to a branch printer [14][15].
  • Scanning: the self-service kiosk scan is free to your own USB drive or email; a CD is $2.00. Staff scanning of Heritage Center materials is $1.00 per item up to 10 pages, only at the Central Library [10], which is closed for renovation [1].
  • Fax: $1.00 for the first page plus $0.30 for each additional page, with no charge for the cover sheet; the library says to check each branch page to see whether fax is offered [10]. The Gillis and Woodward Park branch pages list fax, outgoing only (incoming faxes aren't accepted) [14][15].
  • Branches: Gillis, 629 W. Dakota Ave., (559) 600-9915 [14]; Woodward Park Regional, 944 E. Perrin Ave., (559) 600-3135 [15]; Fig Garden Regional, 3071 W. Bullard Ave., (559) 600-4071, which lists printing, copying and a free scanning kiosk [16]. The library names West Fresno, Mosqueda, Gillis, Betty Rodriguez Regional, Sunnyside Regional and Easton as branches near the closed Central Library [1].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 7 locations in Fresno: 373 E Shaw, (559) 225-6153; 2037 W Bullard Ave, (559) 432-5011; 7726 N 1st St, (559) 261-2600; 1134 E Champlain Dr, Ste. 101, (559) 412-7757; 700 Van Ness Ave, Ste. 120, (559) 272-0600; 6569 N Riverside Dr, Ste. 102, (559) 275-4800; and 6061 E Kings Canyon Rd, (559) 325-4877. Its city page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [17]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [18].

Notary in Fresno

  • Fresno County Clerk: notarizes signatures by appointment for $15 per signature, paid by check, credit or debit card, cash or money order ($100 bills aren't accepted). Bring government-issued photo ID and complete documents; staff can't give legal advice [4].
  • Library: we didn't find notary service on the Fresno County Public Library pages we checked.
  • The UPS Store: its Fresno page lists notarizing [17]. We didn't find store notary prices; California's legal maximum is below.
  • Fees now (statewide): California Government Code § 8211 caps notary fees at $15 for each signature on an acknowledgment and $15 for an oath or affirmation with a jurat; certifying a copy of a power of attorney is also $15 [12]. The Secretary of State's January 2026 Notary Public Handbook lists the same maximums and says a notary may charge less or nothing [19]. Notarizing vote-by-mail ballot envelopes, and a veteran's application or claim for benefits, is free [12].
  • Scheduled change (statewide): AB 1597 (Chapter 39, Statutes of 2026), approved by the Governor on June 30, 2026, amends § 8211 to raise those caps to $20 [13]. The bill text doesn't print a start date; under the state constitution, a statute enacted at a regular session takes effect on January 1 after a 90-day period from enactment [20], which for this bill is Jan. 1, 2027. The published code section still shows $15 [12]. The Secretary of State's January 2026 handbook also still lists $15, and the Secretary of State notary and apostille pages we checked don't mention AB 1597 [19][21].
  • Extras and remote notarization (statewide): the handbook says any travel fee should be entered separately in the notary's journal; it sets no travel amount [19]. California notaries can't yet perform remote online notarizations: those parts of the Online Notarization Act start once the Secretary of State finishes its system, or by Jan. 1, 2030, whichever is earlier [19].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death and marriage records: California Department of Public Health (statewide)

  • State fees: since Jan. 1, 2026, CDPH Vital Records charges $31 for a certified copy of a birth record, $26 for a death record and $19 for a marriage record, under AB 64 (Chapter 662, Statutes of 2025) [24].
  • Paying by mail: make the check or money order payable to CDPH Vital Records, drawn on a U.S. bank; don't send cash. Requests without the right fee are returned. If no record is found, you get a Certificate of No Public Record and the fee is kept as a search fee [24].
  • Ordering from CDPH by mail: send the birth application (VS 111) or death application (VS 112) and a check or money order for $31.00 per birth copy or $26.00 per death copy (the order pages say to make it payable to "CDPH-VR") to California Department of Public Health, Vital Records - MS 5103, P.O. Box 997410, Sacramento, CA 95899-7410. The notarized sworn statement (VS 20), which is part of the application, is needed only for an authorized copy [25][26].
  • Online and in person: CDPH takes electronic orders only through a third-party vendor; for in-person copies it points you to the county office where the birth or death was registered [25][26].
  • Processing time: CDPH's average is 4 to 6 weeks for a certified copy, counted from when it receives the request. It says you can often get a certificate faster from the county office, and that county fees may differ from CDPH's [27].
  • County copies: county recorders also issue certified copies; the county office for this city is below.

Birth, death and marriage certificates: Fresno County Recorder

  • Fees: $31 per birth certificate, $26 per death certificate and $19 per public marriage certificate. The same amounts are charged to search when no certified copy is made [6][5].
  • In person: the Recorder has moved out of the Hall of Records to 1250 Van Ness Ave., with free customer parking; hours are Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m., (559) 600-3476 [5][28]. Apply at the kiosk, then show ID and pay at the counter; most copies are ready the same day. Pay by check or money order, or by credit card for an extra $2.50. The drive-through window is temporarily closed [5].
  • By mail: send a notarized application with a check or money order (no cash) to Fresno County Recorder, P.O. Box 766, Fresno, CA 93712. Certificates come back by regular untracked mail unless you include a prepaid tracked envelope [5].
  • Online: through Vital Access, with a 3.5% service fee and UPS or regular mail delivery [5].
  • Recorded documents: copies are $1.50 for the first letter-size page and $0.50 for each additional page, plus $1.00 per document to certify; military records are free [6].

Birth and death certificates from the last 2 years: Department of Public Health

  • What it covers: Fresno County births and deaths from the last 24 months only; for older records it refers you to the Recorder [7].
  • Fees: $31 per birth certificate and $26 per death certificate, after a $2 state increase on Jan. 1, 2026. Fee waivers exist for homeless people and youth in foster care [29].
  • Where and when: 1221 Fulton St., 1st Floor, Room 128, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m., closed 12–12:30 p.m., (559) 600-3310 [7].

Marriage licenses and confidential marriages: Fresno County Clerk

  • Fees: $58 for a public license and $69 for a confidential license; a civil ceremony at the Clerk's office is $21. Pay by cash or credit or debit card; checks and $100 bills aren't accepted [2].
  • How: licenses and ceremonies are by appointment only, booked online. Both people must appear with a physical government-issued photo ID, and the license is issued the same day. The ceremony must take place within 90 days. A confidential license requires that you live together [2].
  • Copies: the Clerk doesn't sell public marriage certificates; get those from the Recorder for $19. Confidential marriage certificates come from the Clerk for $19 a copy, only to the couple or by court order [2].

Court records: California superior courts (statewide fees)

  • Fees (statewide): under Government Code § 70627, the court clerk charges $0.50 a page to prepare copies of a record on file, $1 a page to compare a copy you bring with the original (plus the certificate fee), and $15 for a records search that takes a court employee more than 10 minutes [30].

Court records: Superior Court of California, County of Fresno

  • Archives: the court's Archives Department will pull your non-confidential file, copy it and certify the documents you ask for; bring photo ID. It's at 1963 "E" Street, open Monday and Wednesday 8 a.m.–3 p.m. and Friday 8 a.m.–noon, (559) 457-4903 [8].
  • Criminal case record fees: the court's notice lists $0.50 a page and $40.00 to certify a copy; it says this doesn't apply to civil case types [31].
  • Court fee schedule: the court's Fee Schedules page links the Statewide Civil Fee Schedule, effective Jan. 1, 2024, which it says includes Fresno County miscellaneous fees. Counter payment can be cash, personal, cashier's or certified check, money order or credit card, with checks payable to Fresno County Superior Court [32].
  • Record fees on that schedule: $0.50 a page for copies (GC 70627(a)), $40 to certify a copy (GC 70626(a)(4)), $1 a page to compare a copy with the original (GC 70627(b)), $15 for a search over 10 minutes (GC 70627(c)), $50 for an exemplification (GC 70628) and $15 for a certified copy of a marriage or domestic partnership dissolution record ($10 if a public agency asks) (GC 70674) [33]. We didn't find probate-specific copy fees.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: California apostilles come from the Secretary of State, not the county. The document must be signed by a California public official or be an original notarized or certified document; photocopies aren't accepted [9]. The office doesn't notarize, so get the notarization first [34].
  • Birth and death certificates: a certificate signed by a local health officer or registrar may first need certifying by the county clerk, or you can get a certified copy from the county recorder or CDPH [34].
  • Fee: $20.00 per apostille. Include a cover sheet naming the country where the document will be used. In person there is also a $6.00 special handling fee for each different public official's signature [9][21]. The fee list also shows $5.00 for affixing the Certificate and Seal of State [21].
  • In person, same day: Sacramento, 1500 11th Street, 3rd Floor (card, check, money order or cash) [9], Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. except state holidays, (916) 653-3595 [35]; or Los Angeles, 300 South Spring Street, Room 12513, (213) 897-5310 [9][36].
  • Los Angeles office details: in person only, same day, first come, first served, with no appointments, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. except state holidays. It handles apostilles and domestic partnership filings only, doesn't take mail requests, and charges the $6.00 special handling fee for each different public official's signature on an apostille request. Payment is Visa, Mastercard, check or money order; no cash. It is temporarily serving customers on the 1st floor in Suite 1215 because of an elevator outage [36].
  • By mail: Sacramento only, with a $20.00 check or money order per apostille payable to Secretary of State and a self-addressed return envelope. USPS mail goes to Notary Public Section, P.O. Box 942877, Sacramento, CA 94277-0001; FedEx, UPS or DHL to 1500 11th Street, 2nd Floor, Sacramento, CA 95814. Mail requests aren't expedited [9]. The office also runs pop-up apostille events around the state[9][37]. At pop-ups the fee is $20 per apostille plus $6 special handling for each different official's signature, paid by Visa, Mastercard, check or money order (no cash); bring the original document and a pop-up cover sheet, and expect waits of up to 2–3 hours [37]. See the apostille guide.
  • Local signature authentication: the Fresno County Clerk authenticates the signature of a notary registered in Fresno County, or of the Fresno County Health Officer, for $2.25 per document, in person by appointment or by mail to 2221 Kern St., Fresno, CA 93721 [38].
  • Local pop-up: the Secretary of State's pop-up shop page lists none in Fresno as of Oct. 30, 2026 [37].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [39]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Can I fax at the Fresno County Public Library?

At some branches. Faxing is $1.00 for the first page plus $0.30 for each additional page [10]; the Gillis and Woodward Park branches list outgoing fax [14][15].

How much does printing cost at the library?

$0.10 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color; photocopies are $0.10 a page [10].

Where can I get something notarized in Fresno?

The Fresno County Clerk notarizes for $15 per signature by online appointment [4]. The UPS Store's Fresno page also lists notarizing [17].

Where can I apply for a passport in Fresno?

The locator lists the Fresno Downtown Post Office, the Fresno County Clerk and The Passport Place at Fresno State [11]. The Clerk takes applications by appointment only [3].

How much is a marriage license in Fresno?

$58 for a public license or $69 for a confidential one, by appointment at the County Clerk; cash or card only [2].

What does a notary cost in California?

At most $15 per signature under Government Code § 8211 as published now. AB 1597 raises the cap to $20, and under the state constitution's timing rule that takes effect Jan. 1, 2027 (statewide) [12][13][20].

Where do I get an apostille in Fresno?

From the California Secretary of State, in Sacramento or Los Angeles or by mail to Sacramento, for $20 per apostille (statewide) [9]. The Fresno County Clerk authenticates local notary and Health Officer signatures for $2.25 [38].
