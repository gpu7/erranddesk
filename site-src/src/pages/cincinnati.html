Cincinnati, Ohio document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Cincinnati, Ohio, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

This is general information, not legal advice. Prices and hours change; check the linked source before you go.
Short version: the Cincinnati & Hamilton County Public Library offers free faxing with no page limit and some free printing each day [1]. Its passport service is by appointment only [2]. The Cincinnati Health Department sells birth certificates for $22 and death certificates for $27, with same-day pickup [3][4][5]. A Hamilton County marriage license is $75, and checks aren't accepted [6]. The Hamilton County Clerk of Courts takes passport applications without an appointment and offers photos [7].
One county: the Census places all of Cincinnati city in Hamilton County [8], so the county offices on this page are Hamilton County offices.

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Cincinnati & Hamilton County Public Library (CHPL)

  • Free pages (the library's pages differ): the library's Print and Fax page says you can print up to 20 pages in color or 33 in black and white per day for free at any of its 41 locations [1]. The library's price list, updated Feb. 2, 2026, says the first 20 pages are free for both black-and-white and color copies and prints [9]. Ask staff which limit applies.
  • After the free pages: $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.25 in color. You don't need a library card [1]. The price list says its prices include 7% sales tax [9].
  • From your device: send documents from home, email or a phone, then release them at any library location within 24 hours with your library card number, guest ID or email release code [1].
  • Fax: free at all library locations, with no page limit and no library card needed. The library provides a cover sheet, and a printed confirmation sheet is available [1].
  • Scanning and lamination: we didn't find a scanning price on the library's print and fax page or price list [1][9]. Lamination is $0.50 a page [9].
  • Main Library: 800 Vine Street, 513-369-6900 [2], which the Census Geocoder places in Cincinnati city [10].
  • The UPS Store: its Cincinnati page lists stores with Cincinnati mailing addresses and says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [11]. The Census Geocoder places none of the 9 open stores in Cincinnati city: for example, 2692 Madison Rd, Ste N1 is in Norwood city, and several are in townships outside any city [11][10]. Prices aren't listed online: the 2692 Madison Rd store page shows no prices and says products, services, pricing and hours of operation may vary by location [12], so call the store for prices (last checked Nov 1, 2026).

Notary in Cincinnati

  • Cincinnati & Hamilton County Public Library: we didn't find notary service on the library's services, print and fax, or price list pages [13][1][9]. Call 513-369-6900 to ask.
  • The UPS Store: its Cincinnati page lists notarizing [11]. Prices aren't listed online; call the store [12].
  • How notaries are commissioned (statewide law): the Ohio Secretary of State issues all notary commissions. Applicants must be at least 18 and Ohio residents (or attorneys admitted in Ohio who practice here), and most must submit a criminal records check from the past six months and complete an education program and test [14]. A commission lasts five years unless revoked; attorneys keep theirs as long as they qualify [15]. Ohio notaries are commissioned "for the state" [14] and have statewide jurisdiction [16].
  • Fees (statewide law): since April 3, 2025, an Ohio notary may charge up to $5 for a notarial act that isn't online, or up to $30 for an online notarization. Fees can't be charged per signature. A notary may also charge a reasonable travel fee agreed before the notarial act, and a technology fee of up to $10 for an online notarization [17].
  • Ohio Secretary of State pages: we tried to read the Secretary of State's notary pages on Nov 1, 2026, but they blocked our check, so the notary rules above come from the Ohio Revised Code.

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates (Cincinnati Health Department)

  • Fees: birth certificates are $22 a copy, and death certificates are $27 a copy. There's a non-refundable $3 search fee for certificates that aren't found [3][4]. Both are more than the state's $21.50 in the statewide section below.
  • What it issues: Ohio birth certificates only, from 1909 to the present [3], and death certificates. The department says Ohio is currently an open state, so no ID is required to get a death certificate [5].
  • In person (same day): 1525 Elm Street, first floor, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–3:45 p.m. (10:30 a.m.–3:45 p.m. on the first Tuesday of the month) [3]. Certificates ordered in person or online can be picked up the same day, 8 a.m.–3:45 p.m.; any not picked up by 3:45 p.m. are mailed within one to two business days [5]. The Census Geocoder places 1525 Elm St in Cincinnati city [10].
  • University Hospital: 234 Goodman Street (under the cafeteria steps), Sunday–Saturday 6 a.m.–9 p.m. Birth certificates ordered here are mailed, which can take up to 20 business days [3].
  • Online, by phone or by mail: order online (added credit card fees apply) or by phone at 1-866-550-1853. The department says VitalChek is its only partner and only source of expedited service [3]. The mail application asks for a money order payable to Treasurer, City of Cincinnati [22].
  • Questions: 513-352-3120 or vitalrecords@cincinnati-oh.gov [23].

Birth and death certificates from the state (statewide)

  • State fee (statewide): the Ohio Department of Health (ODH) charges $21.50 for a certified birth or death record. Since Jan. 1, 2025, that $21.50 is a search fee, charged whether or not a record is found [24]. State law says the state fee can't be less than $12, and that local offices and the state add a $5 fee to each certified copy [25]. Local offices can charge more than the state: compare the city fee above.
  • Any Ohio birth, at local offices (statewide): ODH says more than 100 local offices offer same-day certified copies of Ohio vital records in person or in other ways [24]. State law lets a local registrar issue a certified copy to any applicant, unless the record is no longer public [26].
  • How to order from the state (statewide): online by credit card (processed within five business days; about three weeks for delivery by mail; no expedited service), or by mail to ODH Bureau of Vital Statistics, PO Box 15098, Columbus, Ohio 43215-0098, with a check or money order (four to six weeks) [24]. Online birth certificates are abstract copies that ODH says are valid for all U.S. legal uses, including passports [24].
  • State office walk-in (statewide): 4200 Surface Road, Columbus, Monday through Friday 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., only for central Ohio customers who need an expedited copy of their birth record and pay by credit card [24].
  • Deaths before 1971 (statewide): the state's records are with the Ohio History Connection. ODH says to get certified copies from the local health department where the death happened [24].

Marriage licenses (Hamilton County Probate Court)

  • Where to apply (statewide law): both of you appear in person at the probate court of the county where either of you lives. If neither of you lives in Ohio, apply in the county where you plan to marry [27].
  • Waiting period and validity (statewide law): a probate judge may grant the license "at any time after the application is made" [27], and the license expires 60 days after it's issued [28]. A passport is one of the accepted proofs of age [27]. Ohio law doesn't set one statewide license fee; check the county fee below.
  • Hamilton County fee: $75.00, paid by cash, credit or debit card. Checks aren't accepted, and there's a processing fee for credit cards [6]. Correcting a license costs $90.00, so check the spelling before you leave [6].
  • Who can apply here: Ohio residents who marry in Ohio apply in the county where either of you lives. Out-of-state residents can apply in Hamilton County only if they'll marry in Hamilton County [6].
  • How: you both appear in person, except in very limited cases, and each brings proof of age, such as a driver's license or passport. A divorce decree isn't required, but the court says bringing your most recent one helps. An online pre-application is optional. The license is valid for 60 calendar days, with no blood test or waiting period [6].
  • Where and when: the Marriage License Office is open Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–3:45 p.m.; 513.946.3589 [6]. The court is at 230 E. Ninth Street [29], which the Census Geocoder places in Cincinnati city [10].
  • Upcoming closures: the court's 2026 holiday list shows it closing at noon on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026 (Election Day), and closed on Nov. 11 (Veterans Day) and Nov. 26–27 (Thanksgiving) [29].
  • Copies: the first certified copy is included in the license fee [6]. More certified marriage records are $2.00 each. Ask in person at the court, or mail the court's form with a self-addressed, stamped envelope and a cashier's check payable to "Hamilton County Probate Court" (no personal checks or cash). The license must have been bought in Hamilton County and completed [30]. You can also request copies online, at the 9th-floor File Room or by fax at (513) 946-3565 [30].

Recorded documents (Hamilton County Recorder)

  • Recording fees: $34.00 for the first two pages and $8.00 for each additional page, for deeds, mortgages, leases, powers of attorney and similar documents. The page says all fees are set by the Ohio General Assembly [31]. The fee schedule doesn't show an effective date, so confirm the fee before you mail a document.
  • Copies: $0.10 a page if you make them yourself, or $2.00 a page by staff. A certified copy is $2.00 a page plus $1.00 per document for the certification seal [32][33][31].
  • Card fees: $2.00 for a debit card; for a credit card, $2.00 or 2.39% of the transaction, whichever is greater [32].
  • Where and when: 138 E. Court St., Room 205; (513) 946-4570. The office is open Monday–Friday 7:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m., and the recording desk closes at 3:30 p.m. [31][32]. The Additional Services page lists 8 a.m.–4 p.m. for certified copies [33]. The Census Geocoder places 138 E Court St in Cincinnati city [10].

Court records (Hamilton County Clerk of Courts)

  • Copy fees: the first 100 pages of public-record copies each month are free, then $0.10 a page. Certified copies are $1 a page and can't be sent electronically [34].
  • Where: 1000 Main St., Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m. [34], which the Census Geocoder places in Cincinnati city [10].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [36]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • No county shred events: Hamilton County ReSource says it doesn't hold paper shred events, but it lists one-day events open to all Hamilton County residents [37].
  • Past events: the two 2026 events on its list have already happened: Aug. 29, 2026, in Loveland, and Sept. 26, 2026, in Forest Park. As of Nov. 1, 2026, the page lists no upcoming events [37].
  • Shredded paper at the curb: the City of Cincinnati says to put recycling in the cart loose, except shredded paper, which goes in a clear plastic bag [38].
  • Paid shredding: ReSource says Office Depot, The UPS Store and Staples shred for a fee [37]. The UPS Store's Cincinnati page lists shredding; prices aren't listed online [11][12].

Questions

Where do I get a birth certificate in Cincinnati?

At the Cincinnati Health Department's vital records office, 1525 Elm Street, first floor, for $22 a copy. Orders placed in person or online can be picked up the same day [3][5].

How much is a marriage license in Cincinnati?

$75.00 at the Hamilton County Probate Court. The court takes cash and cards but not checks [6].

Can I get a passport without an appointment in Cincinnati?

Yes, at the Hamilton County Clerk of Courts, which doesn't need an appointment and also takes photos for $20 [7]. Library passport service is by appointment only [2].

Can I send a fax at the Cincinnati library?

Yes. Faxing is free at all library locations, with no page limit, and you don't need a library card [1].
