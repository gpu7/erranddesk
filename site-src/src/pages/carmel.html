Short version: The Carmel Clay Public Library prints and copies for 10 cents a page in black and white, but it has no fax machine [1]. The Carmel City Clerk notarizes for free, for Carmel residents only [2]. The Carmel Post Office is the only passport acceptance facility the State Department lists in Carmel [3]. Birth certificates cost $15 at the Hamilton County Health Department [4].
Hamilton County offices are in Noblesville: the Census places Carmel city entirely in Hamilton County [5]. The county health department, clerk and recorder are in Noblesville, and the Census Geocoder places all three in Noblesville city [6][7][8][9]. In Indiana, county clerks issue marriage licenses, county recorders record documents, and local health departments issue birth and death certificates (statewide) [10][11].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Carmel Clay Public Library (CCPL)

  • Printing: black and white is 10¢ a page and color is 25¢, on library paper only (no cardstock or label paper). You can print from library computers, or upload or email a job and pick it up at the Main Library's printing stations the same day; pay at the station with cash or a credit card [1]. You don't need a library card to use the computers [1].
  • Copying: black and white is 10¢ a page; color is 25¢ a page at the 2nd-floor copier only. The copiers take cash only [1].
  • Scanning: scanners on the Main Library's 1st and 2nd floors can email a scan or save it to a USB drive [1].
  • Fax: the library doesn't have a fax machine; it suggests scanning and emailing instead [1][12]. The addresses differ: the library's contact page says "Try the UPS Store at 484 E Carmel Drive" [12], while The UPS Store's own page for its Carmel Dr store gives 154 W Carmel Dr, Carmel, IN 46032 [15]. 484 E Carmel Drive isn't among the 3 Carmel locations The UPS Store lists [13]. Confirm the address with the store before you go.
  • Where: the Main Library is at 425 E Main St.; the West Branch is at 12770 Horseferry Rd. [14]. The Census Geocoder places the Main Library in Carmel city [9].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 3 locations in Carmel, at 4000 W 106th St, 154 W Carmel Dr and 1950 E Greyhound Pass. Its city page says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [13]. We didn't find prices: the W Carmel Dr store page doesn't list any [15], and The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [16]. The Census Geocoder places the 106th St and W Carmel Dr stores in Carmel city and the Greyhound Pass store in Westfield city [13][9]. The 154 W Carmel Dr store (#2587, (317) 574-0570) lists hours of Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. and Saturday 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.; it's closed Sunday [15].

Notary in Carmel

  • Carmel City Clerk (free, residents only): 1000 S Range Line Road, 317-571-2408. Notary hours are 9 to 11 a.m. and 1 to 3 p.m.; call before you go to make sure someone is available. You must be a Carmel resident and 18 or older. The service is no longer offered at Carmel City Hall [2]. The Census Geocoder places the office in Carmel city [9].
  • What the clerk won't notarize: land transaction documents and W-9 forms. Your document needs its own notary section or a separate Indiana Notarial Certificate, which you have to bring. All signers must be present with a valid photo ID, and bring any witnesses you need [2].
  • Library: the Carmel Clay Public Library says it doesn't offer notary service, and points to the Carmel City Clerk's office at 1000 S. Range Line Road for free notary public services [12] (residents only, per the clerk's page [2]).
  • The UPS Store: its Carmel page lists notarizing [13]. We didn't find prices.
  • How notaries are commissioned (statewide): in Indiana, notaries are commissioned by the Secretary of State for eight-year terms. Applicants pay a $5 processing fee, get a $25,000 assurance (bond) and complete the required education and exam [17].
  • Fees (statewide law): Indiana Code caps notary fees at "not more than ten dollars ($10) per signature" for acknowledgments, oaths and affirmations, witnessing a signature, verifications and certifying a copy. Fees for other acts are negotiable. A notary who charges must display a list of fees in advance. A travel fee is allowed up to the federal GSA travel rate. Public officials and their deputies may not charge for notarial acts done as part of official business [17].
  • Remote notarization (statewide law): a remote notary may charge "not more than twenty-five dollars ($25) for each remote notarial act" [17].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Hamilton County Health Department (birth and death certificates)

  • Where: 18030 Foundation Drive, Suite A, Noblesville; 317-776-8500 [4]. Birth and death certificates are issued Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. [6]; the department's office hours are 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. [20].
  • County fees: $15.00 per certified birth certificate (regular or wallet size) and $17.50 per certified death certificate. Online orders add a nonrefundable $5 service and credit card fee; standard shipping is free [4]. The $17.50 death certificate fee is $15 plus a $2.50 coroner fee [21].
  • State fees, side by side: the Indiana Department of Health lists $10.00 for a birth certificate and $8.00 for a death certificate (statewide) [11]. The county and the state list different prices; check with the office you order from.
  • How long: orders are processed within 2 business days once all information is available, and you can choose pickup or mail [6]. Mid-to-late August is busy, when birth certificates may take up to 10 days; for standard mail, allow 7–10 business days for processing and delivery [4].
  • By mail: send the application, a copy of your photo ID, a self-addressed stamped envelope and cash or a money order (no personal checks or credit cards) [22]. Card payments are for in-office orders only and add $1.00 plus 2% [22].
  • Hamilton County records only: the department has records only for births and deaths in Hamilton County [22][21].

Birth and death certificates (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): the Indiana Department of Health charges $10.00 for a birth certificate and $4.00 for each extra copy in the same order, and $8.00 for a death certificate and $4.00 for each extra copy. Fees aren't refunded and don't include third-party fees for online or phone orders [11]. Local health departments set their own fees; see the county fees above.
  • In person (statewide): the state office has no walk-in service. To buy a certificate in person, go to the local health department in the county where the birth or death happened [11][23].
  • By mail, online or by phone from the state (statewide): mail State Form 49607 (birth) or 49606 (death) with a check or money order payable to the Indiana Department of Health (no cash). Online orders go through VitalChek, and phone orders through (866) 601-0891, with third-party fees [11].
  • Processing times (statewide): the state lists 2–3 weeks for a standard birth certificate, 6–8 weeks for a long-form birth certificate and 60 days for a death certificate [11]. Its home page says to allow 2 weeks for an order to reach the office and 10–15 business days to process it [23]. The long-form birth certificate is only available from the state [24].
  • Who can order (statewide): you need a direct interest, a documented financial or legal interest, or a close family tie (parent, adult sibling or grandparent) to the person named on the record [24].

Marriage licenses (Hamilton County Clerk)

  • Marriage licenses (statewide): in Indiana, the county clerk issues marriage licenses. Both of you apply in person at the clerk's office in an Indiana county where one of you lives; couples from out of state apply in the county where they will marry. The fee is $25.00 if one or both of you live in Indiana and $65.00 if neither does. Some offices add a $4.00 document fee, and each certified copy is $4.00. Most counties require cash. The license is valid for 60 days, and you can marry the same day. You'll need ID and your Social Security number [10].
  • Copies (statewide): certified copies of marriage licenses come from the clerk of the county where the license was issued, not from the state health department [25].
  • Hamilton County Clerk: fill out the online application, then book an application meeting. Meetings are by appointment only, online or in person at the clerk's office in Noblesville, and both of you must attend the same meeting [7]. The office is at 1 Hamilton County Square, Suite 106, Noblesville; 317-776-9629; marriage applications 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Monday–Friday [7].
  • Fees: $25.00 for Indiana residents and $65.00 for out-of-state residents (valid only for ceremonies in Hamilton County), cash or credit card. Both include 2 certified copies. The license expires 60 days after it's issued [7].
  • Where to apply: if one of you lives in Hamilton County and the other in another Indiana county, you can apply in either county. If you both live out of state, apply in the county where you'll marry [26].

Recorded documents (Hamilton County Recorder)

  • Recording fees: $55.00 for mortgages and $25.00 for deeds and all other instruments (one oversize page included; $5.00 for each additional page larger than 8½ by 14 inches) [27].
  • Copies: $1.00 a page (11 by 17 inches or smaller), $5.00 a page if larger, and $5.00 to certify a document. The office takes cash, checks or credit cards; card payments add $0.40 plus 1.96% [27].
  • Deeds: have a deed reviewed by the Assessor's Office (2nd floor) and transferred by the Auditor's Office (1st floor) before you take it to the Recorder on the 3rd floor. The recording deadline is 4:15 p.m. (except mechanic's liens) [8].
  • Where: 33 N 9th Street, Suite 309, Noblesville; 317-776-9618. Staff won't search records for you, but you can use the office's public computers or search online [8].

Court records (Carmel City Court and Hamilton County Clerk)

  • Hamilton County: the Clerk's Office provides copies of documents related to immigration, marriage, divorce, expungements, estates, wills and criminal records through an online request form. It says there are small fees outlined in the form and that "Actual fees will be provided at the time of payment." We didn't find a court-record copy fee on the Hamilton County Clerk's pages we checked [32].
  • Carmel City Court: 1000 S Rangeline Rd; 317-571-2440; Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. We didn't find a copy or certified-copy fee on its page [31].
  • City public records (not court records): the City of Carmel's public records page says copies are available "normally at a cost of 0.10 cents per page" (as published), with higher charges for oversized records, electronic discs and the like [33].

Apostilles and authentications (statewide)

  • Fee (statewide): $2.00 per document from the Indiana Secretary of State. There's no fee for birth and death certificates, adoption documents, or student transcripts and diplomas [28][17]. Pay in person by credit, debit, check or money order, or by mail with a check or money order [28].
  • One certificate (statewide): since July 1, 2022, Indiana uses a single certificate format and no longer tells apostilles and authentications apart. The certificates can't be used inside the United States [28].
  • What it can authenticate (statewide): Indiana vital records only; marriage, divorce, death and birth certificates don't need notarization, but other documents do. A marriage license must first be certified by the current circuit court clerk of the county where the marriage took place. Federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State [28].
  • Where (statewide): there's one office, at 302 W. Washington St., Room E-018, Indianapolis, IN 46204, with no branches. With an appointment it processes up to 10 documents while you wait; without one, up to 5. Phone (317) 234-9768 [28].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [29]. Other sellers: money order guide.

  • Carmel Community Shred-It & Electronics Recycling Day (past): Saturday, August 8, 2026, 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., at Creekside Middle School, 3525 West 126th Street. It was hosted by Crime Stoppers of Central Indiana with the Carmel Police Department, Carmel Utilities and others. Carmel Utilities customers could use the free bulk item drop-off with identification showing proof of Carmel residency; yard waste, household hazardous waste, ammunition and items containing freon weren't accepted there. TV and computer screens cost $20 each in the electronics line. Questions: Carmel Police Department, 317-571-2500 [30]. The event has passed. We didn't find a later 2026 event: Carmel Utilities' site shows only this one and a past Nov. 8, 2025 event, and the city's site describes Crime Stoppers Shred-It Day as "twice per year" without a fall 2026 date [34][35].
  • The UPS Store: its Carmel page lists shredding [13]. We didn't find prices.

Questions

Where can I get something notarized for free in Carmel?

At the Carmel City Clerk's office, 1000 S Range Line Road, if you're a Carmel resident 18 or older; call 317-571-2408 first [2].

Can I send a fax at the Carmel library?

No. The Carmel Clay Public Library doesn't have a fax machine, but you can scan and email documents there [12].

Where do I get a birth certificate in Carmel?

From the Hamilton County Health Department in Noblesville, online, by mail or in person: $15.00 per copy [4].

How much is a marriage license in Carmel?

$25.00 for Indiana residents or $65.00 for out-of-state residents at the Hamilton County Clerk, by appointment [7].

How much can an Indiana notary charge?

Up to $10 per signature for common acts such as acknowledgments and oaths, and up to $25 for a remote notarial act (statewide law) [17].
