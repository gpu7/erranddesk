Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Milwaukee Public Library (MPL)

  • Where: MPL says printing, copying, scanning and fax are offered at all branch locations [1]. The Central Library is at 814 W. Wisconsin Ave. [1], and the Census Geocoder places it inside Milwaukee city [3].
  • Printing and copying: black and white is $0.15 a page ($0.30 double-sided) and color is $0.50 a page ($1.00 double-sided). Pay with cash, coins or a credit or debit card at the print kiosk; card transactions may carry convenience fees [1].
  • Print from your phone or laptop: MPL uses ePRINTit. Email a file to BW@MPL.ORG for black and white or COLOR@MPL.ORG for color, or use the ePRINTit portal or ePRINTit SaaS app, then release the job at a kiosk. Submitted files are deleted after 24 hours [1].
  • Scanning: free. Scan up to 40 pages a minute to a flash drive, a Google documents folder or email, as PDF, Word or JPEG files [1].
  • Fax: outgoing only; incoming faxes aren't permitted. In the U.S. it's $1.75 for the first page and $1.00 for each additional page; international is $3.95 and $3.45. You can check or resend a fax at faxscan24.com [1].
  • How to pay for fax: the page's summary says fax works with "a credit card, debit card, or check card", but the fax section says the service is "credit card only" (Visa, MasterCard, American Express and Discover). Confirm with the library [1].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 3 locations with Milwaukee addresses: 1345 N Jefferson St, (414) 273-7780; 1314 S 1st St, (414) 897-7713; and 3900 W Brown Deer Rd, Ste. A, (414) 354-3393. Its city page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [6]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [7].
  • Inside the city: the Census Geocoder places 1345 N Jefferson St and 1314 S 1st St inside Milwaukee city, and places 3900 W Brown Deer Rd in the village of Brown Deer [6][3].

Notary in Milwaukee

  • Library: we didn't find notary service on MPL's print, scan and fax page [1].
  • The UPS Store: its Milwaukee page lists notarizing [6].
  • Fees (statewide): Wisconsin's Department of Financial Institutions (DFI) says a notary "may charge no more than $5 for most in-person notarial acts." Ask for the price before the notary starts [8].
  • Statewide commission: DFI says a Wisconsin commission lets a notary act in all counties in the state [8].
  • No copies of vital records (statewide): DFI tells notaries they are "strictly prohibited" from making copies, certified or uncertified, of birth, death, divorce, marriage and other vital records. Get certified copies from a state or county vital records office instead [8].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth, death, marriage and divorce records (statewide rules)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS) says Wisconsin birth, death, marriage and divorce records from October 1907 to now are available from the Wisconsin Vital Records Office, and also through any Wisconsin county Register of Deeds office. Availability depends on the event type and date [4].
  • Fees (statewide): $20 for the first copy of each record and $3 for each additional copy of the same record [4]. State law sets the same $20 and $3 fees [11]. DHS says the long form birth certificate doesn't cost extra; write "Long Form" on the application [4].
  • Who can get a certified copy: state law limits who can get one; an uncertified copy is printed on plain paper and stamped as not usable for legal or identification purposes [4].
  • State office by mail: Wisconsin Vital Records Office, PO Box 309, Madison, WI 53701. It takes about 10 business days after the office gets your order, plus mail time. Pay by cash, check or money order in U.S. funds [4].
  • Online and phone: through VitalChek, online or at 877-885-2981; there is a fee for using VitalChek, and requests take about five business days [4].
  • Milwaukee City Health Office: DHS says that for Wisconsin birth and death records you can also contact the Milwaukee City Health Office [4], one of two city health offices that act as local vital records offices [12]. The City of Milwaukee's certificate pages blocked our check on Oct 31, 2026, so we didn't find the office's address, hours or payment rules on an official page.
  • Milwaukee County Register of Deeds: county Register of Deeds offices also issue Wisconsin vital records [4]. Milwaukee County's website blocked our check on Oct 31, 2026, so we didn't find the office's address, hours or payment rules on an official page.

Marriage licenses

  • Marriage licenses (statewide law): in Wisconsin the county clerk issues marriage licenses. A license can't be issued within 3 days of the application unless the clerk, at its discretion, issues it sooner for an added fee of up to $25. Each applicant must show a certified copy of a birth record, or one of the other documents the law allows, and swear to the application before the clerk. State law sets the county clerk's fee for each license at $49.50; that is the statutory clerk fee, not necessarily the full price a county charges [13].
  • Milwaukee County Clerk: we couldn't read the County Clerk's marriage license page (the county website blocked our check on Oct 31, 2026), so we didn't find the county's total license price, hours or appointment rules. Confirm with the County Clerk at 414-278-4067, the number the passport locator lists for the office [2].

Recorded documents and court copies

  • Recording fees (statewide law): Wisconsin law sets the register of deeds fee for recording a document at $30. Copies of recorded documents are $2 for the first page and $1 for each additional page, plus $1 to certify a copy [14].
  • Court copies (statewide fee table): the Wisconsin court system lists Clerk of Circuit Court copies at $1.25 a page, and certified copies at $5 to certify the document plus $1.25 a page [15].
  • Milwaukee County offices: we didn't find the Milwaukee County Register of Deeds' or Clerk of Circuit Court's own fee pages; the county website blocked our check on Oct 31, 2026.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Two state offices: both the Wisconsin Secretary of State (SOS) and DFI can issue apostilles for Hague Convention countries. Authentications for countries outside the Hague Convention come only from the SOS [16].
  • DFI: standard service is $10 per document (3–7 business days) and expedited is $35 per document (1–2 business days), not counting mail time. DFI, 4822 Madison Yards Way, North Tower, Madison, WI 53705; (608) 266-8915 [17].
  • Secretary of State: standard service is $10 per document (an estimated 7–20 days from when the office gets the documents) and expedited is $35 per document (2–4 business days). Walk-in expedited service is generally available the same day at the office on the basement level of the State Capitol in Madison. It accepts card, cash, check or money order [16].
  • Notarized documents: the SOS accepts only wet-ink stamped or embossed notarizations by a Wisconsin notary, not electronic ones. It can't process federal documents or vital records from another state or country [16].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [18]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

How much is printing at the Milwaukee library?

$0.15 a page for black and white and $0.50 for color [1].

Can I receive a fax at the library?

No. MPL says incoming faxes are not permitted [1].

Is scanning free at MPL?

Yes. MPL says there is no charge for scanning [1].

How much is a birth certificate in Wisconsin?

$20 for the first copy and $3 for each additional copy of the same record (statewide fee) [4].

How much can a Wisconsin notary charge?

No more than $5 for most in-person notarial acts (statewide) [8].

Where do I get an apostille?

From either the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions or the Wisconsin Secretary of State, both in Madison: $10 per document standard or $35 expedited (statewide) [17][16].
