Houston, Texas document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Houston, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

This page is general information. Hours and fees change, so call or check the source before you go.
Which offices serve Houston: the city's public library is the Houston Public Library [1]. Birth and death certificates for births and deaths inside the city come from the Houston Health Department's Bureau of Vital Statistics [2]; the Harris County Clerk issues them for the rest of the county, and also issues marriage licenses and records deeds [3][4]. The Harris County District Clerk sells copies of court records and accepts passport applications [5][6]. Apostilles come from the Texas Secretary of State in Austin [7]. Most of Houston is in Harris County, with parts in Fort Bend and Montgomery counties; the Health Department points those parts to their own county clerks [2]. This guide covers Harris County offices.
Short version: Houston Public Library prints are $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color, card or phone payment only, scanning to USB is free, and the library doesn't offer fax [1]. The passport locator lists 38 Houston facilities, including post offices, libraries, the District Clerk and the Municipal Courts [8]. The Harris County Law Library notarizes for free, with limits [9]. A Harris County marriage license is $81 [3]. Texas notary fees are capped per signature (statewide) [10], and apostilles are $15 at the Secretary of State [11].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Houston Public Library

  • Prints and copies: $0.15 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color. Payment is cashless only, by credit card or Apple Pay or Google Pay; a card payment puts a $5 hold on the card, with a $1 minimum [1].
  • Scanning: free scanning to a USB drive from all public printers. SimpleScan stations (free scan to USB, print or email) are at the Central Library and 15 branches: Alief-Henington, Collier, Clayton (FHRC), Flores, Johnson, Jungman, Kendall, McGovern-Stella Link, Oak Forest, Park Place, Robinson-Westchase, Scenic Woods, TECHLink Dixon, Vinson and Young [1].
  • Fax: the library says fax services are not available at this time [1].
  • Document delivery: $2 per request plus $1 a page [12].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 39 locations in Houston; see its city page for addresses and phone numbers. That page says they offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [13]. We didn't find prices on the store pages; The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [14].

See printing prices and fax prices.

Notary in Houston

  • Harris County Law Library (free): notary service is free at the front desk, Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m., at 1019 Congress Street, 1st Floor. It doesn't notarize foreign documents, real property documents, financial instruments, trusts or documents to be recorded [9].
  • City of Houston Municipal Courts: public notary service at the Herbert W. Gee Municipal Courthouse, 1400 Lubbock Street, Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–10:00 p.m. and Saturday 8:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m. The city says fees vary between $4 and $10 for basic one-signature notary services, and some documents add $1 fees; it takes cash, check, money order or American Express, Discover, Mastercard or Visa. Phone 713.247.5479 [15].
  • Library: we didn't find notary service on the Houston Public Library pages we checked.
  • The UPS Store: its Houston page lists notarizing [13]. We didn't find store notary prices; the Texas maximums are below.
  • Fee limits (statewide): Government Code § 406.024 lets a Texas notary charge up to $10 for taking an acknowledgment or proof of a deed or other instrument for the first signature and $1 for each additional signature, $10 for administering an oath with a certificate and seal, $10 for a certificate under seal not otherwise provided for, $1 a page for a copy of a record, and $10 for a notarial act not otherwise provided for. The section says the Secretary of State adjusts these amounts for inflation every five years; we didn't find a published adjusted schedule [10]. The Secretary of State says a notary may charge less or nothing, and an employer may decide what an employee notary charges [16].
  • Online notarization (statewide): under § 406.111, an online notary may charge up to $25 for an online notarization on top of any other fee allowed [10].
  • What notaries can't do (statewide): a Texas notary can't certify a copy of a birth certificate or a marriage license, and a notary who isn't a lawyer can't advertise as a "notario publico" [16][10].

A $35 acceptance fee is paid separately to the acceptance facility, and expedited service is $60 more [19].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Texas Vital Statistics (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): Texas Vital Statistics (DSHS) charges $22 for a birth certificate (long or short form), $60 for an heirloom birth certificate, $20 for the first death certificate and $3 for each additional copy, and $20 to verify a marriage or divorce. Expedited processing is $25 and is shipped overnight. Paying by card in person in Austin adds $2.25 [20].
  • By mail (statewide): send a notarized application, a copy of your ID and a check or money order [21] to Texas Vital Statistics, Department of State Health Services, P.O. Box 12040, Austin, TX 78711-2040. Its phone number is 888-963-7111, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m. [22].
  • Local offices (statewide): local registrars and county clerks also issue certificates and set their own fees and hours [23]. The local offices for this city are below.

Birth and death certificates: Houston Health Department

  • Where: Bureau of Vital Statistics, 8000 North Stadium Drive, Houston, 832-393-4220. Walk-ins Monday–Thursday 8 a.m.–4 p.m., arriving by 3 p.m.; no walk-ins on Friday. Pay by cash, money order, or debit or credit card (no American Express); checks aren't accepted. Parking is $7 [2].
  • Fees: the department's online order site lists $23 for a birth certificate and $21 for the first death certificate and $4 for each additional copy. Mail delivery is $3 (4–6 weeks) and same-day pickup is $15, plus a $6 online service fee [24].

Birth and death certificates: Harris County Clerk

  • Fees: $23 for a birth certificate (short form for any Texas birth, long form if the record is on file) and $21 for the first death certificate and $4 for each additional copy. The County Clerk's own records cover births and deaths outside the city limits of Houston, Baytown, La Porte, South Houston and Jacinto City and Justice of the Peace Precincts 3 and 4 [3].
  • By mail: include a notarized copy of your ID and a cashier's check or money order payable to Harris County Clerk. Vital Statistics: 713-274-8690 [3].

Marriage licenses: Harris County Clerk

  • Fee: $81, or $181 if neither applicant shows Texas residency on their ID. A Twogether in Texas premarital course certificate takes up to $60 off and waives the 72-hour waiting period [3].
  • How: both applicants apply in person at any of the County Clerk's 11 locations; a military ID also waives the wait. The officiant must return the license within 90 days. An informal marriage (declaration) is $46, and certified copies are $6 [3]. All 11 locations are open Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–4:30 p.m.; the downtown office is 201 Caroline St., 3rd Floor, (713) 274-8600 [25].
  • Waiting period and course (statewide): Texas has a 72-hour wait between issuing a license and the ceremony. It doesn't apply to active-duty military or Department of Defense employees, if a judge waives it, or if you completed a premarital education course within the past year. A license expires if the ceremony isn't held within 90 days [26]. The state's "Twogether in Texas" course page didn't load for us (hhs.texas.gov/services/health/twogether-texas), so we didn't confirm the course details there.

Recorded documents: Harris County Clerk

  • Recording fees: the County Clerk lists $25 for the first page and $4 for each additional page under Local Government Code § 118.011, plus $0.25 for each name over five [4].
  • Copies: plain copies are $1 a page; certified copies are $5 plus $1 a page [4][27]. Electronic copies of public records are $1 for 1–10 pages and $0.10 a page after that, and a search is $5. A certified copy of a marriage license is $7 and a plain copy $2 [27].
  • Payment: cash, credit card, money order or business check [4].

Court records: Harris County District Clerk

  • Fees: in person, $1.00 a page plus $5.00 for certification and seal. Online, paper copies are $1 a page and electronic copies $1 for 1–10 pages and $0.10 a page after that; certification and seal are $5 per document. A $5 search fee applies if you don't give the case information [5].

District clerk copy fees (statewide)

  • Certification (statewide): Government Code § 51.318 sets the district clerk fee for a certified copy, including the certificate and seal, at $5, and § 51.606 says no fee may be charged just to look at records [28]. Per-page copy fees are on the county page above.

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who: the Texas Secretary of State is the only office that issues apostilles for Texas public documents. It doesn't apostille federal documents, and it doesn't apostille documents meant for use inside the United States [29][7].
  • Fee: $15 per apostille, or $10 per document for adoption-related documents, with a $100 maximum [11].
  • In person in Austin: Authentications Unit, 400 West 15th Street, Austin, (512) 463-5705, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Same-day service is limited to 10 documents. Tuesday–Thursday are by appointment; Monday and Friday are walk-in, with waits that can be over an hour. More than 10 documents go in the drop box, with about a 72-hour turnaround. Pay by check, money order, card or exact cash [7].
  • By mail: about 2 weeks. Send Form 2102 naming the country, with a check or money order payable to the Office of the Texas Secretary of State. Certified vital records must have been issued within the past 5 years, and photocopies aren't apostilled [7]. See the apostille guide.

More on certified copies: how to get a certified copy.

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [30]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • City events: the City of Houston's document shredding initiative page says the initiative has ended and no more events are scheduled [31]. We didn't find an upcoming free document shred event on the other official pages we checked.
  • The UPS Store: its Houston page lists shredding [13]. We didn't find prices.

Questions

Can I fax at the library in Houston?

No. The Houston Public Library says fax services are not available at this time [1].

How much does printing cost at the library?

$0.15 a page in black and white and $0.50 in color, paid by card or phone; scanning to USB is free [1].

Where can I get something notarized for free in Houston?

The Harris County Law Library notarizes for free at its front desk, but not real property, financial or recordable documents [9].

Where can I apply for a passport in Houston?

The locator lists 38 Houston facilities, including post offices, six Houston Public Library branches and the Harris County District Clerk, which takes walk-ins at 5900 Canal Street [8][17][6].

How much is a marriage license in Harris County?

$81, or $181 if neither applicant shows Texas residency on their ID; a Twogether in Texas certificate takes up to $60 off [3].

Where do I get a Houston birth certificate?

For births inside Houston city limits, from the Houston Health Department, 8000 North Stadium Drive, or its online order site; a birth certificate is $23 [2][24].

Where do I get an apostille?

From the Texas Secretary of State in Austin, in person or by mail, for $15 per apostille (statewide) [7][11].
