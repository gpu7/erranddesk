Notary in Grand Junction

  • Colorado fee limit (statewide): "the maximum fee allowed for a notarization is $15 per document." The maximum for an electronic or remote notarization is $25. Notaries must tell you all charges before the notarial act [14].
  • Banks (national policy): Bank of America says notary service is available "at no cost to our customers" and "in many of our financial centers." Schedule an appointment. All signers must be present [15]. For other banks, see free notary options.
  • The UPS Store (example): its Grand Junction page lists notarizing among its services, without prices [19]. Under state law the fee can't exceed $15 per document in person [14].
  • Recording a notarized document: the Mesa County Clerk and Recorder says the entire document must be completed and signed before a notary (if required) before you come in to record it [6].
  • Library, county and city: we didn't find a public notary service on the Mesa County Libraries, Mesa County Clerk and Recorder or City of Grand Junction City Clerk pages we checked.

Birth, death and recorded-document copies

Birth and death certificates: Mesa County Public Health

  • Where: Birth and Death Certificate Counter, Community Services Building (Public Health), 510 29 1/2 Road, Grand Junction, CO 81504, 970-248-6900. Certificate hours are Monday–Thursday 7:30 a.m.–5 p.m. and Friday 7:30 a.m.–noon. Certificate staff: 970-683-6658 [9].
  • Fees: $25.00 for the first copy, and $20.00 for each additional copy of the same record bought at the same time. Starting June 1, 2026, a processing fee is added to all credit and debit card payments [9]. The Colorado Office of the State Registrar of Vital Statistics sets the fee schedule statewide [10].
  • Which records: birth certificates for all Colorado counties. Death certificates for deaths in Mesa County from 1910 to present, and death records for the whole state from 1975 to present [9].
  • Who and what to bring: the registrant, parents, siblings, spouses and people with a tangible interest. Current ID is required, and proof of relationship may be required [9].
  • Other ways to order: online through VitalChek (an additional fee is charged), or by email or mail with a copy of your ID. Mail goes to P.O. Box 20,000, Department 5033, Grand Junction, CO 81502-5001 [9].

Recorded documents: Mesa County Clerk and Recorder

  • Where: Central Services (Recording), 200 S. Spruce Street, Grand Junction, CO 81501. Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4:30 p.m. [6].
  • Recording: a flat $43.00 per document regardless of page count, with no fee for official death records. The chart is dated July 23, 2026 [8].
  • Copies: in-person copies of recorded documents are $.25 per page, and certification is $1.00 per document. Extra charges apply for mailing or faxing copies [7]. The fee chart lists a non-certified copy at $.25 and a certified copy at $1.25 [8].
  • Searching: recorded documents are free to search, view and print online after you create an account [7].

Money orders (national USPS fees)

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [16]. Mesa County Public Health accepts a check or money order with mailed or emailed certificate requests [9]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Questions

Where can I apply for a passport in Grand Junction?

The State Department locator lists the Grand Junction Main Post Office, 241 N. 4th St., with passport hours by appointment. Book at usps.com/scheduler [12].

How much does printing cost at the library in Grand Junction?

Mesa County Libraries charges 15 cents per side for black and white and 70 cents per side for color. Color is at Central, Clifton or Fruita only [1].

Is faxing free at the library in Grand Junction?

Yes, online through Got Free Fax, up to 10 pages per fax and 5 times per day [1].

Where can I get a birth certificate in Grand Junction?

At Mesa County Public Health, 510 29 1/2 Road, Monday–Thursday 7:30 a.m.–5 p.m. and Friday 7:30 a.m.–noon. The first copy is $25 [9].
