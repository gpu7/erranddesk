Burlington, Vermont document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Where to print, fax, notarize, apply for a passport and get certified copies in Burlington, using only official and provider pages. Each fact links to its source. Some facts are statewide or national rather than local. We label those.

Short version: the locator lists 2 passport acceptance facilities in Burlington (post office and Chittenden Superior Court) [1]. City Clerk/Treasurer staff notarize free of charge (limit 3 signatures or 3 documents per day) [2]. Certified vital copies are $10 at the clerk or at VDH ($2 online processing fee at VDH) [3][4][5]. Vermont has no statutory dollar cap we could find on ordinary notary customer fees [6].
Counties in Vermont (statewide): Vermont has 14 counties. Land records are kept by each town or city clerk — there is no county registry of deeds. Vital records are issued by town and city clerks and by the Vermont Department of Health Vital Records Office [4][7][8].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

  • Fletcher Free Library (235 College Street; New North End Branch 1127 North Avenue): black-and-white printing is free up to 10 pages per day (donation requested over 10; cash only). Color printing is $0.25 per sheet. Copier: $0.15 single-sided black-and-white, $0.20 single-sided color, $0.25 double-sided. Scan and fax are free [9].
  • FedEx Office Print & Ship Center: 199 Main St. Lists self-service copying, faxing, scanning, printing, passport photos and shredding [10][11].
  • FedEx at Walgreens: FedEx's Burlington page also lists 1024 North Ave [11].
  • The UPS Store: we didn't find a The UPS Store in Burlington, VT on The UPS Store find-a-store search (Burlington / 05401, last checked Nov 5, 2026); the Burlington city locator URL also 404s [12]. The nearest official city listing is South Burlington at 150 Dorset St Ste 245, The Blue Mall ((802) 860-7428; Mon–Fri 8–6:30 Sat 9–3 Sun 10–3), which advertises printing, faxing, shredding and notarizing [13][14].

Notary in Burlington

  • City Clerk/Treasurer (free): 149 Church Street. Staff notarize documents free of charge. Bring two pieces of identification; do not sign until you are in front of the notary. Limits: no witnesses provided, no ID notarization, no sound-mind attestations, and a daily cap of 3 signatures or 3 documents (whichever is less). Hours Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–1:00 p.m. and 1:30–4:30 p.m. (closed 10:15–11:45 a.m. on the third Thursday for staff meeting) [2][15].
  • The UPS Store (nearby South Burlington): 150 Dorset St Ste 245 advertises notary service; fees vary by location. Vermont sets a $30 commission fee for notaries but we didn't find a statutory cap on customer notarial-act charges [13][16][17][6].
  • Notary fees (statewide): Vermont's notary chapter sets a $30 commission fee for becoming a notary public (26 V.S.A. § 5324). We didn't find a statutory dollar cap on what a Vermont notary may charge a customer for an ordinary notarial act in Chapter 103 (last checked Nov 5, 2026). Remote and electronic notarial acts need a special endorsement from the Office of Professional Regulation [17][6][18].

Birth, death, marriage and land record copies

Burlington City Clerk/Treasurer (vital records)

  • Where and when: 149 Church Street. Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–1:00 p.m. and 1:30–4:30 p.m. Phone 802-865-7000. In person, by mail, or online request forms [15][3].
  • Fees: certified vital record certificates are $10.00; non-certified copies are $2.00. Checks payable to "City of Burlington." Mail requests generally process in 2–4 business days and need a self-addressed stamped envelope plus ID per the form [3]. Statewide VDH certified copies are also $10 (plus $2 online) [4][5].

Birth, death and marriage copies (statewide rules)

  • Town or city clerk (statewide): Vermont town and city clerks issue certified copies of vital records; since 2019 clerks can print eligible Vermont birth and death records filed anywhere in the state [4].
  • Vermont Department of Health Vital Records (statewide): 280 State Drive, Waterbury, VT 05671-8300. Certified copies with raised seal are $10 each; online orders add a $2 processing fee [4][5].
  • Need an apostille? (statewide): get a certified (or notarized, as required) copy from a clerk or VDH, then submit it to the Vermont State Archives & Records Administration for an apostille [22][4].

Marriage licenses (Burlington City Clerk)

  • Where: 149 Church Street. Appointments every half hour 8:00 a.m.–3:30 p.m.; walk-ins if no appointment is booked. At least one party must appear with state or federal ID; both must sign the application [23][15].
  • Fee: $80.00; cash, check, or credit card (2.65% card processing fee) [23].
  • Waiting period: none; the license is valid for 60 days [23][24].
  • State rules (statewide): obtain a marriage license from any Vermont town or city clerk. The license costs $80, there is no waiting period, and it is valid for 60 days. Both parties must sign the application; at least one must sign in front of the clerk. You must be 18 or older [24].

Land records (Burlington City Clerk)

  • Office: 149 Church Street. Vermont records land by town or city — there is no county recording system. Burlington records only Burlington property (South Burlington is separate) [25][7].
  • Recording: $15 per page for documents recorded; mylar maps $25 per map (18×24 inches). Documents that affect more than one previously recorded instrument are $15 for each prior instrument. Property Transfer Tax Return filing $15 [25].
  • Copies: $1 per page; certified copies are $1 per page plus $10 for the certification (per document) on the City's Land Records page — different from the statewide statute's $10.00 per page certified-copy wording, so confirm at the counter [25][8].
  • Recording fees (statewide statute): 32 V.S.A. § 1671 sets $15.00 per page to record most documents at the town or city clerk; certified copies of recorded documents are $10.00 per page; uncertified copies are $1.00 per page (minimum $2.00). A property transfer tax return filing is $15.00; survey plats are $25.00 per sheet size listed in the statute. Vermont records land by town or city — there is no county recording system [8].

Apostilles (statewide)

  • Who issues them (statewide): the Vermont State Archives & Records Administration (VSARA) on behalf of the Secretary of State. Physical/drop-box address: 1078 U.S. Route 2, Middlesex; mailing address 1078 U.S. Route 2 - Middlesex, Montpelier, VT 05633-7701 [22].
  • Fee (statewide): $10 per apostille or authentication. Do not send cash by mail; credit and debit cards are not accepted. Checks or money orders payable to the VT Secretary of State, in U.S. dollars [22].
  • Mail (statewide): include the VSARA-35 request form, payment, and documents; preliminary review before submitting is encouraged [22].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [26]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • The UPS Store (South Burlington): the South Burlington listing advertises shredding [13].

Questions

Where can I get a free notary in Burlington?

City Clerk/Treasurer, 149 Church Street. Free notary; two IDs; limit 3 signatures or 3 documents per day; no witnesses provided [2].

How much is a birth certificate in Burlington?

Certified copies are $10.00 at the City Clerk; non-certified $2.00 [3]. VDH statewide is also $10 certified plus $2 online [4][5].

How much can a Vermont notary charge?

26 V.S.A. § 5324 sets a $30 commission fee for becoming a notary. We didn't find a statutory dollar cap on customer charges for ordinary notarial acts in Chapter 103 (last checked Nov 5, 2026) [17][6].

Can I get an apostille on a Burlington birth certificate?

Get a certified copy from the City Clerk or VDH, then submit it to VSARA (1078 U.S. Route 2, Middlesex / Montpelier mailing address). Apostille is $10 per document; check or money order to VT Secretary of State; no cash or cards by mail [22].

Where do I record a deed for Burlington property?

Burlington City Clerk land records, 149 Church Street. Recording is $15 per page (statewide statute and City page). Vermont has no county registry of deeds [25][8].
