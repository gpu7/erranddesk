Evansville, Indiana document errands: printing, fax, notary, passports and certified copies (2026)

Short version: The Evansville Vanderburgh Public Library offers free notary service at six locations and passport applications at EVPL Central [1][2]. Library printing is 15 cents a page in black and white, and a domestic fax is $1.75 for the first page [3]. Birth certificates cost $16 at the Vanderburgh County Health Department, with same-day walk-in service [4]. Marriage licenses cost $25 or $65 at the Vanderburgh County Clerk [5].
One county: the Census places Evansville city entirely in Vanderburgh County [6], so the county offices on this page are Vanderburgh County offices. In Indiana, county clerks issue marriage licenses, county recorders record documents, and local health departments issue birth and death certificates (statewide) [7][8].

Printing, copying, scanning and fax

Evansville Vanderburgh Public Library (EVPL)

  • Printing: available at all eight locations, from library computers, the ePRINTit app, email or a web browser. Black and white is $0.15 a page and color is $0.50, on EVPL letter-size paper only [3].
  • Copying: black and white at every location for $0.10 a page; color for $0.25 a page at EVPL Central, McCollough, North Park and Red Bank [3].
  • Fax: self-service at all locations except Red Bank (currently unavailable there). Domestic: $1.75 for the first page and $1.00 for each additional page. International: $3.95 and $3.45. Pay by debit or credit card or cash and coins [3]. The Board of Trustees fee schedule dated March 12, 2026 lists the same domestic fax prices [9].
  • Scanning: free at all locations except Red Bank (currently unavailable there) [3].
  • Where: EVPL Central is at 200 SE Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd. [2]. The Census Geocoder places it in Evansville city [10].
  • The UPS Store: it lists 2 locations in Evansville, at 5625 Pearl Dr and 5444 E Indiana St. Its city page says its stores offer packing, shipping, printing, shredding, notarizing, faxing and mailbox services [11]. We didn't find prices: the E Indiana St store page doesn't list any [12], and The UPS Store's site says products, services, prices and hours may vary by location [13]. The Census Geocoder places the E Indiana St store in Evansville city; it places 5625 Pearl Dr in Vanderburgh County outside any city or town [11][10].

Notary in Evansville

  • EVPL (free): notary service at no charge at EVPL Central, McCollough, North Park, Oaklyn, Stringtown and West; you don't need a library card. Drop-in only at Central (9:00–11:00 a.m. Tuesdays and 3:00–5:00 p.m. Thursdays, second-floor information desk) and West (3:00–5:00 p.m. Mondays). At McCollough, North Park, Oaklyn and Stringtown, request an appointment three days ahead or call the location [1].
  • EVPL rules: sign in front of the notary, bring the whole document, any witnesses and a current photo ID (or one expired within the past 3 years). Library notaries won't notarize documents not in English, I-9 forms, real estate transactions, auto titles, corporate or business documents, or depositions, among others [1].
  • The UPS Store: its Evansville page lists notarizing [11]. We didn't find prices.
  • How notaries are commissioned (statewide): in Indiana, notaries are commissioned by the Secretary of State for eight-year terms. Applicants pay a $5 processing fee, get a $25,000 assurance (bond) and complete the required education and exam [14].
  • Fees (statewide law): Indiana Code caps notary fees at "not more than ten dollars ($10) per signature" for acknowledgments, oaths and affirmations, witnessing a signature, verifications and certifying a copy. Fees for other acts are negotiable. A notary who charges must display a list of fees in advance. A travel fee is allowed up to the federal GSA travel rate. Public officials and their deputies may not charge for notarial acts done as part of official business [14].
  • Remote notarization (statewide law): a remote notary may charge "not more than twenty-five dollars ($25) for each remote notarial act" [14].

Birth, death, marriage, court and recorded-document copies

Vanderburgh County Health Department (birth and death certificates)

  • Where: same-day walk-in service at the Vital Records Division, 420 Mulberry Street. The birth and death pages list hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday and 8:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. Wednesday; the site header lists 8:00 am to 4:30 pm [4][18]. The sources differ; call (812) 435-2400 before you go [4]. The Census Geocoder places the office in Evansville city [10].
  • County fees: $16.00 per certified birth certificate [4]. $20.00 per certified death certificate if the person who died was a Vanderburgh County resident, $25.00 if not, and $18.00 for a non-certified copy, which can't be used for legal purposes [18].
  • State fees, side by side: the Indiana Department of Health lists $10.00 for a birth certificate and $8.00 for a death certificate (statewide) [8]. The county and the state list different prices; check with the office you order from.
  • How to pay: in person: cash, check, U.S. money order, debit or credit card, Apple Pay or Cash App. By mail: check or U.S. money order (no cash). Online: debit or credit card [4].
  • By mail: use the online form and choose mail delivery; you get an emailed invoice once the record is found [4]. The office says it turns mail requests around in 3–5 business days and mailing takes 2–3 weeks [19].
  • Other counties: the office says it can issue birth certificates from other Indiana counties [19].
  • ID and eligibility: bring one item from the primary ID list or two from the secondary list, and you must have a "direct interest" in the record [19].

Birth and death certificates (statewide)

  • State fees (statewide): the Indiana Department of Health charges $10.00 for a birth certificate and $4.00 for each extra copy in the same order, and $8.00 for a death certificate and $4.00 for each extra copy. Fees aren't refunded and don't include third-party fees for online or phone orders [8]. Local health departments set their own fees; see the county fees above.
  • In person (statewide): the state office has no walk-in service. To buy a certificate in person, go to the local health department in the county where the birth or death happened [8][20].
  • By mail, online or by phone from the state (statewide): mail State Form 49607 (birth) or 49606 (death) with a check or money order payable to the Indiana Department of Health (no cash). Online orders go through VitalChek, and phone orders through (866) 601-0891, with third-party fees [8].
  • Processing times (statewide): the state lists 2–3 weeks for a standard birth certificate, 6–8 weeks for a long-form birth certificate and 60 days for a death certificate [8]. Its home page says to allow 2 weeks for an order to reach the office and 10–15 business days to process it [20]. The long-form birth certificate is only available from the state [21].
  • Who can order (statewide): you need a direct interest, a documented financial or legal interest, or a close family tie (parent, adult sibling or grandparent) to the person named on the record [21].

Marriage licenses (Vanderburgh County Clerk)

  • Marriage licenses (statewide): in Indiana, the county clerk issues marriage licenses. Both of you apply in person at the clerk's office in an Indiana county where one of you lives; couples from out of state apply in the county where they will marry. The fee is $25.00 if one or both of you live in Indiana and $65.00 if neither does. Some offices add a $4.00 document fee, and each certified copy is $4.00. Most counties require cash. The license is valid for 60 days, and you can marry the same day. You'll need ID and your Social Security number [7].
  • Copies (statewide): certified copies of marriage licenses come from the clerk of the county where the license was issued, not from the state health department [22].
  • Vanderburgh County Clerk: start online, then both of you finish together in person at Room S240, Civic Center Complex City/County Admin Building, 1 NW Martin Luther King Jr Blvd, between 7:30 a.m. and 3:45 p.m. Monday–Friday. Bring a valid state ID or driver's license with your current address, a certified birth certificate, a passport or a military ID [5]. The Census Geocoder places the building in Evansville city [10].
  • Who can apply here: couples where one person lives in Vanderburgh County and plans to marry in Indiana, or couples where neither lives in Indiana and they'll marry in Vanderburgh County [5].
  • Fees: $25.00 if at least one of you lives in Vanderburgh County; $65.00 if both of you live out of state and marry in Vanderburgh County. The clerk takes cash, cashier's checks, money orders and credit cards (with a $1.50 convenience fee), but not personal checks. The license is void if you don't marry within 60 days [5].
  • Copies: regular copies $1.00 each and certified copies $4.00 each; call (812) 435-5188 to have them ready for pickup [5]. Online orders through Official Records Online add a $10.00 convenience fee per transaction, plus extra for FedEx delivery [23].

Recorded documents (Vanderburgh County Recorder)

  • Recording fees: $55.00 for mortgages and $25.00 for deeds and other documents, including UCCs (one oversize page included; $5.00 for each additional page larger than 8½ by 14 inches). Payment is cash, company checks or money order only [24].
  • Copies: $1.00 a page (11 by 17 inches or smaller), $5.00 a page if larger, and $5.00 to certify a document [24].
  • How to record: in person at Room 231 of the Civic Center Complex, 1 NW Martin Luther King Jr Blvd [24]; by mail to P.O. Box 1037, Evansville, IN 47706-1037, with a self-addressed stamped envelope; or electronically through an e-recording service, which charges its own fee. Copies of military discharges are free with photo ID [25].
  • Format: pages that don't meet Indiana's format rules can still be recorded for an extra $1 a page [26].

Court records (Vanderburgh County Clerk)

  • Copy fees: $1.00 a page and $3.00 per certification. To get copies by mail, pay in full first by cashier's check or money order and include a self-addressed stamped envelope [27].
  • How to ask: submit the records request form by mail, fax or in person; the clerk says it will reply within 7 days [28].
  • Two addresses: the records request page gives 825 Sycamore Street [28], while the marriage page gives Room S240, 1 NW Martin Luther King Jr Blvd [5]. Both addresses geocode to Evansville city [10]. Call (812) 435-5160 to check which office handles your request [28].

Apostilles and authentications (statewide)

  • Fee (statewide): $2.00 per document from the Indiana Secretary of State. There's no fee for birth and death certificates, adoption documents, or student transcripts and diplomas [29][14]. Pay in person by credit, debit, check or money order, or by mail with a check or money order [29].
  • One certificate (statewide): since July 1, 2022, Indiana uses a single certificate format and no longer tells apostilles and authentications apart. The certificates can't be used inside the United States [29].
  • What it can authenticate (statewide): Indiana vital records only; marriage, divorce, death and birth certificates don't need notarization, but other documents do. A marriage license must first be certified by the current circuit court clerk of the county where the marriage took place. Federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State [29].
  • Where (statewide): there's one office, at 302 W. Washington St., Room E-018, Indianapolis, IN 46204, with no branches. With an appointment it processes up to 10 documents while you wait; without one, up to 5. Phone (317) 234-9768 [29].
  • Evansville note: the Vanderburgh County Health Department says long-form birth certificates for an apostille are available only by mail from the Indiana Department of Health [4]. The Secretary of State's only authentication office is in Indianapolis [29].

Money orders

USPS money order fees are national: $2.65 for $0.01 to $500.00, and $3.75 for $500.01 to $1,000.00. Pay with cash or a debit card, not a credit card. The limit is $1,000 per domestic money order [30]. Other sellers: money order guide.

Shredding

  • City of Evansville Paper Shredding Days (past): the 2026 events were Friday, April 10, and Friday, September 18, 2026, 9:00 a.m. to noon, at Wesselman Woods, 551 N. Boeke Road. They took up to 100 pounds of documents at no charge, for Vanderburgh County residents only. Questions: Vanderburgh County Solid Waste District, (812) 436-7800 [31]. Both dates have passed; we didn't find a 2027 date.
  • The UPS Store: its Evansville page lists shredding [11]. We didn't find prices.

Questions

Where can I get something notarized for free in Evansville?

At six EVPL locations. EVPL Central and West are drop-in only at set times; the other four take appointment requests [1].

Where do I get a birth certificate in Evansville?

At the Vanderburgh County Health Department, 420 Mulberry Street: $16.00, with same-day walk-in service [4].

How much is a marriage license in Evansville?

$25.00 if at least one of you lives in Vanderburgh County, or $65.00 if both of you live out of state [5].

Can I get a passport at the Evansville library?

Yes. EVPL takes passport applications by appointment; call 812.428.8396 [2].

How much can an Indiana notary charge?

Up to $10 per signature for common acts such as acknowledgments and oaths, and up to $25 for a remote notarial act (statewide law) [14].
